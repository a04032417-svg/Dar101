# STUDY — small florist / wedding and event floral design business (market and workflow)

Method: 25 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-15 (the maximum). Builds on Research 11 in research6_20261115.md (the five competitor pages found there are re-used, not repeated, except where a page was re-read for price and contents). Evidence is thin in places and says so; "NOT verified" marks anything not confirmed. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (retail shop or studio):** open and check the cooler (what is fading, what must go into today's arrangements); take walk-in, phone and online orders and write card messages; make arrangements from the recipe or by eye; check the delivery list and route, load and send; confirm and photo-confirm deliveries; log waste and substitutions; answer enquiries within the day; post sales and till; set up next day's prep. Studio: answer wedding and event enquiries, book consultations, send price guides.
**Weekly jobs:** place the wholesale order for the week (by recipe for events, by sales history for the shop); receive, condition and record stock; check the event calendar for the next 4-8 weeks (who owes a deposit, who owes a final balance, what is still to be ordered); build and revise event proposals (2-4 hours each, more with revisions); chase unanswered proposals and unpaid balances; schedule delivery runs and helpers; weekly cash check; supplies check (containers, ribbon, wire, foam-free mechanics, tape); post to social media and ask for reviews after events.
**Monthly jobs:** sales, cost of flowers and waste against target; gross margin by order type (wedding, retail, sympathy, corporate, wholesale supply); invoice corporate and sympathy (funeral home) accounts and chase overdue; pay freelancers and staff; rentals out and back (vases, arches, stands) with damage and loss; marketing spend and enquiry source review; set-aside for tax; owner pay; ask "which products lose money?".
**Yearly jobs and peaks:** holiday peak planning (Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, Christmas, Easter and local equivalents; UK Mothering Sunday): forecast stems by colour and type, pre-orders, extra staff and drivers, cooler space, delivery cut-offs, surcharges; wedding season load (booked weekends, minimum per event, helpers); price list and markup review; supplier price rises; insurance and licence renewals (say "your local rules"); equipment and van; year-end pack for the accountant; cash plan for the slow months (January to February, late summer for some markets); budget.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Owners price by guess, relax markups when busy, and "fight with the left and right sides of my brain"; "you need to know your expenses down to the penny"; guilt about charging drives owners out of business (owner replies on a florist community forum). https://www.florist20.com/community/threads/find-financial-salvation-when-you-accurately-mark-up-prices.37236/latest
- Markup set without a profit goal: the practice article advises "begin with the end in mind" (profit target first); a worked example with 3x markup and 20% labour came out $200 short of the $1,000 target. https://curate.co/blog/florist-floral-markup/
- Proposal time is a hidden cost: 2-4 hours per proposal, 6-8 hours with three revision rounds; the same page puts it at $900-1,200 of unpaid time per booked event at $50 an hour (example numbers from a vendor blog, not survey data). https://curate.co/blog/wedding-florist-pricing/
- Cash timing: a 50% retainer with 33% cost of goods leaves only 17% to run the business, and supplies cannot all be bought upfront; clients who cut their budget late cause serious problems; "only take clients that can afford you". https://curate.co/blog/florist-retainer-deposit/
- Overbuying: wholesalers "tempt us with bulk discounts" that lead to unsold stock; last-minute shortages; price discrepancies and phone back-and-forth. https://curate.co/blog/florist-inventory-management/
- Software gaps seen in owner reviews: duplicate customers, limited reports, no partial payments on the event page (one review; another praised partial payments), no recipe printing, no automatic stem-count stock update, longer card messages wanted, bulk order entry wanted. https://www.capterra.com/p/122876/Floranext/
- General POS tools are weak at freshness, stem counts and waste (vendor comparison page, opinion-grade). https://theretailexec.com/tools/best-florist-pos-systems/
- Holiday concentration: Mother's Day and Valentine's Day together about 40% of annual flower purchases among large U.S. sellers; 21% of a year's spend in the two weeks before Mother's Day (2017, large online sellers, not small shops). https://secondmeasure.com/datapoints/the-mother-of-all-sales-its-the-top-holiday-for-u-s-florists/
- NOT verified: owner-voice threads on cash flow in slow months, freelancer pay, and rentals damage (searches returned vendor and podcast pages, not readable posts). Podcast pages for florists exist (e.g. a "Floral CEO" podcast on transistor.fm), but transcripts were not read.

## 2. Leading florist / event-floral software (research only)
Prices seen per month:
- Floranext: from $29.99 "per feature, per month" (Basic), 7-day trial; one reviewer said the package they used cost about $180 a month; another page says pricing on request and a free plan with pay-as-you-go. Capterra 4.7 from 74 reviews, 92% positive. https://www.capterra.com/p/122876/Floranext/
- FloristWare: Basic $149, Advanced $199, Enterprise $500 per month (flat per plan); 4.7 from 40 reviews, value 4.3. https://www.capterra.com/p/121554/FloristWare/pricing/
- QuickFlora: from $149 a month for 1-4 computers, billed annually; UK-oriented POS from $39 a month; large wire-network products from $1,099 (period not stated); several others on quote only. https://theretailexec.com/tools/best-florist-pos-systems/
- Proposal software for event florists (wedding pricing tools sold by a vendor blog): price NOT verified.
Features: order entry, recurring orders, customer accounts, POS and card processing, e-commerce site, delivery routing and photo confirmation, wedding and event proposals, card printing, loyalty, reports, QuickBooks-type export.
User complaints: see section 1 (duplicates, thin reports, no recipe printing, no automatic stem-count stock, mobile weak, slow support, set-up complex, Windows-only, add-on pricing per feature, per-user and per-store price rise). Vendor comparisons say general POS "weakly handles freshness, stem counts and waste".
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one flat 29 dollars once, no per-feature or per-user fee; recipe-based price built from stems times cost times markup with a profit-first target; proposal that shows margin before it is sent; waste and shrink by cause (not only a sales report); wholesale order per event and per week with buffers; holiday forecast from last year's own numbers; cash view across seasons with owner pay and tax set-aside; rentals that must come back; works for a solo designer and a shop; owner keeps the data.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Every item is a real job with its calculation. Keep generic: no brand, law, agency or standard names.)

1. **Recipe price builder.** Stems x wholesale cost per stem (from bunch price and stems per bunch) + hard goods, then markup per class (flowers, greens, hard goods) and labour charge; shows retail price, gross margin and a verdict "price covers target / short by X".
2. **Profit-first price check.** Start from the yearly profit target and number of events and shop orders; the tool returns the markup and labour percent needed ("begin with the end in mind"), with the shortfall of a chosen markup as one number.
3. **Budget-back design tool.** Client budget in, minus labour, tax and delivery, gives the stem and hard-goods budget and suggests how many focal, filler and green stems fit; ends with "fits / over by X".
4. **Event proposal builder.** Lines for bouquets, boutonnieres, ceremony, tables, installation, delivery, set-up, strike, rental; totals by section, margin by line and a red flag under the margin floor.
5. **Package and a la carte tier comparison.** Three tiers from the same recipes, showing margin and what each adds; ranked by margin per hour of design work.
6. **Minimum spend tool.** Compute the smallest event worth taking from proposal time, design hours, travel and overhead; verdict "accept / quote a minimum / decline" for a given enquiry.
7. **Proposal time cost.** Hours spent per proposal and per booked event, conversion rate, cost of unpaid proposal time per booking; shows whether paying consultations or a design fee is worth it.
8. **Revision rounds charge.** Count rounds per event; extra rounds priced as a line item; report of events with more than two rounds.
9. **Deposit and balance schedule.** Retainer percent, second payment and final-balance date per event; status (paid, due, overdue), amount outstanding by week, and a next-30-days cash-in forecast.
10. **Deposit cash-flow test.** Shows the margin left after COGS when a deposit is taken, and the days before the event when cash in is below cash needed for wholesale orders ("deposit high enough / too low").
11. **Cancellation and late budget cut.** Records the kept retainer, reduced budget and effect on margin; flags events whose budget fell more than a chosen percent.
12. **Event wholesale order.** All recipes of an event summed into a stem buying list with overbuy buffer (15-25% default) and substitution buffer, rounded to bunches; cost and order-by date.
13. **Weekly wholesale order.** Merge events, standing orders and walk-in forecast for the week into one list per supplier, by order day and arrival day, with spend against the weekly budget.
14. **Supplier price compare.** Same stem priced from up to three suppliers; cheapest per usable stem after minimum, delivery and shrink; ranked list.
15. **Price-rise impact.** Change any stem or supplier price and see the effect on every recipe, open proposal and the year's margin; list of proposals that now miss the margin floor.
16. **Cooler stock and age.** Stock in by date, age in days, status "use first / watch / dump"; value at cost of what is older than the rotation rule (an editable setting, no longevity claim).
17. **Waste log by cause.** Waste by cause (overbuy, damage, age, quality, event change); shrink percent of purchased cost versus a target setting; ranked list of stems with the highest shrink.
18. **Overbuy versus waste check.** Per event: bought, used, left over, leftover cost, what buffer would have been right; adjusts the next default buffer from the owner's own history.
19. **Substitution log.** Planned versus actual stem, cost difference and client sign-off; report on which substitutions cost money.
20. **Retail order and ticket.** Order average by type (walk-in, phone, online, sympathy, corporate, delivery), everyday versus holiday; compare with own history.
21. **Sympathy and corporate accounts.** Account list with standing price rules, invoice, days outstanding and ageing buckets; ranked list of accounts by margin and by days to pay.
22. **Standing order scheduler.** Weekly or monthly arrangements by customer, next date, stems to include in the weekly order and revenue per month; flags undercosted standing orders.
23. **Delivery fee and zone check.** Cost per delivery (driver time, vehicle, tolls) versus fee charged by zone; verdict "raise fee / fine / loss-maker" and revenue lost from free deliveries.
24. **Delivery route sheet.** Orders by zone and time window, stop order, and total drive time; cost per stop; carry-forward of missed or failed deliveries.
25. **Event day delivery and set-up estimate.** Staff hours for load, drive, set-up and strike, vehicle cost, and the charge needed to cover it.
26. **Rental inventory and return.** Items out, due back, returned, damage and loss; deposit kept; utilisation per item and payback months (price of item / rental income); list of overdue returns.
27. **Rental pricing tool.** Rent per item from purchase price, expected uses per year and damage allowance; ends with "price covers cost in N bookings".
28. **Freelancer pay and hours.** Day rate or hourly, hours per event, cost as a share of event price against a labour target (25-35% default); pay statements per helper.
29. **Staff plan for big weekends.** Events and orders per day versus designer hours available; gap in hours and extra-helper cost; verdict "enough / hire N helpers".
30. **Holiday peak forecast.** From last year's own orders and stems, apply a growth percent; output stems by type, extra staff, cooler space, pre-order cut-off and expected revenue; compares with the same holiday last year.
31. **Holiday pre-order tracker.** Pre-orders by day and product, deposits taken, fill rate against capacity; stop-selling alert at capacity.
32. **Holiday dependency test.** Share of yearly sales in the top peaks (flag past a set share), and the cash left to carry the slow months.
33. **Seasonal cash plan.** Twelve-month cash forecast with wedding season, holidays and slow months, tax set-aside, owner pay and the cash floor; first month below the floor in red.
34. **Owner pay and tax set-aside.** Profit, set-aside percent (editable, "confirm with your adviser"), owner draw affordable by month, and months of cover in cash.
35. **Margin by order type.** Gross margin by wedding, event, retail, sympathy, corporate, wholesale supply and delivery; ranked, with a verdict on which type to push or reprice.
36. **Margin by event, after the fact.** Quoted versus actual cost, labour hours, waste and delivery per event; variance and a lessons list; compare with own average margin.
37. **Lost-money finder.** Flags recipes, products and clients whose margin is below target, with the price that would restore the target.
38. **Supplies and mechanics stock.** Containers, ribbon, tape and foam-free mechanics by par level, usage per event, reorder alert and cost per event allocation.
39. **Enquiries to bookings.** Funnel by source (referral, venue, social, search, walk-in): enquiries, consultations, proposals, booked, value per booked event; keep-or-drop decision for paid listings and ads.
40. **Review and referral follow-up.** Events completed with review asked, review received, repeat client and venue; list of clients to ask this week; spend and return of each venue or planner relationship.

Packaging items (apply to every workbook, not counted above): "Start here" tab with "Replace demo data"; sample plus blank; no macros; works in Excel and Google Sheets; editable settings for tax label, currency, bunch size, markup per class, buffers, margin floor and peaks; real screenshots in the listing (the one competitor complaint is "not as pictured"); one-line "why a workbook" (flat price, your data stays yours, built for florists).

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, platform, bank or company names, and no names of marketplaces, directories or social networks.
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, programme or standard names; say "your local rules", "your licence or insurance renewal", "confirm with your adviser" on every tax cell. Use one generic "tax" label that the owner can rename (sales tax, VAT, GST).
- No flower-supplier, grower, wholesaler, wire-service or product brand names; say "your wholesaler", "your supplier", "wire or relay order".
- No claims about flower longevity, freshness days, vase life, or safety guarantees; ageing rules are the owner's own editable settings ("rotation rule in days").
- Gender-neutral wording throughout (client, couple, owner, designer, they); no "bride and groom" as the only option ("couple", "client").
- Accept market variants as labels: florist, floral designer, flower studio, florist's shop; boutonniere / buttonhole; rental / hire; centerpiece / centrepiece.
- Benchmarks shown as editable defaults labelled "starting point, replace with your own history"; do not state them as industry facts.
- No code, no certification names, no competitor names; demo data fictional (made-up studio and clients).
