# Florist and floral design business suite — eight parts (all share fl_core.py: owner-designer, 2 floral designers, shop assistant, delivery driver, 4 freelance helpers, 7 outside contacts, ~60 events a year, ~6,100 shop orders, ~2 years of history)

The demo business is "Juniper & Vine Florals" (fictional): a flower studio with a small retail counter run by an owner-designer (Marisol Vega, FL-01). Two floral designers are on salary (Thea Lindgren, Kofi Asante), a part-time shop assistant (Hana Sato, hourly) and a delivery driver (Dmitri Petrov, hourly) run the counter and the vans, and four freelance helpers (Aisha Rahman, Lucas Weber, Camila Torres, Emeka Obi, day rate) join on big wedding weekends and holiday peaks. Seven outside contacts: the accountant, two flower wholesalers ("Wholesaler A", "Wholesaler B"), a local grower, a supplies wholesaler, a van mechanic and a rentals partner. About 60 weddings and events a year (proposals built from flower recipes: stems x wholesale cost x markup plus labour, deposits and final balances), daily retail (walk-in, phone and online orders, about 7 a day, far more at Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and the year-end holidays), sympathy work (5 funeral-home accounts plus direct orders), 8 corporate standing-order accounts, local delivery in 4 zones, weekly wholesale orders and event orders, a cooler with waste and substitutions, 12 rental items (vases, arches, stands) that go out and come back, 16 supplies. Each part is a standalone workbook that carries its own copies of the reference tables it needs. A solo floral designer can use most parts (the help texts say so: "solo designer: leave the staff rows you do not have empty"). Charts group by month or by a list column whose values the demo data uses fully (event type, source, order type, product kind, zone, supplier, flower class, cause, holiday, status); never by a calc column, an id, a client name, a quarter or a year list, and never with hundreds of rows as an axis; the flower variety list has 34 values, so never chart by variety (rank varieties in a tool). A table with fewer than 12 rows may have only 3 columns: design for that (Zones, Suppliers, Size options, Markup rules, Event types). Money is ONE generic currency (demo wedding about 2,800; hand-tied bouquet 50 to 195; stems 0.3 to 3.6 each at wholesale); shares are 0..1 fractions. Benchmarks from STUDY.md are editable starting points ("starting point, replace with your own history"), never stated as industry facts.
