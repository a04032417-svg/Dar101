# STUDY — balloon and event decor business (garlands, arches, walls, backdrops, bouquets, rentals): market and workflow

Method: 25 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-15, on top of "Research 15" in research6_20261115.md (not repeated here). Evidence is moderate to weak and says so. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Prices are mostly US dollars from vendor price pages and blog guides; anything not seen is marked NOT verified.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner, often home-based, plus 1-4 helpers and a van):** answer new enquiries (most arrive by message or form, with a date, venue, colour palette and a photo); send the price guide and colour chart; turn a theme or colour brief into a quote (size in feet, style, extras); chase unpaid deposits; confirm tomorrow's delivery or install window with the client and venue contact; blow up, build and wrap pieces in the workshop; load the van; deliver, install, photograph the finished work; collect take-down jobs; check balloons and helium left; post photos and reply to reviews.

**Weekly jobs:** next weekend's calendar check (jobs per day, crew per job, van loads, drive time, set-up windows that clash); weather check for outdoor jobs and client warning; pick lists for each job; stock check by size and colour against next week's jobs; helium refill or tank swap; supplier order; balance reminders (due before the event); rental return check and damage notes; follow-up with quotes sent but not answered; trade-account enquiries from planners and venues; weekly cash check; pay helpers.

**Monthly jobs:** revenue by stream (garlands and arches, walls and backdrops, bouquets and deliveries, marquee letters and numbers, rentals, trade jobs); material cost, helium cost and waste by job type; labour hours against quoted hours; unpaid balances and old quotes; supplier statement check; vehicle and travel cost; rental prop repairs and replacements; owner pay and tax set-aside transfer; marketing and referral source review; price list review.

**Seasonal and yearly jobs:** peak planning (graduation, holidays, school dances, baby-shower and wedding months): extra stock, extra crew, booking caps per weekend, rush-fee rules; quiet-week plan (markets, workshops, rental promotion, school and trade outreach); price rise and notice to trade accounts; supplier terms and bulk buys before peak; prop and frame replacement budget; insurance renewal and venue approvals; year-end pack for the accountant; reviewing which streams earn most per hour.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Underpricing is "perhaps the most common and detrimental mistake" of new owners: volume trap and burnout, lower perceived value, no money to reinvest. Owners also forget overhead (rent, software, insurance, marketing, wages). https://www.podpage.com/the-bright-balloon-1/blog/pricing-your-balloon-business
- Hobbyists "typically do not factor in overheads, a labour cost, or profit margin" and undercut professionals; one owner with over 30 years in the trade says she was "barely covering my costs" in her first four years as a hobbyist. https://theverybestballoonblog.blogspot.com/2025/06/pricing-hobbyist-vs-small-businesses-in.html
- A price per foot hides real cost differences: helium or air fill, balloons per cluster, and extras change the cost, so one balloon educator prefers "direct costs x 2.5" (materials plus labour at your own hourly rate for prep, design, build and take-down) over a fixed price per foot. https://www.balloon-decoration-guide.com/how-do-you-determine-your-rates-for-your-balloon-decorations.html
- Enquiry overload: an owner was "drowning in email", could not tell who needed what, searched the inbox for event details, and stopped answering; her fix was automatic first replies with the price guide and colour chart, one client file per enquiry, invoices with delivery details, and scheduled review and rebook reminders. https://www.podpage.com/the-bright-balloon-1/blog/crm-transformed-my-balloon-business
- Deposits and dates: a booking-tool vendor's page cites an artist who chased a deposit "for three weeks", clients asking for dates the artist cannot work, and slow quotes ("No consultation. No quote PDF. No four-hour reply."). Vendor marketing, not an owner survey. https://www.fanfaire.io
- Outdoor weather: wind pulls arches apart, heat swells balloons and dark colours pop first, rain and dew make balloons stick, helium arches do poorly outdoors; clients do not understand colour limits or float time, so owners tell clients a shorter life than expected (if 3 days, say 1 to 2), charge extra for rigging and wind-proofing, tell the client at once if rain is forecast, give a credit for cancellation before balloons are blown but charge the full job after inflation, and never guarantee outdoor results. https://theverybestballoonblog.blogspot.com/2016/05/balloons-and-great-outdoors.html
- Helium and cost shocks: a balloon owners' podcast page lists "rising helium prices and unexpected expenses" as a pricing topic (page not readable in full). https://www.podpage.com/the-bright-balloon-1/blog/pricing-your-balloon-business
- Pricing guides disagree: two public price lists show garland at 10-20 dollars a foot and others 25-45, so owners cannot tell where they sit (see section 5).
- NOT verified: forum and group threads in the owners' own words (the trade's groups are mostly private; searches returned blogs, vendor pages and listing sites). Venue-rule pain (helium bans, set-up windows) is NOT verified beyond the weather page above.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Every item is a real job with its calculation. Keep generic: no brand, law or licence names.)

1. **True cost of a job (Part 2).** Job costing: balloons by size and colour (balloon count from feet and size, spares percent), helium or air fill, frame and hardware, glue dots, labour (prep, build, wrap, travel, install, strike) at own hourly rate, travel cost, card fee. Ends with cost, price, profit, profit per hour and a verdict "price covers it / below target / loses money".
2. **Price per foot that comes from cost (Part 2).** For each style (classic, organic, spiral, wall per square foot, column) show cost per foot, price per foot, and the gap to the owner's own local band; verdict "raise / hold / check"; replaces a guessed price per foot.
3. **Cost multiplier test (Part 2).** Compare the owner's actual price with direct cost x multiplier (default 2.5 in Settings) and with target margin; show which jobs in the last 90 days fell below.
4. **Underpricing finder (Part 2 / 8).** Rank last jobs by profit per hour and by margin; list the five worst; show the extra income if those had been priced at target; verdict per job type.
5. **Package builder (Part 2).** A package (for example a birthday set: garland, columns, table pieces) priced from parts with a bundle discount; shows effect on margin and the lowest discount that still hits target.
6. **Price list with rush, outdoor and venue extras (Part 2).** One table of extras: rush (same-week, same-day percent), outdoor rigging, wind-proofing, high-reach, floor-level difficulty; quote total auto-adds them; show how many jobs paid extras and what they added.
7. **Quote from a colour brief (Part 1).** Quote builder: event type, date, venue, palette (number of colours), size in feet, style, extras; prints a one-page quote with deposit, balance due date, delivery, install and take-down; keeps quote number and valid-until date.
8. **Enquiry tracker with speed and cause (Part 1).** Hours to first reply, replied within a day (gauge), quote sent, won, lost; lost reasons (no reply, price, date unavailable, venue rule, changed mind, outdoor risk); source of enquiry.
9. **Quote follow-up ranked list (Part 1).** Quotes open for more than N days, ranked by value times chance, with next action and a "last touched" date; shows money sitting in unanswered quotes.
10. **Win rate by client type and job type (Part 1).** Win rate, average quote and average margin for family, school, corporate, venue, planner; verdict "spend time here / limit time here".
11. **Date availability check (Part 3 / 4).** Type a date: shows crew booked, van loads, set-up windows used, rentals out, stock needed; verdict "take it / take with extra crew / decline / offer another day"; same data feeds the quote.
12. **Deposit and balance schedule (Part 3).** Deposit share (editable, default 50%), balance due days before event, status per job (deposit paid, balance paid, overdue); list of balances to chase this week ranked by event date and amount.
13. **Deposit-held date rule (Part 3).** A date is held only when the deposit is in: shows held dates without deposit after N days and releases them to a "re-offer" list; counts lost dates.
14. **Cancellation and change rules by stage (Part 3).** Stage table (before build, balloons blown, delivered) with credit or full charge as owner settings; on cancellation, one cell gives what the client owes and what is kept; a log of cancellations by cause (weather, client, venue).
15. **Money owed ageing (Part 3 / 8).** Buckets 0-7, 8-30, 31+ days after event for unpaid balances and trade invoices; oldest first; total at risk.
16. **Booking calendar with weekend view (Part 4).** Weekend-by-weekend sheet (Friday to Sunday): jobs, setup times, crew, van; colour for "full", "tight", "open"; capacity per crew as an owner setting (jobs and build hours per day).
17. **Crew hours check by weekend (Part 4).** Build hours plus install hours plus drive hours per crew member per weekend against their available hours; flag overload; "can we add one more job?" verdict.
18. **Delivery, install and strike slots (Part 4).** For each job: venue access window, arrival, finish, strike time, conflicts with other jobs on the same day; route order and drive minutes; tells which job goes first.
19. **Travel zones (Part 4).** Zone table (distance bands, fee, drive minutes, minimum order); job's travel cost from zone; compare fee charged with real cost (fuel, driver time); flag zones that lose money.
20. **Van load planner (Part 4).** Estimated volume or number of large pieces per job against van capacity; tells when two trips are needed and adds the second trip cost to the quote.
21. **Venue rules log (Part 4).** Venue list with helium allowed (yes/no/ask), set-up window, access limits, ceiling and fixing rules, contact; checked against each quote so a job is flagged "venue rule check needed" before it is accepted.
22. **Outdoor job risk score (Part 4).** Wind, heat, rain, helium use, colours dark/light, base weight, hours between set-up and event; score with verdict "go / go with changes / move indoors / decline"; prints the lifespan wording to tell the client.
23. **Weather day-before checklist (Part 4).** For each outdoor job, a log: forecast, call made, change agreed, extra charge; later compare cost of weather changes by month.
24. **Pick list per job (Part 5).** From the job's pieces: balloons by size and colour with spares, weights, frames, glue dots, tape, ribbon; checks against stock and shows shortfalls before the weekend.
25. **Stock by size and colour (Part 5).** Opening stock, bought, used by job, waste, closing; reorder point per size and colour set from average weekly use; weeks of cover; ranked "buy now" list.
26. **Peak-season stock plan (Part 5).** Forecast balloon use from booked jobs plus last year's same weeks plus a growth percent; shows what to buy before the peak, and the cash needed.
27. **Helium tracker (Part 5).** Tanks bought, fills, cost per fill, helium pieces made; helium cost per piece and per bouquet; compare with the helium cost in quotes; flag when the price list under-charges for helium.
28. **Waste and pop rate (Part 5).** Balloons used vs balloons quoted, pops, returns, old stock; waste percent by job type, by crew and by season; compare with the spare percent in pricing; verdict "raise spares / reduce spares".
29. **Supplier order planner (Part 5).** Supplier price per bag by size and colour, minimum order, delivery fee, lead time; cost per balloon; cheapest source per item; reorder list grouped by supplier; supplier price rise effect on the price list.
30. **Rental prop register (Part 6).** Each prop (arch frame, backdrop, stand, shimmer wall, neon sign style piece, marquee letters) with cost, hire price, hire period, status, repairs, number of hires; revenue per prop and payback.
31. **Hire availability check (Part 6).** Type a date range: which props are free, out, or in repair; double-booking alert; return due list.
32. **Damage and late return log (Part 6).** Condition out and back, damage fee, late fee, deposit kept; damage rate and late rate by client type and by prop; verdict "raise deposit / keep".
33. **Prop payback and keep-or-sell (Part 6).** Months to pay back each prop, hires per year, repair cost; ranked list; decision "buy more / keep / retire"; also the rent-vs-buy test for a new backdrop.
34. **Trade accounts (Part 7).** Planner, venue and school accounts with agreed discount, payment terms, jobs and revenue per year, margin after discount, late payments; verdict "keep terms / change discount / drop".
35. **Referral and review engine (Part 7).** Review requested, received, replied; referral source per job and revenue per source; cost of each source (ads, markets, commissions); ranks sources by profit per enquiry, not by contacts.
36. **Repeat clients and rebook reminders (Part 7).** Event date next year, birthday of child shown as a month only, last job, favourite palette; list of "contact this month" clients; repeat share of revenue.
37. **Revenue and profit by stream (Part 8).** Garlands and arches, walls and backdrops, bouquets and deliveries, letters and numbers, rentals, trade; revenue, margin, hours, profit per hour; ranked; verdict on which stream to push.
38. **Cash flow across busy and quiet weeks (Part 8).** Week-by-week forecast of deposits in, balances in, stock bought for peaks, helper pay, van and rent; lowest cash week; how much to keep back to survive quiet weeks.
39. **Owner pay and tax set-aside (Part 8).** Pay yourself a fixed amount: profit left after costs, tax set-aside percent (editable, "confirm with your adviser"), reserve months; verdict "safe to pay / reduce / wait"; compare with own history.
40. **Scenario tests (Part 8).** What if helium price rises 20%, supplier price +10%, a helper is added, price list +8%, one more job per weekend: effect on margin and owner pay; "should I hire a helper?" with break-even jobs per weekend; compare with last 3 months and the same month last year.

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, platform, bank or company names (including the research sources above, design tools, booking tools, card processors, marketplaces).
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, programme or standard names: say "your local rules", "the venue's rules", "your insurance renewal"; tax is "tax" with the line "confirm with your adviser".
- No safety-certification claims. Helium and outdoor handling appear only as plain owner checklists ("check the venue allows helium", "weigh the base", "check the forecast"), never as a guarantee.
- No balloon, helium, supplier, prop or maker brand names; no character, licensed-theme or franchise names; no brand-style product names.
- Gender-neutral wording (client, owner, crew, helper, installer, planner).
- No contract or legal-advice text: order terms are plain editable lines ("deposit due", "balance due", "cancellation steps"); no clause language.
- Demo prices, rates and percentages are editable examples; say "enter your own local band" and never present a figure from this study as a market claim.
- Accept UK/AU/CA words as labels: balloon stylist, hire, metre, cylinder, tax.
