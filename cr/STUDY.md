# STUDY — small independent car rental / car-sharing host business, 3-40 vehicles (market and workflow)

Method: 25 web calls (7 searches, 18 fetches) on 2026-11-15. Evidence is thin in places and says so. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Note: the file research6_20261115.md has no section titled "Research 18"; the nearest evidence there is Research 15 (car rental / fleet, no car-rental spreadsheet with reviews found) and Research 17 (host listing search failed). This study builds on those and does not repeat them. Every figure not marked with a source is NOT verified.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner, manager, cleaner/driver):** answer enquiries and send quotes (vehicle class, dates, add-ons, delivery fee); check the booking calendar for overlaps and for the same car booked on two channels (own site, phone, marketplace); confirm tomorrow's pickups and returns; plan delivery and collection runs (airport, hotel) with a driver; hand over a car (check-in/out: fuel or charge level, odometer, photos taken yes/no, keys, deposit taken); receive a car (same list, plus damage seen yes/no, late return minutes, extra mileage); clean and prepare the car before the next booking; move cars that are due for service out of the calendar; log tolls and tickets that arrive and match them to the rental that had the car; record the day's cash, card and marketplace payouts; reply to guests mid-rental (breakdown, extension, early return).
**Weekly jobs:** next-week availability by vehicle class and date, with gaps (idle days) and weekend pressure; price changes for weekends and events; chase deposits not yet released or balances unpaid; chase tolls/tickets not yet passed on; check which cars are due for oil/service/tyres; book workshop slots so the car loses the fewest rental days; pay the cleaner and driver (hours or per job); reconcile marketplace payouts against bookings (fee taken, payout received, date); update the claim list (status and amount only); review the enquiry-to-booking rate and lost enquiries; weekly cash check.
**Monthly jobs:** utilisation, revenue per vehicle per day and per month, idle days and profit per vehicle; cost per vehicle (financing, insurance, maintenance, cleaning, depreciation, fuel/charge); marketplace fee share of revenue; extra-mileage, late-return and fuel fees billed vs collected; damage and claims summary (open, paid, written off); financing payments and balance; profit and loss and cash; tax set-aside transfer; owner draw; maintenance schedule review; marketing source review (own site vs marketplace).
**Yearly jobs:** registration and insurance renewal dates for every vehicle (generic: "registration renewal", "insurance renewal", "inspection/roadworthiness due"); seasonal rate card (peak, shoulder, off-peak, holidays); review of add-on prices (child seat, extra driver, insurance options in generic words, GPS, delivery); decide which vehicles to sell or replace (age, kilometres/miles, repair cost trend, resale value); fleet plan and budget; tyre season changes; year-end pack for the accountant; review of terms and deposit sizes.

**Pain points reported (sources, owners' own words where found):**
- Manual booking and no live availability: operators on paper or spreadsheets cannot see which cars are free; "vehicles can show as available while still rented, causing missed bookings", and late returns go unnoticed. https://www.simplyfleet.app/blog/rental-fleet-tracking-issues (vendor blog, no figures)
- Idle cars and wrong place: "some vehicles sit idle while others are overused, often because assets are in the wrong place"; delays in reallocating cars at peak. same page.
- Software users say adding an extension to a booking is hard, a car cannot be swapped mid-rental (a new reservation is needed), the system does not suggest the next free date when a car is unavailable, similar cars cannot be grouped in one booking, there is no field for reservation source (hotel, agency) to track commissions, no mobile app, little choice for vehicle condition checks at return, reports hard to customise, and orders cannot be split into several invoices. https://www.capterra.com/p/192757/RentSyst/reviews/ (72 reviews, 4.8; 25 shown)
- Damage disputes and the unknown culprit: without automated detection, operators "identify the responsible customer in fewer than one in four cases"; "1 to 2% of vehicle rentals in car sharing are responsible for more than 40% of total damage costs" (cited from an industry trends report). https://invers.com/en/academy/vehicle-damage-in-car-sharing (vendor page, secondary citation)
- Hidden software costs: setup fees, training, hosting billed separately, payment gateway markups on top of the processor rate, paid phone support, per-user fees for counter staff, "vehicles parked for maintenance counted in per-vehicle billing". https://www.carrentalsolutions.com/blog/how-much-does-car-rental-software-cost/ (vendor page)
- A host's own words: "I dislike retrieving a filthy car." "...but time is money!"; friends and family are needed for "last-minute commitments"; early hosting was "anxious. Nervous."; fuels, cleans and takes about 40 photos between rentals; about 9-10 hours a month for two cars; depreciation is "a big factor" with no figure. https://vitaldollar.com/?p=5441 (one host, anecdotal)
- Fixed pricing that limits income and weak follow-up of customers and damage disputes (vendor blog, no figures). https://www.coastr.com/post/expert-solutions-to-the-7-most-common-vehicle-rental-business-challenges
- Amortisation/depreciation is left out of daily rates: an industry article tells operators to convert amortisation and depreciation into a daily cost and compare it with the rental rate. https://www.autorentalnews.com/articles/how-to-make-more-money-in-car-rental
- NOT verified: owner forum threads and review pages of marketplaces (searches returned articles and review sites; I did not read forum posts). Complaint themes about fees, tolls and tickets, deposits and late claims are therefore NOT verified from first-hand posts.

## 2. Leading car-rental / fleet-booking software (research only)
Prices seen:
- Cloud rental software overall about $50-$500+ per month by fleet size and features; four pricing models: flat tiers, per-booking commission, per-vehicle (illustrative $5-$10 per car per month), one-time licence plus annual maintenance. One vendor's own plans: $69.95 (1 branch, 3 users), $99.95 (up to 125 units, 5 users, e-signatures), $179.95 (unlimited), with set-up $197/$297/$397; no booking commission. https://www.carrentalsolutions.com/blog/how-much-does-car-rental-software-cost/ (vendor's own page)
- Another vendor via a third-party comparison page: $129 a month (1 user, 1 location, under about 15 cars), $149 (2 users, about 15-50 cars), $399 (50+ cars); extra users about $20 each a month; about 20% off for annual billing; payment processing billed separately; set-up fee not published. https://fleets.levyelectric.com/compare/hq-rental-software-pricing (competitor's page about a rival; treat as indicative)
- Managed fleet platform: 15% of gross booking value (20% with 24/7 support) or $14 per vehicle a month for 100-249 vehicles. Same page (not for small fleets).
Features offered: reservations and calendar, online booking website with live availability, e-signature agreements, rate engine for seasonal prices and extras, fleet and maintenance tracking, payments, reports, API, multilingual booking.
User complaints: see Section 1 (RentSyst reviews): extensions, mid-rental swap, next-free-date suggestion, source field, no mobile app, report customisation, condition checks, invoice splitting.
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** flat 29 dollars once, no per-vehicle, per-user or per-booking fee; a "next free date" and "which class can I offer" answer for any date range; a mid-rental swap and extension as normal rows; a source column (own site, phone, marketplace, hotel/agency) with fee share by source; true profit per vehicle (financing, depreciation, cleaning, repairs, fees), not just revenue; replace-or-sell and idle-day verdicts that software dashboards do not give; keep the data with the owner; one-car hosts and 40-car fleets on the same files.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Part numbers refer to the 8 suggested parts listed at the end. Keep every item generic: no brand, law or licence names. Every item is a real job with the calculation it needs.)

1. **Part 2 (next free date and class finder).** Pick class, start, end: return every vehicle free for the whole span, and if none, the next date a class opens (software users complain it is missing). Calculation: overlap test per vehicle across all bookings and service blocks; verdict "Offer car X / Offer class Y from date Z / Cannot offer".
2. **Part 2 (double-booking guard).** Every new row is tested against existing bookings, the turnaround buffer and maintenance blocks; verdict cell "Clear / Overlaps booking 123 / Inside turnaround time". Buffer minutes set per class in Settings.
3. **Part 2 (booking calendar by vehicle and day).** Month grid of vehicles by day with status (rented, reserved, cleaning, service, idle); utilisation per vehicle per day computed from it, so idle days are visible at a glance.
4. **Part 2 (extension and mid-rental swap).** Extension check ("can the car stay to date X?") and a swap tool that moves a booking to another car of the same class and recalculates the rate, mileage allowance and fees; both are named software complaints.
5. **Part 2 (source column and fee share).** Each booking carries a source (own site, phone, hotel/agency, marketplace) and a fee percent; report: revenue, fee, net by source, and the net rate per day by source.
6. **Part 1 (instant quote).** Vehicle class, days, season, add-ons, delivery distance, young-driver fee, deposit: total price, price per day, deposit due, and a plain written quote text; cost-based floor shows the lowest price that still covers cost per day.
7. **Part 1 (seasonal rate card).** Peak, shoulder, off-peak and weekend multipliers per class; check "rate below cost per day" and "rate above what the last 12 months achieved" in plain verdicts.
8. **Part 1 (weekly and monthly discount tool).** Test discount steps against idle probability: net revenue of 3 days at daily rate vs 7 days at weekly rate vs expected idle days; verdict "keep / raise / lower".
9. **Part 1 (add-on profit).** Add-ons (child seat, extra driver, delivery, cover option in generic words) with cost, price, uptake rate and profit per rental; ranked list of which add-on earns most and the upsell target per booking.
10. **Part 1 (enquiry funnel and lost enquiries).** Enquiries, quotes, bookings, hours to first reply; lost-enquiry cause (price, no car free, dates, no reply) with a ranked "what to fix" list; conversion by source.
11. **Part 3 (check-out and check-in log).** Fuel or charge level, odometer, photos taken yes/no, damage seen yes/no, keys, deposit taken, time; auto-calc of extra kilometres, fuel shortfall charge and late minutes against the agreement settings; "fee due" total per return.
12. **Part 3 (late-return and extra-mileage fee collection).** Fees billed vs collected, written off, and still open; ageing; a monthly "money left on the table" line when a fee was due but not billed.
13. **Part 3 (damage and claim status log).** Status and amounts only: reported, estimate, repair cost, deposit used, claim paid, written off, days open; KPI "claims open over 30 days" and "net damage cost per 100 rentals".
14. **Part 3 (repeat-damage finder).** Rank vehicle classes, add-on mixes and sources by damage cost per rental, using the concentration finding (1-2% of rentals cause most damage cost) to flag which segments need a larger deposit.
15. **Part 3 (tolls and tickets pass-through).** Register each toll or ticket with date, car, amount; auto-match to the rental covering that date and time; verdict "pass on / handled by renter / no rental found"; admin fee setting; open and recovered totals.
16. **Part 3 (renter status screen).** Status-only fields (documents checked yes/no, age band ok, extra driver ok, restricted yes/no) with a go/no-go verdict; no personal data beyond a booking reference.
17. **Part 4 (deposit and hold tracker).** Deposit taken, hold date, release due date, released/used, amount kept; alert list of holds due to release this week and holds past the release date; liability total held right now.
18. **Part 4 (marketplace payout reconciliation).** Booking value, fee, expected payout, received payout, date; difference column and "payouts overdue" list; fee share by month in generic words.
19. **Part 4 (invoice and balance chase).** Unpaid balances with ageing buckets (0-7, 8-30, 31-60, 60+), promised date, last contact; split invoice into rental, fees and damage lines.
20. **Part 4 (cash in by channel and day).** Cash, card, bank transfer, marketplace payout by day; daily till check against bookings; weekly cash view with 4-week forecast of payouts and deposits to be returned.
21. **Part 5 (vehicle register).** One row per car: class, purchase or lease, financing payment, registration and insurance renewal dates, service interval, tyre date, odometer; colour list of renewals due in 30/60 days.
22. **Part 5 (service schedule and downtime).** Service due by date or kilometres, whichever first; planner that proposes the workshop slot with the fewest booked days lost; "rental days lost to service" per vehicle per year.
23. **Part 5 (repair and tyre cost by vehicle).** Repair log with category, cost, days off the road; running repair cost per month and per 1,000 km; flag "repair cost trend rising" for the replace-or-sell tool.
24. **Part 5 (renewals dashboard).** All registration, insurance and inspection dates across the fleet in one sorted list with red (overdue), amber (30 days), and the cost of renewals due over the next 12 months for cash planning.
25. **Part 6 (turnaround and cleaning board).** Per return: time returned, cleaner start and finish, next pickup time; verdict "ready on time / tight / late"; average turnaround per class vs setting; cleaning cost per rental.
26. **Part 6 (delivery and collection run planner).** List the day's deliveries and collections with times, distance, driver; total run time and cost; verdict whether the delivery fee covers driver time and fuel; ranked list of the least profitable delivery zones.
27. **Part 6 (staff roster and pay).** Shifts for manager, cleaner, driver and part-timers against bookings per day; hours cost per rental; pay from hours or per-job rate; "days short of staff" check against pickups and returns.
28. **Part 6 (task checklist by role).** Daily, weekly and monthly task lists per role with done/not done and an owner "what was missed" count, so a one-car host or a six-person team both use it.
29. **Part 7 (utilisation by vehicle and by class).** Rented days divided by available days per vehicle per month, with band: below 60% red, 60-80% amber, above 80% green (editable); compare with own last 3 months and same month last year.
30. **Part 7 (profit per vehicle per day).** Revenue per available day minus financing, insurance, maintenance, cleaning, fuel/charge, depreciation and marketplace fee, per vehicle; ranked list best to worst with a verdict "keep / reprice / sell".
31. **Part 7 (break-even utilisation per vehicle).** Fixed cost per day divided by (rate minus variable cost per rental day) gives the break-even utilisation; verdict if actual utilisation is below it for 3 months.
32. **Part 7 (idle-day finder).** List runs of 3+ idle days by vehicle; suggest a discount or longer-hire offer and compute the revenue gain if filled at a lower rate; ranked by lost revenue.
33. **Part 7 (rate vs history).** Achieved ADR by class and month against the rate card, with "discount given" share, and a plain flag when achieved rate falls below last year's same month.
34. **Part 7 (demand forecast and weekend pressure).** Next 12 weeks: booked days by class against fleet days; shortage and surplus by weekend; simple forecast from last year's same weeks plus trend, with verdict "raise rates / hold / promote".
35. **Part 8 (replace-or-sell tool).** Inputs: age, kilometres, resale estimate, repair cost last 12 months, financing balance, new-car cost; compare cost per day of keeping one more year vs replacing; verdict "keep / sell this season / replace", using the 2-2.5% a month depreciation as an editable default and a replacement age and kilometres setting (36 months / 90,000 km as an example, weak).
36. **Part 8 (financing and loan view).** Payment, balance, rate, months left, equity (resale minus balance) per car; total monthly financing as a share of revenue; "months of payments covered by cash".
37. **Part 8 (profit and loss and owner draw).** Monthly P&L by vehicle class; owner draw and tax set-aside from profit with a cash floor; verdict "safe to draw / draw less" using cash after next 30 days of known costs.
38. **Part 8 (scenario test).** What if utilisation falls 10 points, fleet adds two cars, financing rate changes, marketplace fee share rises, or rates go up 5%: profit and cash 12 months ahead, shown in a table of 4 scenarios.
39. **Part 8 (add a vehicle business case).** Cost, financing, expected utilisation by season, ADR, cleaning, insurance: payback months and break-even utilisation; verdict "buy / wait / do not buy"; used also by a one-car host starting out.
40. **Shared (packaging, settings and wording).** Every workbook has a "Start here" tab, sample plus blank files, "no macros", "Excel and Google Sheets", and a one-line "Why a workbook" block (flat price, no per-vehicle or per-user fee, your data stays with you, reports for owners: profit per vehicle, idle days, replace-or-sell). Shared settings: buffer minutes, utilisation bands, depreciation percent a month, target ADR by class, tax set-aside percent, cash floor, fee share, deposit size by class; every tax cell says "confirm with your adviser"; every UK/EU/AU term is accepted as a label.

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, platform, marketplace, bank, insurer, car-maker or company names anywhere in the product (say "a car-sharing marketplace", "your payment provider", "your insurer").
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, program or standard names; say "your local rules", "your insurance terms", "registration renewal", "inspection due". Do not name tax forms or tax codes; say "sales tax" generically and "confirm with your adviser".
- No legal or insurance advice and no safety guarantees: fee, deposit and cover cells are the owner's own settings; claim cells hold status and amounts only; say "check your insurance terms".
- Renter data kept as status and dates only: a booking reference, dates, yes/no checks and amounts; no names, addresses, licence or card numbers, no photos stored in the file (only "photos taken yes/no").
- Gender-neutral wording (renter, guest, host, owner, driver, staff); accept UK/EU/AU words (car hire, hire agreement, hirer, excess, bond, rego) as labels beside the US word.
- Benchmarks shown in the product are editable defaults labelled "example, set your own"; never present a weak figure from this study as a fact.
