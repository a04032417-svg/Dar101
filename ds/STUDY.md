# STUDY — independent dance studio / dance school (US first; Canada, UK, Australia) — market and workflow

Method: 25 web calls (searches and fetches) on 2026-11-15, building on /home/claude/work/research6_20261115.md (Research 13 and its REVISION). One Capterra page for a second product was blocked by the fetch allowlist (counted). Evidence is moderate; owner forums were NOT readable (searches returned trade-magazine articles, vendor blogs and review sites, not forum threads), so owner "own words" come from trade-magazine Q&A pages. Brand names appear here only as research sources; none may reach the product. Anything marked NOT verified was not confirmed.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily / weekly jobs (owner plus front desk):** build and adjust the weekly timetable by room, style, level and instructor; check each class against its capacity and waitlist; answer enquiries and book trial classes (studios in one large data set average about 10 trial students and 6 waitlisted classes a month); take attendance; arrange make-up classes; cover absent instructors with a substitute; check overdue accounts and chase them; handle drop-outs and pauses; post cash, card and transfer payments; log room rental and private lessons; sell and restock dancewear and shoes; reply to parents (communication is the retention lever owners name); check which instructor is late with attendance or music/choreography for the recital.
**Monthly jobs:** run tuition billing (monthly billing is the main cycle, 62% of clients in one report); apply sibling, multi-class and family discounts (about 21-23% of classes carry a discount in that report); late fees and failed payments; pay instructors (per class, per hour, salary) and substitutes; pay rent, utilities, insurance, software and processing fees; monthly profit and loss and cash check; enrolment and drop count against the goal; owner pay and tax set-aside transfer; marketing spend against new sign-ups.
**Seasonal jobs:** (spring) re-enrolment for next season and registration fee; price rise decision (about half of surveyed studios planned a rise of around 5% for 2025-26); summer plan: camps, intensives, workshops, early-bird offers to fund the lean months; (summer) back-to-dance open days, trials, timetable launch; (autumn) new-season registration fees, costume sizes measured and costumes ordered with deposits per class; competition team audition, team fees and entry plan; (winter) costume balances, recital fees, venue booking, ticket plan, rehearsal schedule, programme ads; (spring) recital week: venue hire, tickets, backstage helpers, costume hand-out, recital cost per dancer; competition travel (entries, hotel, meals, coach time).
**Yearly jobs:** season budget and fee schedule; insurance renewal; instructor contracts and pay review; accountant pack; planning for rent renewal; succession and exit thinking (owners say fatigue is real).

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Unpaid fees on choreography, costume, competition and tuition are "a tough problem"; some parents fall behind only after costumes are ordered, "building up thousands of dollars in debt"; owner writes "I cannot afford to carry these debts". The studio's policy: 10% fee over 30 days late, removal from class after 60 days, written three-month payment plan to join recital or nationals trip. https://dance-teacher.com/ask-the-experts-dealing-with-overdue-payments/
- Profit is not cash: a $10,000 profit while cash falls from $20,000 to $15,000; income swings by month while costs stay steady; lean summer months; fixes named: pre-registration incentives, summer intensive, early-registration cash to cover rent, automatic billing, discounts for early or full payment, monthly cash-flow statement, name the three biggest cash transactions each month. https://dance-teacher.com/understanding-cash-flow/
- Staffing is "the hardest part"; higher payroll and thin margins make each missed class or unfilled slot costly; insurance, utilities and payroll rise unpredictably and do not track tuition; owners find managing finances "takes as much effort as teaching"; owners hesitate to raise tuition; bandwidth for parent communication is short. https://www.ensembleschools.com/blog/the-state-of-the-dance-studio-industry-in-the-2025-26-season/
- Costume admin is hidden labour: selecting, measuring, sizing, ordering, receiving, fitting, exchanges, returns, alterations; advice to treat the $25-30 per costume add-on as "a necessary operating cost" and to separate "cover costs" from "net a profit"; parents typically charged about $75-100 per recital costume. https://dance-teacher.com/ask-the-experts-how-much-profit-do-you-make-from-recital-costumes/
- Attrition: "a minimum turnover of approximately 20 percent" is normal in recreational programmes, with no fixed normal rate; losses cluster after the recital. https://dance-teacher.com/ask-the-experts-normal-dropout-rate/ ; https://dance-teacher.com/ask-the-experts-how-do-i-reduce-attrition-at-my-studio-after-recitals/ (title only; page not read)
- Competition cost to families is large and bundled, so owners must quote and collect it in instalments: one parent's year lists dances (solo 2,250; duo/trio 1,270; small group 1,050; large group 950; production 725), team fee 150, conventions 200-275, hotel from 150 a night, 5% discount for paying in full (Southern California, 2022; one family). https://danceparent101.com/how-much-it-costs-one-family-for-a-year-of-competitive-dance/
- Owner finance is thin: illustrative studio examples show 12.5% net margin on 80,000 revenue and 20-25% on larger studios (illustrations, not reported data); owner pay sites give 41,197 (Indeed) and 57,000 (Glassdoor) averages. https://www.thestudiodirector.com/?p=3912
- Software side (reviews): too many reports and hard to find the needed ones; parents struggle with auto-pay; tuition must be posted manually each month; high card fees; price rises; parent-portal login problems. https://www.capterra.com/p/131996/DanceStudio-Pro/reviews/
- NOT verified: forum threads, private owner groups, any measured late-payment or failed-payment rate (the large benchmark report has none).

## 2. Leading dance-studio software (research only)
Typical monthly prices seen: US product A from $49, "Plus" from $93, enterprise $245, counted by active and inactive students, with a $169 set-up fee on one tier, a $25 late fee and possible data deletion for non-payment; US product B $49 / $79 / $165 (unlimited students, texting limits by tier, app add-ons listed separately); AU product A$35.95-245.95 by customer band, add-on charges unlisted; UK products from about £29-69 by active students, from £34.99, or a free tier with 2.7% + 20p (UK) / 3.9% + 30c (US) per online payment. Processing rates often unpublished. Sources: https://classmanager.com/blog/how-much-does-class-management-software-really-cost-uk-usa-and-aus-pricing-compared (a vendor's own comparison, so treat as one-sided but the figures are list prices); https://www.capterra.com/p/131996/DanceStudio-Pro/reviews/ (4.5 of 5 from 52 reviews at the time read). Reviews counted earlier: 51, 87 and 56 reviews for three products (Research 13).
Features: class registration and online enrolment, parent portal and app, auto-pay and invoicing, attendance, waitlists, costume ordering and recital wizard, ticketing, payroll and time clock, retail point of sale, messaging, reports.
User complaints: report overload; manual monthly tuition posting; auto-pay set-up hard for parents; high processing fees; price increases; portal login problems; support slow by email (mixed); limited customising of rules and permissions. Praised: costume section and recital wizard.
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one flat 29 dollars with no per-student pricing, no processing fee and no set-up fee; owner-grade answers (profit by class, by room, by instructor, by family, by stream) instead of report lists; clear verdicts (keep, change, drop) for classes, camps, retail lines; planning tools software does not do (season budget, price-rise test, cash flow with the summer dip, costume and recital budget, competition team budget); works offline and keeps the owner's data; usable by a one-room studio.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Generic wording only. "Class" = the weekly class; "family" = household account.)

1. **Season timetable with room clash check.** One grid of room by day by hour with instructor, style, level and capacity; flag double-booked rooms and instructors, and report room hours used against room hours open, per room.
2. **Class profit per class.** Income (enrolled x tuition after discounts) minus instructor cost minus room-hour cost; show profit per class hour, rank all classes best to worst, and flag classes below break-even.
3. **Break-even dancers per class.** For each class: instructor pay plus room cost divided by net tuition per dancer; verdict "keep / merge / drop" when enrolment sits under the number for two months.
4. **Class fill rate and waitlist conversion.** Fill rate per class (enrolled over capacity); waitlist length; "open a second section" verdict when waitlist exceeds a set number of dancers; money lost on empty places.
5. **Tuition setting tool.** Cost per class hour, overhead per dancer, target margin and the local fee band the owner types in: a suggested monthly tuition per class length and a verdict "your fee is below / within / above".
6. **Price-rise test.** Enter a rise per class length and an expected drop-out share; result: extra income, break-even drop-out, verdict "raise / hold". Show last season's rise history.
7. **Family billing engine.** One family account with several dancers, sibling and multi-class discounts by editable rule, registration fee per dancer, and the monthly amount due; show the effective rate per class hour per family.
8. **Failed and late payment ladder.** Balance by family with ageing (current, 1-30, 31-60, 60+), late fee rule, days to removal rule, promise-to-pay date, and a ranked "chase first" list.
9. **Payment plan builder.** For costume, recital, trip or team balances: instalment dates and amounts, remaining balance, "behind plan" flag, and a signed-plan checkbox before the event.
10. **Enrolment and re-enrolment tracker.** Count by class and by month from summer to May against a goal; re-enrolment rate per class and per instructor; list of families not yet re-enrolled, ranked by value.
11. **Attrition by cause and timing.** Drop log with reason (moved, other activity, cost, schedule, level, teacher, injury as owner-reported) and month; show the post-recital drop bump; compare with own history and the 20% baseline as an editable target.
12. **Trial class conversion.** Trials per month, enrolled after trial, hours to first reply, conversion by source and by style; verdict on which marketing source pays.
13. **Attendance and make-up credits.** Attendance per class with absence count, make-up credit balance per dancer, expiry date, and "classes owed" for the month.
14. **Instructor pay engine.** Pay per class, per hour, per head or salary, with substitute rate; month-end pay sheet per instructor; check against hours taught.
15. **Instructor cost ratio.** Instructor cost as share of tuition by instructor and by class; ranked list; flag instructors whose classes lose money; compare with own history.
16. **Substitute cover log.** Who covered whom, rate, reason; monthly cover cost and "classes cancelled" count.
17. **Costume order sheet.** Per class: dancers, size, quantity, unit price, supplier order total, due dates; sizes ordered vs sizes received; exchanges and returns.
18. **Costume price and markup check.** Cost per costume plus shipping, tights/accessories, handling add-on; fee charged; margin per costume and total; verdict "fee covers cost? yes/no" and suggested fee for a target margin.
19. **Costume deposit and balance ledger.** Deposit due, paid, balance, due date per family; money collected before supplier payment is due; cash gap by week.
20. **Recital budget and break-even.** Venue hire, tech, programme, photos, hall helpers, costumes, ticket income, recital fees, ad income; result: profit per dancer, ticket price needed to break even, scenarios (sell-out / 70% / 50%).
21. **Ticket and seating plan.** Tickets by show, price tier, sold, comps, remaining; revenue vs plan; "price test" at a different price and sell-through.
22. **Rehearsal and production schedule.** Rehearsal dates by class and room, extra room hours and instructor cost, conflicts with the timetable.
23. **Competition team budget.** Entries per routine with entry fee per dancer (entry fee divided by dancers), choreography fee, team fee, costume, travel and hotel; cost per dancer and per family; fee to charge for a target margin.
24. **Competition entry planner.** Routines by category, dancers, entry fee, late-entry fee, early discount; compare 2-3 contests (fee + travel) and rank by cost per dancer.
25. **Team payment schedule.** Instalments per family from audition to last event with full-pay discount; paid vs due; "who is behind" list before each entry deadline.
26. **Travel and trip tool.** Hotel nights, rooms, meals, transport, chaperone costs split per dancer; per-dancer price and deposit; money in vs out.
27. **Private lesson and room rental book.** Bookings by room and hour, rate, no-shows, income per room hour; "is renting out this hour worth more than a class?" verdict.
28. **Summer camps and intensives.** Camp budget per week: sign-ups, price, instructors, room, supplies; break-even sign-ups; profit per camp week; ranked list of camps; verdict "run / change / skip".
29. **Birthday party and event pricing.** Price, room hours, helper pay, supplies; profit per party; minimum price for a target margin.
30. **Dancewear and shoe stock.** Stock by item and size, reorder point, cost, price, margin, days on shelf, dead-stock list, stock value; sales per month; verdict on lines to stop.
31. **Revenue by stream.** Tuition, registration, recital fees, costume fees, competition, camps, privates, rentals, retail, parties; share of revenue and margin by stream, compared with own history.
32. **Season cash-flow forecast with summer dip.** 12-month cash by week or month with registration spikes, costume deposits, venue payments, summer drop; lowest cash month and the month it occurs; shows how many months of rent are covered.
33. **Summer survival tool.** Enter planned camps, early-bird offers and cost cuts; shows the lowest summer cash and the shortfall; verdict "safe / tight / short".
34. **Owner pay and tax set-aside planner.** Profit before owner pay, set-aside share (editable; "confirm with your adviser"), owner draw by month, and what is left; pay-yourself-first check.
35. **Fixed-cost and rent check.** Rent share of revenue, cost per room hour used and open, peak hours (after school) vs off-peak; verdict on whether a room is worth its cost; "what-if we drop a room".
36. **One-page studio health check.** Scores 8 numbers at once with editable targets: instructor cost share, rent share, profit margin, fill rate, re-enrolment, overdue balance, cash cover months, revenue per room hour. Each green/amber/red with a plain verdict.
37. **Hire / add a class business case.** New instructor or new class: expected enrolment, ramp-up months, cost, break-even month, result under three scenarios.
38. **Small-studio mode.** One room, one teacher: settings that hide unused parts (competition team, retail, several rooms) so a one-room studio can use the core in 15 minutes.
39. **Enrolment forecast and scenarios.** Next season dancers by class from re-enrolment rate, waitlist, new sign-ups; income under low/mid/high; the number needed to cover costs.
40. **Start here, sample plus blank, and own-history comparison.** Each workbook has a "Start here" tab, a sample dataset and a blank copy, "Replace demo data" steps, works in Excel and Google Sheets without macros, and a "compare with my own last season" block (this month vs last month vs same month last year); a "Why a workbook" line: flat price, no per-student fee, your data stays with you.

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, platform, bank or company names anywhere in the product (workbooks, guides, shop copy).
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, programme, syllabus-board or standard names; say "your local rules", "your exam board" (generic phrase only), "your insurance renewal"; add "confirm with your adviser" on every tax or legal-looking cell.
- No medical or injury-treatment claims; injury notes are owner-reported incidents only; add "ask a health professional".
- No music, costume-maker or dancewear brand names; no named dance syllabi, contests or conventions.
- Child-safe, gender-neutral wording: "dancer", "parent or guardian", "family", "instructor"; no children's personal data beyond first name and class in samples; use made-up sample names.
- Use generic currency, editable demo values that work for US, Canada, UK and Australia; accept "term fees", "show/concert", "competition" (generic for eisteddfod/festival).
- State each benchmark as the owner's own editable "target", never as a fact about their studio; say "your own history first".
