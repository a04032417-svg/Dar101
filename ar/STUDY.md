# STUDY — independent auto repair shop / garage / workshop (market and workflow)

Method: 25 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-15 (the maximum). Evidence is thin in places and says so; "NOT verified" marks what could not be confirmed. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Earlier evidence (research6, Research 8 and 12) is built on, not repeated: a cheap estimate/invoice form (18 item reviews) and a vehicle repair log (5 reviews) from an 8.3k-sale shop at $1.24; no complete Excel back office seen.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner, service writer, lead technician):** open the day's bay schedule against technician hours and walk-ins; write estimates and repair orders (labour hours x labour rate plus parts with markup); call the customer for approval of extra work found during the job and log who approved, when and how; assign and dispatch jobs; clock technicians on and off jobs; receive parts deliveries and check them against orders; order parts for tomorrow; return wrong or core parts; take payment at pickup and close the repair order; count cars in and cars out; log comebacks; quick end-of-day cash check.
**Weekly jobs:** check next week's bays and booked jobs; technician hours billed vs hours clocked (efficiency) and billed vs available (productivity); declined-work follow-up calls; service-reminder calls and texts by mileage or date; supplier statements and returns/cores credit chase; fleet and business account invoices and overdue chase; pay run (flat-rate, hourly, salary plus bonus); car count and average repair order; shop-supplies charge check; comebacks review with the lead technician; weekly cash and bills; review requests.
**Monthly jobs:** close the month: sales split labour / parts / sublet / supplies; gross profit on labour and on parts; effective labour rate against posted rate; payroll as share of sales; parts markup matrix review; supplier statements matched to invoices; fleet receivable ageing (30/60/90); comeback and warranty cost; declined-work value recovered; tool and equipment upkeep list; marketing source review; owner pay and tax set-aside transfer; profit and loss against budget.
**Yearly jobs:** labour rate and markup-matrix review (notice to fleet accounts); licence, insurance and equipment-inspection renewal dates (generic dates only, status only); waste-disposal status checks (status only); technician pay-plan and bonus review; training plan; budget and capital plan (lift, scanner, alignment gear); inventory count; year-end pack for the accountant; fleet contract renewals; warranty terms review.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Hiring and pay pressure. Owners on a public trade forum: ads, postcards and school contacts produced "none actually showed up"; one mailed 25,000 postcards with no responses in three weeks; fast-food pay "$15-18 per hour" mentioned as competition; one owner says rent rose about $4 per produced hour and fixed costs added "$8.81 per billable hour"; "everything on average right now is up between 7.5-16%". Several pad labour times 10-30% for rust or slow work. https://www.autoshopowner.com/forums/topic/18389-hiring-techs-now-impossible
- Pay plan dilemma. "There are techs who thrive under the pressure of flat rate"; flat rate used "to cut labor expense rather than make technicians more efficient"; "a hybrid pay system is best as it rewards both quality and speed"; a clock-hours technician should still get credit for time on waived or diagnostic work (so billed hours and clocked hours diverge). Most of the thread is behind a membership (partly verified). https://www.autoshopowner.com/forums/topic/19597-technician-pay-plans-is-there-a-right-choice
- Customer-supplied parts: owners double the labour or the margin they lose ("I might as well make a killing installing them"), some refuse entirely, and warranty is disputed ("we cannot warranty the repair, and we want to guarantee our work"). The thread is from 2015 (old). https://www.autoshopowner.com/forums/topic/9849-dealing-with-customer-supplied-parts-lets-share-ideas/
- Effective labour rate leak: posted rate 150 but effective 112 in one worked example; gap more than 10-15% flagged as a recurring leak (article, not owner words). https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/19/auto-repair-shop-bookkeeping-flat-rate-vs-clock-hours-parts-matrix-markup-comeback-warranty-receivables-sublet-epa-hazmat-shop-supplies-effective-labor-rate-guide
- Warranty/comeback money arrives slowly: warranty reimbursement 30-90 days, often below posted rate; claims age past 60 days; fleet accounts build receivables (same article).
- Parts markup out of date: 67% of 618 surveyed US general repair shops reportedly need to update their parts markup strategy (a vendor survey, summary page only). https://get.partstech.com/report
- Declined and deferred work is under-followed: one owner found "$3.3 million" of potential revenue from two years of deferred jobs in his own database; "decline means never, deferred means later" (podcast page). https://getcarcurious.com/episodes/deferred-work-the-most-overlooked-growth-opportunity-in-auto-repair-rr-1101
- UK pricing confusion: garage rates vary from 47 to 141 an hour (section 5). Owners in some regions feel stuck below competitors; one says under $100 an hour cannot sustain a shop in his area (forum above).
- NOT verified: reddit and podcast transcripts (search returned titles only); owner words on bay scheduling and tool upkeep; fleet payment terms.

## 2. Leading auto-repair shop management software (research only)
Pricing per month seen (CostBench, 2026, https://www.costbench.com/software/automotive-shop-software/): median about $246 across 7 tools with published prices; typically per shop/location, sometimes tiered by users or features. Examples: one product $99-449 (tiers 199/349/449); one $69.99-149.99; one $199-299; one $249-999 (tiers 279/389/499); one $199-439 (199/349/439); a heavy-duty product $188-318; one free tier for a parts-lookup tool ($0/45/85); some custom quotes. Hidden costs listed: add-ons for digital inspections, marketing, customer texting; payment-processing rates; parts/labour data subscriptions; one-time onboarding, training and migration. A reviewer on Capterra called a $250 starter tier "kind of expensive for startup businesses" (one user, March 2025).
Features offered: estimates and repair orders, digital vehicle inspections with photos, text approvals, scheduling, technician time tracking, parts ordering and catalogues, inventory, labour guides, invoicing, customer reminders, reports, accounting links.
User complaints (Capterra reviews of two leading products): "everything takes a million mouse clicks" and 30-40 clicks before estimate building; labour guide "almost always wrong" or "less robust" than a rival; parts integration problems and weak handling of parts returns and warranties; no separate schedule per technician and cannot split a job between technicians; reports "a little on the light side" or lacking data access; no ability to reorder recommendations; no auto-fill; inventory too rigid for high-volume shops; new releases sometimes add bugs; "a sales tool and not an accounting program" (extra steps for the books); no quick phone quote or counter-sale option. Praise: easy to learn, support, cloud access, text approvals, inspections. https://www.capterra.com/p/190952/Tekmetric/reviews/?page=2 , https://www.capterra.com/p/153469/Shop-Ware/reviews/
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one flat 29 dollars once (vs about 250 a month, about 3,000 a year at the median); a quick phone quote tool; owner-level answers that software leaves "light" (effective labour rate by technician, parts margin by supplier, comeback cost, declined-work value, fleet ageing, profit per bay hour); technician-by-technician views; your own labour times and markup matrix, not a paid labour guide; one-bay owner-mechanic can use it without staff; data stays with the owner; books-ready monthly summary. It does not replace live parts catalogues or payment processing and must say so plainly.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Every item generic: no brand, law, agency, licence or programme names. "Settings" = editable targets, never hard-coded.)

1. **Repair order with live margin.** Repair order builder: labour hours x rate, parts cost with markup from the matrix, shop supplies, tax; shows gross profit and margin per order and flags orders below target before the customer is quoted.
2. **Quick phone quote.** One-screen quote for a caller (job, hours, parts cost, vehicle type) with a good/better/best price and a "call back" log; software users complain they cannot do this fast.
3. **Parts markup matrix tool.** Editable cost-tier table (example tiers shown as demo values); every sale priced by tier; "blended parts margin vs target" and a check that lists parts sold below the matrix.
4. **Markup test.** Scenario tool: change the matrix by +/-5 points, see effect on yearly profit using last 12 months of parts sold; ends with a verdict "raise / hold".
5. **Labour rate decision tool.** Inputs: wages, overhead per hour, target profit, billable hours; output: break-even rate and rate for target margin, effect of a 5, 8, 10% rise; verdict.
6. **Effective labour rate by technician and by month.** Labour sales / billed hours vs posted rate; flag a gap over the Settings threshold (default 10%); list the jobs causing the gap (discounts, waived diagnostics, wrong hours).
7. **Technician efficiency and productivity by person.** Billed hours / clocked hours and billed / available hours per technician per week, with bands (under 80%, 80-100%, 100-120%, 120%+), a rank list and a trend against own history.
8. **Bay schedule and capacity.** Week grid by bay and technician with booked hours vs available hours; shows free hours, overbooked bays and the next free slot for a new job.
9. **Walk-in vs booked capacity rule.** Reserve a share of bay hours for walk-ins; tool shows how many more booked jobs can be accepted today and when to turn work away.
10. **Estimate approval log.** Each extra-work request: what, price, who approved, how, when; "waiting for approval" list ranked by age and bay hours blocked; time lost waiting.
11. **Declined-work follow-up list.** Every declined or deferred item with value, reason, follow-up date; ranked by value and age; "value recovered this month" and "value still open" KPIs; script helper text.
12. **Declined-work rate and recovery.** Declined value / recommended value by service writer and by service type; compare with own history; forecast of recovery at a settable follow-up rate.
13. **Service reminders by mileage and date.** Last service date and odometer, interval, estimated next date from average daily miles; list "due in 30 days", "overdue", "contacted"; response rate to reminders.
14. **Car count and average repair order.** Weekly and monthly car count, ARO, hours per RO, and revenue per bay; compare with own last year; "what ARO do I need to cover costs at this car count".
15. **Break-even car count.** Fixed costs per month, gross profit per car; break-even cars per week and per bay; "safe / tight / loss" verdict.
16. **Technician pay plan comparison.** Same technician under flat-rate, hourly, salary plus bonus: pay cost per billed hour, shop margin per hour, and which plan costs the shop least at their own efficiency; verdict and a bonus-rule test (efficiency bonus + comeback deduction).
17. **Technician pay run.** Pay by plan from billed and clocked hours, overtime, bonus, comeback deduction; cost per billed hour; totals tie to payroll share of sales.
18. **Payroll share of sales.** Total pay / sales by month with a Settings target band; split technicians, writer, owner; traffic light; own history trend.
19. **Technician profit table.** Per technician: billed hours, sales, parts attached, comebacks, pay cost, gross profit; ranked list so the owner knows who earns the shop its margin.
20. **Comeback log and cost.** Each comeback: original RO, cause (diagnosis, part, workmanship, communication), free labour, free parts; comeback rate vs sales and per technician; "cost of comebacks this year".
21. **Own warranty tracker.** Warranty period by job type, open warranty claims, parts-supplier credit expected; ageing of expected credits (30/60/90).
22. **Parts ordering list.** Daily buy list from open ROs; supplier, price, expected delivery, received yes/no; late-parts alert linked to the bay that is blocked.
23. **Supplier comparison.** Same part from two or three suppliers: price, delivery speed, return friendliness, error rate; "best supplier by category" and spend by supplier.
24. **Returns and cores.** Core charge owed, core returned, credit received, ageing of unclaimed credit; "money waiting at suppliers" KPI.
25. **Supplier statement check.** Statement lines matched to invoices received; shows differences, duplicate charges and missing credits; ends with amount to pay.
26. **Shop supplies charge tool.** Test flat vs percent-of-labour (with cap) against actual consumables cost; shows recovered share and under-recovery; verdict.
27. **Fleet and business accounts.** Account list with terms, credit limit, rate/discount, monthly billing; ageing 30/60/90; credit-limit warning; "stop work" rule list.
28. **Fleet profitability check.** Per fleet account: sales, discount given, effective labour rate, days to pay; "keep, reprice, drop" verdict; late-payer ranking.
29. **Receivables chase list.** Overdue invoices ranked by age and amount, promised date, last contact; cash expected in 7 / 30 days.
30. **Cash flow forecast.** 13-week forecast from booked jobs, payroll, rent, parts payable, taxes; low-cash week warning; a scenario for a slow week or a late fleet payer.
31. **Gross profit by service type.** Brakes, suspension, diagnostics, tyres, oil service, inspections, electrical: sales, labour margin, parts margin, hours; ranked; "what to sell more of / price up" verdict.
32. **Diagnostic time check.** Diagnostic hours sold vs clocked by technician; unbilled diagnostic cost; test of a minimum diagnostic charge.
33. **Maintenance vs repair mix.** Share of sales; effect of shifting five points to maintenance; repeat-visit rate.
34. **Tools and equipment upkeep.** Item, service interval, last done, next due (generic dates), cost; replace-or-repair test for a lift or scanner with payback months.
35. **Renewals and compliance dates (status only).** Licence, insurance, equipment inspection, waste-disposal status: dates, 30 and 60 day alerts, owner, done/not done; no legal wording.
36. **Marketing and reviews.** Source of each new customer (search, referral, drive-by, fleet), spend, cars and revenue by source, cost per new customer; review requests sent vs received; "keep/drop a channel" verdict.
37. **Owner pay and tax set-aside.** Profit after payroll; owner pay level the shop can afford; set-aside percent as a Settings value with "confirm with your adviser"; cash cushion in months.
38. **One-page monthly scorecard.** Ten ratios (ARO, car count, effective labour rate, efficiency, productivity, labour margin, parts margin, comeback rate, payroll share, receivables over 60 days) vs own history and vs Settings targets, with a sentence of verdict for each.
39. **One-bay owner-mechanic mode.** A switch or Start-here path that hides staff columns; owner counts as the only technician; simple day sheet, hours billed vs hours worked, and a money-per-hour test so the owner sees what an hour of own time earns.
40. **Packaging for the shop listing.** A "Start here" tab in each workbook with sample data and a clear "replace demo data" step; no macros; opens in Excel and Google Sheets; one-line "Why a workbook" block (flat price, no monthly fee, data stays with you, owner reports that software leaves light); local words list (garage, workshop, service centre; labour/labor) and one editable tax percent and currency.

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, platform, bank or company names anywhere in the product, descriptions or listing.
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, programme or standard names; write "your local rules", "your licence or insurance renewal", "your annual inspection". Waste disposal and renewals are generic dates and status only.
- No vehicle-maker, parts-maker or tool brand names; vehicles as type + year + generic model words (for example "compact sedan, 2016", "pickup truck, 2012").
- No safety guarantees, no emissions or inspection programme names, no claim that a vehicle is safe or compliant; the warranty tool tracks the shop's own terms only.
- Tax cells say "confirm with your adviser"; no tax-form or tax-code names.
- Gender-neutral wording (technician, owner, service writer, "they").
- Accept US/UK/CA/AU words as labels (garage, workshop, service centre, labour/labor, tyre/tire) without naming local schemes; one editable currency and tax percent.
- Do not copy competitor text; targets (margins, efficiency bands, comeback rate) are editable Settings with a note "starting values, set your own"; benchmarks stay inside STUDY.md.
