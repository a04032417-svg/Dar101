# STUDY — small lawn care and landscaping company, 2-4 crews (US first; Canada, UK, Australia accepted) — market and workflow

Method: 25 web calls (searches and fetches) on 2026-11-15, building on the earlier "Research 8" note (Etsy listing 4389637035 and 4583129261 re-fetched live; the earlier finding that the niche ranks in the top third with low competition is taken from that note and was not re-checked). Evidence is thin in places and says so. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Many web pages here are vendor blogs and affiliate reviews, so their numbers are marked weak unless a trade survey stands behind them.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner, crew leads):** check the weather and decide which routes run; load the day's route sheet and hand each crew its stops; confirm trucks, trailers, mowers, fuel and the day's material are loaded; log hours per crew member and per stop (start, finish, drive); mark each visit done, skipped (gate locked, rain) or extra work found; photo or note of problems (damage, dead patch, irrigation leak) for a follow-up quote; end-of-day fuel and equipment faults; reschedule rain-outs into the next days without breaking other routes; answer calls and texts from clients (skip this week, add a clean-up, price query).
**Weekly jobs:** build next week's routes by day and by crew (neighbourhood clusters, weekly vs bi-weekly visits, rain carry-over); crew assignment and hours against a weekly labour budget; overtime check; invoice or bill finished visits (per visit, monthly flat, season contract); chase unpaid balances; quote follow-up (estimates sent but not answered); equipment hours and blade/oil checks; fuel by truck; order material for install jobs (soil, mulch, stone, plants, edging) and dump fees; payroll hours to payroll; lead and referral inbox; check the crew's finished-job photos and punch list; week cash check.
**Monthly jobs:** monthly flat-fee billing run and card or bank collection; statements for commercial clients; job costing for finished jobs (labour hours, material, dump fees, fuel, equipment hours against the quoted price); profit by service line (mowing, seasonal programs, install, snow); profit by route and by client (find the losing lots); overdue ageing; equipment repair and replacement tracking; seasonal-program visit counts (planned vs done); cancellations and reasons; new clients by source; payroll review and seasonal-crew headcount; tax set-aside transfer; owner draw; cash forecast across the slow months.
**Yearly jobs:** price rise and notice to all recurring clients (the owner-side job with the most reported pain: see below); renewing season contracts and sending next season's program; snow contracts (per-visit, per-season, per-event) signed before first frost; hiring and onboarding seasonal crews; equipment replacement plan and winter service; licence and insurance renewal reminders (generic wording only); winter work plan (snow, holiday lights, pruning, design sales); year-end pack for the accountant; budget with the seasonal curve (spring ramp, summer peak, autumn leaf peak, winter dip in cold regions); win-back list of lapsed clients.

**Pain points reported (with sources and honest weight):**
- **Price rises across many recurring accounts are slow to do and risky.** One review calls the lack of bulk editing "the single most common complaint on every lawn care forum": raising a price across 40 weekly accounts means "40 individual edits". Weight: one affiliate review, the claim about forums NOT verified. https://mowbosshq.pages.dev/guides/jobber-review
- **Customers leave over how a price rise is announced, and over missed visits, sloppy edging and slow callbacks.** The page says the callers about price are the ones likely to cancel. Weight: vendor blog, assertions without data. https://www.applausehq.com/blog/lawn-care-customer-retention-in-2026
- **Per-user software costs add up as crews grow; add-ons are pushed.** "The team tiers ($169-$599) add up fast once you're paying $29/month per additional user"; a marketing add-on costs $79 a month extra; some reviewers feel pressured toward paid add-ons. Weight: affiliate review, March 2026 prices. Same mowbosshq URL.
- **Routing in the lower plans is "basic".** Weight: one review. Same URL.
- **Seasonal cash flow: winter dip and slow collection.** Advice to owners: set aside part of summer income as a cushion; keep a payroll buffer of "1-2 months of payroll buffer minimum"; sell prepaid season packages with an early-bird discount; add spring clean-up and autumn leaf work to keep crews busy. Weight: vendor and community advice, no winter figures given. https://www.joist.com/workshop/cashflow-payments/7-tips-for-seasonal-cash-flow-management-for-lawn-care-businesses/ , https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/entrepreneurship-forum/looking-to-scale-need-advice/3730/replies/3746
- **"You can't scale doing everything"; start with one part-time lead, not five employees; a full booking calendar may mean rates are too low.** Advice from an outside writer to one owner, not the owner's own words. Same community URL.
- **Seasonal businesses are getting harder, not just because of weather** (headline of a vendor blog; body not read). https://www.serviceautopilot.com/field-service/why-seasonal-service-business-challenges-are-getting-harder-and-its-not-just-the-weather/
- **Rain days:** a podcast episode is titled "If rain ruins your week, you have a bigger problem" (title only; episode not heard). https://growinggreenlandscapespodcast.buzzsprout.com/1795533/episodes/19102610-if-rain-ruins-your-week-you-have-a-bigger-problem
- Depositing and prepay: advice pieces exist on collecting installation deposits and on motivating customers to prepay; NOT read, so no percentages are claimed.
- NOT verified: real forum threads in owners' own words (searches returned vendor blogs and review sites; the one community thread read held advice, not an owner's complaint). Treat the pain list as moderate evidence.

## 2. Leading lawn-care / field-service software (research only)
**Monthly prices seen** (CostBench category page, 2026, https://costbench.com/software/lawn-care-software/): the field-service tool most used by small crews is priced per user ($29-$149 per user per month across three plan levels); one scheduling and billing tool $49-$499 a month; one route and billing tool $129-$499 (three plans); one estimating-and-job-costing tool $297-$648; one mid-market tool $220-$550; several others "custom quote". Median about $339 a month across the four tools that publish prices (small sample). An affiliate review (March 2026) lists the per-user tool as: single-user plans $39 / $119 / $199 monthly ($29 / $84 / $140 billed annually); team plans $169 (5 users), $349 (10 users), $599 (15 users); each extra user $29; marketing add-on $79. Hidden costs listed by CostBench: card and invoice processing fees, per-user charges as crews grow, add-ons for marketing, routing and text reminders, set-up and onboarding fees, optional online booking and review requests.
**Features offered:** client list, quotes and proposals, job scheduling, route lists, crew assignment and time tracking, recurring jobs, invoicing and card payments, online booking, text reminders, estimating by measured area or by material, job costing and budgets (the pricier ones), equipment and chemical-application records (field-service specific tools), snow route tools, reports.
**User complaints (weight: reviews by others, not read first-hand on the review site):** no bulk editing of recurring prices; basic routing below the higher plans; per-user fees as the team grows; add-ons; the more powerful estimating and job-costing tools cost several hundred dollars a month and take setup time. Capterra review pages for the main tools were returned by search but not read: NOT verified.
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one flat 29 dollars, no per-user fee and no add-ons; a "price rise" tool that applies a percent or dollar rise to every recurring account at once, with the new monthly revenue, the expected loss of clients and the net result; a profit-by-lot and by-route finder that ranks the visits that lose money; a job-costing sheet that compares the quote with actual labour hours, material, dump and fuel; a seasonal cash forecast with the winter dip; works for a solo operator and scales to four crews; owner keeps the data; no setup fee, no onboarding call.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Every feature is a real owner job with the calculation it needs. Keep wording generic: no brand, code, law, agency or licence names. "Part n" refers to the eight suggested parts listed after item 40.)

1. **Part 3 (price rise tool).** "Raise my prices" tool: a percent or dollar rise applied to every recurring account at once, by lot size band or route, with a "client loss you can stand" input; shows new monthly revenue, expected clients lost, break-even loss percent and a verdict ("raise", "raise half now", "hold") — answers the most reported pain (40 individual edits).
2. **Part 3 (per-visit price by lot).** Price-per-visit calculator from measured area, mow rate (area per hour), crew size, drive time, labour cost with on-costs, equipment cost per hour, overhead per hour and target margin; ends with price per visit, minimum stop charge and a flag if the price is more than 15% above the owner's own local price entered.
3. **Part 3 (profit by lot and by route).** Ranked list of every recurring stop by profit per visit-hour, with the bottom 10 as "fix or drop" and the cost of keeping each losing stop for the season.
4. **Part 3 (minimum stop and tiny lots).** Decision tool for small lots: price per visit versus drive and setup time; verdict "take, raise, or decline".
5. **Part 2 (route build).** Weekly route sheet by day and crew: visits, stops, area, expected time per stop plus drive minutes; flags a day over the crew's working hours and shows the balance between crews.
6. **Part 2 (drive-time share).** Drive minutes as a share of paid crew hours per day, with a band set by the owner and a compare with own last four weeks; shows the cost of drive time in dollars a week.
7. **Part 2 (rain-day reschedule).** Rain-out planner: the day's lost stops, free capacity on the next days, overtime cost versus skipped visits; verdict "catch up Saturday", "spread over three days" or "skip and credit"; remembers rain days per month for the forecast.
8. **Part 2 (crew hours and overtime).** Hours per crew member per week against a limit; shows when a helper would cross overtime and what moving one stop saves.
9. **Part 2 (visits per crew per day).** Count and hours per crew per day against the owner's own best weeks, as the productivity compare with own history; no outside number is claimed.
10. **Part 3 (estimate by measured area).** Estimate builder for install and seasonal work: area by service, rate per area, material in cubic measure, waste percent, delivery, dump fee, labour hours by task, markup; printable quote with deposit and payment stages.
11. **Part 3 (material take-off).** Cubic-yard (cubic-metre) calculator for mulch, soil, stone, sod (turf) from area and depth, with waste percent and cost.
12. **Part 3 (quote follow-up).** Estimates sent, days open, win or lose reason, win rate by service and by size; ranked list "quotes to chase today"; win rate compared with own last 12 months.
13. **Part 3 (lost-quote causes).** Cause split: price, timing, found another provider, no reply, scope changed; shows the money lost by cause.
14. **Part 4 (job costing against quote).** For every install job: quoted labour hours and material versus actual hours, actual material, dump fees and fuel; margin result and the largest overrun line; verdict "price up next time" or "on target".
15. **Part 4 (labour hours per job line).** Learning table: actual hours per unit (per square metre of paving, per bed, per hedge) from finished jobs, to feed the next estimate.
16. **Part 4 (maintenance vs install margin check).** Two margin lines side by side (maintenance and install) against the owner's own targets, with the survey figures offered as a one-time "typical for larger firms" reference note, not a hard-coded target.
17. **Part 4 (subcontract and extras).** Subcontract cost and client extras added during a job, with a flag when extras are not yet billed.
18. **Part 5 (recurring billing run).** Monthly billing for per-visit, monthly flat and season contracts: visits done, skipped and credited, amounts due, invoice list; checks that each flat-fee client was billed.
19. **Part 5 (aged balances).** Ageing buckets (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+) with a "chase today" ranked list and a payment-promise date column so chasing is logged by outcome.
20. **Part 5 (late-payment rule).** Owner-set rule (days, late fee percent, stop-service days) applied to every overdue account; shows fees due and clients at the stop-service line.
21. **Part 5 (deposit and stage payments).** For install jobs: deposit percent, stage payments and final payment, cash needed before the job starts (material, labour) and the cash gap if the deposit is too low; owner sets percentages.
22. **Part 5 (card or auto-pay on file).** Share of recurring clients with a payment method on file and the effect on late balances; "money not chased" estimate.
23. **Part 6 (seasonal program planner).** Planned visits per program (aeration, overseeding, feed, weed control in plain words, leaf clean-up) by month, visits done and visits due, with a count of clients not yet enrolled; status and quantities only, "follow the product label and your local rules".
24. **Part 6 (upsell finder).** Clients with mowing but no seasonal program, ranked by lot size and tenure, with the revenue if 10, 20 or 30% enrol.
25. **Part 6 (application records).** Status-only record per visit: date, area covered, quantity used, crew member, done or not; no product advice, no label values.
26. **Part 6 (snow contract compare).** Per-push versus per-season versus per-event quote tool: expected pushes from the owner's own history, salt and fuel, crew hours, equipment; verdict on which contract to offer and the break-even snowfall count.
27. **Part 6 (winter work plan).** Winter revenue plan (snow, pruning, holiday work, design sales) against the winter fixed cost, with the gap to cover.
28. **Part 7 (equipment cost per hour).** Per machine: purchase price, expected life in hours, fuel per hour, repairs, blades and parts, insurance; owning and operating cost per hour, used inside the price-per-visit tool; ranked list "most expensive machine per hour".
29. **Part 7 (service hours and next due).** Hours since last service by machine, next-due flag (amber/red), repair log with cost, and "repair or replace" verdict when repair spending over 12 months passes a share of replacement cost.
30. **Part 7 (fuel by truck).** Litres or gallons, cost and distance per truck per week; fuel as a share of revenue; flag trucks that depart from their own average by more than a set percent.
31. **Part 7 (renewals).** Generic "renewal due" register for licences, insurance, vehicle checks and operator training, red at 30 days and amber at 45, with no names of licences or programs.
32. **Part 8 (customer retention).** Client list with start date, service, price, status; retention per season, churn by cause, lost revenue; win-back list ranked by value; compare each season with the owner's own previous season.
33. **Part 8 (leads and referrals).** Enquiries by source, hours to first reply, quote rate, win rate, value per client by source; "keep, renegotiate, drop" verdict for paid lead sources with months to pay back.
34. **Part 8 (hiring a seasonal crew member).** Business case: wage, on-costs, tools and uniform, training time, extra stops per week, extra revenue and margin; verdict "hire now", "wait" or "overtime is cheaper".
35. **Part 8 (new truck or crew).** What-if tool: one more crew, one more route; needed stops per week to cover the cost; months to pay back.
36. **Part 8 (seasonal cash forecast).** 12-month cash view with the winter dip, payroll, loan and equipment payments, tax set-aside and owner draw; lowest cash month and "months of cash" cover against an owner-set floor (suggested 1-2 months of payroll as a starting default, flagged as community advice).
37. **Part 8 (owner draw and tax set-aside).** Safe draw per month from the forecast, tax set-aside percent on profit (owner-set; "confirm with your adviser"), and a check that the draw does not break the cash floor.
38. **Part 8 (scoreboard).** One-page "Is the business healthy?" with gross margin by service, labour share, net margin, overdue share, retention, cash cover, each with the owner's own target, a verdict colour and a compare with own last season.
39. **Shared (solo mode and sizes).** Every part works with one crew or four: crew names are a list, not hard-coded; a "solo operator" tab path that hides crew columns; metric and imperial areas and currencies chosen on a settings tab; words follow UK/AU variants as labels (round, quote, tip fee, fortnightly).
40. **Shared (packaging and positioning).** "Start here" tab in every workbook with "replace demo data" steps, sample plus blank copies, "no macros", "works in Excel and Google Sheets", a one-page quick-start, and a one-line "why a workbook" block (flat price, no per-user fee, no add-ons, your data stays with you, reports built for owners: price rise, profit by lot, job costing, winter cash).

## Product wording rules
Hard rules for everything the product says (workbook text, tab names, help text, shop listing, images):
- No brand, software, platform, marketplace, payment-processor, bank or company names. Say "your payment provider", "your accounting tool" or "your bank".
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, program or standard names, and no tax-form or code names. Say "your local rules", "your licence or insurance renewal", "confirm with your adviser".
- No chemical-product or brand names and no agronomic or safety guarantees. Seasonal programs (aeration, overseeding, fertilising, weed control) appear only as status and quantities (date, area, quantity, crew member, done or not). Add the line "Follow the product label and your local rules."
- No claims of results or earnings ("raises profit by x%", "saves y hours") and no invented reviews, sales counts or benchmarks. Typical figures appear only as optional notes flagged "typical for larger firms, set your own".
- Gender-neutral wording everywhere (owner, crew lead, helper, client, the client's, they).
- Words follow the buyer's market through labels: round (route), quote (estimate), tip fee (dump fee), fortnightly (bi-weekly) are accepted as alternates, never as brand terms.
- Sample data uses invented first names only and invented streets; no real addresses, phone numbers or company names.
- Say once that the workbook is a record and planning tool, not accounting, tax or legal advice.
