# STUDY — home daycare and small childcare centre (30-60 children, 3-4 age rooms, 5-10 staff)

Method: 25 web calls (searches and fetches) on 2026-11-15, on top of the Etsy findings in /home/claude/work/research6_20261115.md ("Research 7"), which are not repeated beyond a table row. Evidence is thin in places and says so. Anything not read on the page is marked NOT verified. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner-director, often with a deputy):** open the setting and check who is booked in by room; sign children in and out and record real hours; check staff-to-child ratio per room against who is actually present, at opening, at lunch cover and at close; adjust cover when a staff member is off; check allergy and medical-permission status before meals; run meals and snacks to the menu; log any accident or incident; send the daily report to parents; take payments handed over at pickup; charge a late-pickup fee when the clock passes closing; answer enquiries and book tours.
**Weekly jobs:** next week's attendance and part-time schedule; staff rota, breaks and relief cover; weekly tuition invoices for families billed weekly; chase unpaid balances; waitlist check (who can start in which room on which date); tours; menu and shopping list; cleaning and safety checks; room-by-room headcount against capacity; payroll hours.
**Monthly jobs:** monthly tuition invoices; sibling and part-time adjustments; ageing of balances (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+); deposits and registration fees posted; third-party / government childcare payments matched to families (generic words); meal-program claim for the month (generic words); occupancy by room and revenue per enrolled child; staff cost share of revenue; cost per child-hour; cash flow and bills; emergency-drill log check; staff training hours and renewals due; newsletter; marketing and referral source review; summer camp and after-school fill; home-use share of the house for a home daycare (generic words).
**Yearly jobs:** fee rise and notice to parents (30-60 days is typical); re-registration and supply fees; room moves when children age up (infant to toddler to preschool, usually in a set month); parent conferences and observation / portfolio status; policy review; background-check and training renewals ("renewal due" only); budget; payment statements parents use to claim childcare costs back (year-end statement); records archive dates; summer camp plan; tax pack for the adviser.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Billing and payment friction is the loudest owner theme: owners enter tuition by hand into spreadsheets, chase late payers, issue invoices that do not match attendance, and meet "angry parents" when software bills wrongly. https://www.himama.com/blog/childcare-billing-payment-guide/ (vendor blog, so treat as marketing-grade); https://news.dailyconnect.com/2026/02/03/daycare-tuition-collection (vendor); a director's Trustpilot review describes parents' anger over billing (https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/mybrightwheel.com).
- Processing fees eat margin and anger parents: one vendor page's worked example shows 60 families at 1,000 a month paying a 1% fee = 600 a month; parents' reviews quote a 3% card fee and 30+ bank fees they did not expect. https://mykidreports.com/blog/how-much-does-childcare-software-cost-a-2026-pricing-guide (vendor page, example only); Trustpilot page above.
- Cost structure leaves no room: staff pay is more than 70% of expenses in the three centres in a New Hampshire cost primer, and one of the three lost money, one broke even, one had a surplus. https://carsey.unh.edu/publication/operating-thin-margins-cost-providing-new-hampshire-child-care (research source, strong).
- Staff turnover: mean 40% a year among 575 publicly funded Louisiana centres in 2015-2019; about a third of centres lost more than half their teachers in a year. https://www.edworkingpapers.com/ai21-474 (research paper, strong but old and one state).
- Reports in the software are not built for the owner's money questions: Capterra reviews of the biggest product say "no way to project future revenue", "can't assign a student to multiple classes", "too many unused reports", PC-only and outage complaints. https://www.capterra.com/p/23486/Procare-Child-Care-Management/reviews/?page=50 (one page of 2,809 reviews; mixed to positive overall, rating 4.6).
- Support and usability: a director says support calls "must be scheduled in advance" and are capped at 15 minutes; an educator says one lesson-plan row takes "no less than 16 clicks"; another reviewer calls cancelling "nearly impossible". https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/mybrightwheel.com (score shown 1.3 of 5 from 308 reviews, but the page's own star breakdown contradicts that score, so use only as a list of complaints, not as a rating).
- Tour-to-enrolment leaks and empty places: vendor blogs talk about tour no-shows and slow follow-up. https://blog.lineleader.com/tour-to-enrollment-revenue-leak-in-childcare (search result only, not read; NOT verified).
- NOT verified: owners' own words on forums (searches returned vendor blogs, not readable forum threads). All quotes above come from review pages.

## 2. Leading childcare-management software (research only)
Pricing seen (2026, mostly vendor pages, so weak):
- Per child: 0.99 to 3+ a child a month (https://mykidreports.com/blog/how-much-does-childcare-software-cost-a-2026-pricing-guide); one product listed at 2.25 per active child and one at 1.59 (https://costbench.com/software/childcare-management/; the 1.59 unit is unclear).
- Per site: about 50 to 300+ a month regardless of enrolment (same MyKidReports page).
- Free for up to 5 children and about 99 for 100 children at one vendor (same page).
- Six of eight products on the CostBench page show only "custom pricing"; no median can be given. Large products (Procare, Kangarootime, Jackrabbit and others) are quote-only.
- Hidden costs named: payment-processing fees on autopay tuition (ACH and card), set-up and onboarding fees, per-staff seats at some vendors, paid add-ons (billing, learning, website), annual contracts with auto-renewal, export or cancellation fees.
Features: enrolment and waitlist, check-in/out and attendance, staff scheduling, tuition billing and autopay, parent messages with photos and daily reports, ratio and compliance support, reports.
Complaints (reviews, see section 1): processing fees surprise parents; billing errors; support hard to reach; clunky screens; reports hard to find and none that project revenue; outages; PC-only on one product; price too high for small programs (Capterra, one reviewer).
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** flat 29 once, no per-child or per-site fee and no processing cut; revenue forecast by room and start date; ratio check by hour from the rota; true cost per child-hour and break-even occupancy; the owner's own numbers and own history; a working home-daycare mode (one room, mixed ages); no outages, no contract, data kept by the owner. What a spreadsheet cannot do (say so in the product): live parent app, photo sharing, kiosk check-in, card collection.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. "Part n" = suggested part below. Each item is a job with its calculation. No brand, law or licence names in the product.)

1. **Part 3: ratio by hour per room.** Rota by 30-minute slot against children present (from booked hours); for each room and slot: staff needed = children / ratio (rounded up), staff present, gap; verdict "covered / short by n / over-staffed by n". Ratio is an editable input per age room labelled "check your local rules".
2. **Part 3: opening-to-closing cover heat map.** Count of slots short per week by room, ranked, to show where relief hours are needed; end with the weekly "short hours" total.
3. **Part 3: break planner.** Each staff member's break slots checked so a break never drops a room below ratio; list of clashes with the fix "move to".
4. **Part 3: staff cost per child-hour by room.** Paid hours x wage and on-costs / child-hours attended; ranked rooms from dearest to cheapest.
5. **Part 3: relief hire test.** Short hours a week x relief wage versus cost of turning away the waitlist; verdict "hire relief", "use part-timers" or "hold".
6. **Part 3: turnover and replacement cost.** Leavers over average headcount (annual turnover %), compared with own history, with the cost of a replacement (hiring time, training, relief) from owner inputs; verdict on whether a raise pays back.
7. **Part 3: renewals and training register by staff.** Hours done / hours still needed this year and "renewal due" dates in 30/45/60-day bands (red/amber), shown as status only, with a cost per person.
8. **Part 1: waitlist by room and start date.** Each family's wanted days and start date matched to the room and its free places by month; shows "can start", "start on date X", "no place before Y"; rank by waiting time and by value of the days wanted.
9. **Part 1: place-filling decision tool.** For each empty place, which waitlisted family fills it best: weekly revenue of the days wanted, room fit, start date; ranked list with a verdict.
10. **Part 1: tour funnel.** Enquiry -> tour booked -> tour held -> application -> enrolled -> started, with conversion at each step, hours to first reply, tours that did not show; compare with own history (benchmark not available, so no invented target).
11. **Part 1: lost-enquiry causes and referral source value.** Cause split (no place, price, hours, distance, slow reply) and value per enquiry and per enrolled child by source (word of mouth, sign, directory, other settings), ranked by revenue not by counts.
12. **Part 1: marketing keep-or-drop.** Cost of each listing or ad per enrolled child and months to pay back; decision "keep / renegotiate / drop".
13. **Part 2: occupancy by room and day.** Booked places / licensed places per room per weekday; also "paid but absent" share; ranked room-days with empty places and the weekly revenue left unfilled.
14. **Part 2: part-time schedule puzzle.** Which part-time requests (days and hours) fit a room without breaking ratio or capacity; verdict "accept", "accept with a day swap", "decline" with the swap suggestion.
15. **Part 2: aging-up planner.** Children turning the next room's age within the next 6 months, with places needed in each room and the month a room runs out; verdict "open a place / hold / move on date".
16. **Part 2: booked hours vs attended hours.** Per child and per week; flag drift above an editable tolerance and the money gap if billing is by booked hours versus attended hours.
17. **Part 2: attendance sign-in/out log with computed hours and late minutes** past closing, passing to the late fee in Part 4.
18. **Part 2: before/after school and holiday camp fill.** Places, sign-ups, days, break-even sign-ups and price per day; verdict "run / run with minimum / cancel" for each week of summer camp.
19. **Part 4: rate card and invoice builder.** Weekly, monthly and hourly rates by room and schedule; invoice lines for tuition, registration, supply fee, sibling discount, drop-in days, late fees, with a check that invoice = booked days x rate - discounts.
20. **Part 4: sibling and multi-day discount tester.** Discount rule options (percent off second child, flat, none): revenue given away per month and per year; verdict showing the cheapest rule that still keeps the family.
21. **Part 4: fee-rise planner.** Current fee, proposed rise, notice date, expected leavers (owner input) and net revenue after leavers; compare with the rise needed to hold the staff cost share target; verdict "rise now / rise at date X / rise less".
22. **Part 4: late-pickup fee tool.** Grace minutes, rate or blocks, number of late events per family by month, staff overtime cost versus fees collected; ranked repeat-late families (status only); verdict whether the fee covers the cost.
23. **Part 4: deposit and registration ledger.** Deposits held, applied to last month or refunded by date and notice rule; total deposits held as a liability not income; check at month end.
24. **Part 4: yearly payment statement for parents.** One page per family totalling amounts paid by period, written in generic words so parents can claim costs; counts check against the ledger.
25. **Part 5: ageing of balances.** Buckets 0-30/31-60/61-90/90+, by family, with a promised-date column; days outstanding average and the 90+ tail as its own KPI; verdict "reminder / call / payment plan / pause care".
26. **Part 5: card-on-file and autopay effect.** Share of tuition on autopay versus manual, late-payment balance, processing cost at the owner's percentage; verdict whether autopay saves chasing time net of the fee, and whether to pass the fee on.
27. **Part 5: third-party / government payment reconciliation.** Expected payment per family versus received, gap by month, and the family share still owed; ranked unreconciled items.
28. **Part 5: 13-week cash flow forecast.** Opening cash, tuition by week from bookings, payroll, rent, food, other bills, known dates (fee rise, holidays, camp income); lowest week and the month the account is short; scenario test "if three families leave".
29. **Part 5: payment plan builder.** Balance, instalments and dates; effect on the 13-week forecast and on the 90+ bucket.
30. **Part 6: meal and menu planner with cost.** Menu by day, meal counts from attendance, shopping list quantities, cost per child per day compared with own history (no outside benchmark available).
31. **Part 6: meal-program claim sheet (generic words).** Meals served by type and age group by day, eligible counts, monthly total and a check that meals served <= children present; flags days that fail.
32. **Part 6: allergy and medical-permission status board.** Status only (on file / due / missing), by room, with dates due; today's list by room for the cook; no health detail stored.
33. **Part 6: incident log with follow-up status.** Date, room, type (fall, bite, other), parent informed yes/no, follow-up done; monthly count by room and by hour of day to find pattern times; no medical wording.
34. **Part 6: emergency drill and cleaning/safety schedule.** Drill types due by month, last done, overdue list; daily and weekly checklist completion rate by room.
35. **Part 7: daily report and parent contact tracker.** Daily reports sent by room and by staff, share sent the same day; parents' messages awaiting reply; communication gaps by family (status only).
36. **Part 7: newsletter, event and conference planner.** Calendar of events and conferences, slots booked by family, no-shows, items due.
37. **Part 7: observation / portfolio status by child.** Count of observations or portfolio entries this term per child and per staff member, flagged children with none; status and dates only, no developmental content.
38. **Part 8: break-even and occupancy scenario.** Fixed costs, staff cost, variable costs; break-even occupancy and places filled; sliders for "enrol two more infants", "lose a family", "open a part-time day"; verdict with the month the result turns.
39. **Part 8: cost per child-hour and fee check.** Total cost / child-hours attended by room and for the whole setting; "is my fee right" against staff cost share target (editable; the 65-75% and 70% figures are weak), margin per room, loss-making room finder.
40. **Part 8: home-daycare mode and owner pay.** Business share of the house (rooms or hours, generic words) turned into cost lines, one-room mixed-age capacity, owner draw and tax set-aside (confirm with your adviser); the year-in-review compared with own history.

**Suggested 8 part titles (no overlap):**
1. Enquiries, Tours and Waitlist
2. Rooms, Places and Attendance
3. Staff, Rota and Ratios
4. Tuition, Fees and Invoices
5. Payments, Balances and Cash
6. Meals, Health and Safety Records
7. Families, Programs and Camps
8. Business Health and Planning
Boundary rules: Part 1 owns everything before the first day of care (including marketing sources); Part 2 owns places, rooms, schedules and hours; Part 3 owns everything about staff (rota, ratio check, training renewals, turnover); Part 4 creates charges and invoices; Part 5 receives and chases money and forecasts cash; Part 6 owns meals, allergy status, incidents, drills and cleaning; Part 7 owns parent communication, observations status, conferences, newsletters and the camp and after-school programs (fill and break-even); Part 8 only reads other parts' totals (cost, occupancy, margin) and never re-enters data.

## Product wording rules
1. No brand, software, platform, bank, marketplace or company names anywhere (including "Excel" lookalikes such as brand-named forms; "Excel and Google Sheets" may be used only if the listing rules allow it, otherwise say "spreadsheet").
2. No law, regulation, agency, licence, accreditation, program or standard names. Say "your local licensing rules", "a government childcare payment", "a meal-program claim", "your local ratio rules".
3. No medical, developmental-diagnosis or safety-guarantee claims. Allergy and medical records are "status and dates only" (on file / due / missing), never content. Incident log has date, room, type and follow-up status only. Observations are counts and dates only.
4. Children's data is kept as first name or code, room, dates and status only; no birth dates beyond month of birth if needed for aging-up; no photos, addresses or diagnoses in the demo.
5. Gender-neutral wording (parent, guardian, family, educator, staff member, they).
6. Ratios, fees, occupancy and food cost numbers are editable "set your own" defaults, with "check your local rules"; never present weak benchmarks as standards.
7. Tax, subsidy and payment-statement cells carry "confirm with your adviser" and no tax-form names.
8. Do not promise outcomes ("fully compliant", "guaranteed safe", "inspection-ready"); say "helps you keep track".
9. Accept regional words as labels (nursery, childminder, educator, setting, early years, crèche) in help text; use one main word per concept in headings.
