# STUDY — Author and self-publisher business (independent authors, pen names, small presses; market and workflow)

Method: 25 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-15, on top of the earlier evidence in research6 (Research 13-15) and research7_strict (group A, author row). Evidence is thin in places and says so. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Anything not read on a page is marked NOT verified.

Earlier evidence, not repeated in detail: the only author listing with item reviews found before was a launch/promotion tracker at 7.99 USD (6 item reviews, writer shop about 1.1k sales, https://www.etsy.com/listing/1706174820). The strict bar (100 reviews or 1,000 sales per listing) was NOT met by any author listing; this study therefore rests on owner jobs, tool prices and author-written pain points, not on proven spreadsheet sales.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (a few minutes each):** check store dashboards for sales, page reads and ad spend of the day; pause or raise ad bids; answer reader and reviewer emails; log a contractor message or approval; record an event or direct sale.

**Weekly jobs:** enter or import last week's sales by store, format and country; ad review per campaign (spend, clicks, sales, cost per sale, break-even check); newsletter send and list growth count; reviewer/ARC follow-up; production milestone check per title (editing, cover, formatting, narration, translation) and next payment due; release calendar and pre-order date check; price promotion start/end and result; hand-sold stock count after any event; pay the virtual assistant per job.

**Monthly jobs:** close the month per store (reports arrive late, figures are estimates until paid); convert foreign currency, match payments received to royalties earned; royalties by title, format, store, country, pen name; page-read income line; direct store sales and bundle margin; print-on-demand margin and author-copy cost; ad profit and loss by campaign and title; newsletter open, click, unsubscribe and cost per subscriber; series read-through (book 1 to last book); contractor payments and invoices matched to job quotes; expenses by category; cash balance and 3-month forecast; tax set-aside transfer; owner draw; pen-name profit and loss; for a small press or collective: author royalty statements and payments.

**Yearly / per-project jobs:** title catalogue review (keep, reprice, retire, repackage); yearly check "does each title earn back its investment?" (one working author uses a 10% yearly return as a test, https://selfpublishingadvice.org/tracking-book-sales/); series plan and next-year release calendar; box-set and translation business cases; rights deals and licences sold (generic: date, territory, language, term, fee, payment dates); identifier and edition register clean-up; tax year pack for the accountant; contractor contract renewals; budget and cash plan; event season plan.

**Per-title job list (what the register must hold as plain fields):** title, series and number, pen name, formats and editions (ebook, paperback, hardcover, large print, audio), language, identifier per edition (plain text field, no scheme name), release date, pre-order date, list price per format and store, page count, word count, production cost items, break-even copies per format.

**Pain points reported (sources; authors' own words where available):**
- No single place shows all sales in all channels: one author-business article says no current tool offers a single platform for tracking all sales in all channels; retailer and distributor reports miss book-fair, consignment and direct web sales; authors must match distributor to retailer data by title, format, price, language, country and retailer; several commenters could not build the spreadsheet themselves and asked the writer for her file. https://selfpublishingadvice.org/tracking-book-sales/ (a working author with 25 titles keeps about 30 rows per month, feeds pivots, and treats figures as estimates until royalties are paid and currency conversion is final).
- Reporting and payment trouble reported by authors on a public complaints round-up: a paperback sale known to be made does not appear in the report; a "fixed" period did not change the numbers; an author in Australia reports a fixed 25 AU fee taken from each royalty wire since 2019 and two months of support without a result; an author in Ireland reports the payment method chosen by the store cut earnings by about 90 pence per book; some authors say accounts closed and royalties went unpaid. https://writersweekly.com/angela-desk/more-amazon-kdp-complaints-poor-quality-royalties-sales-customer-service (collected complaints, one-sided; treat as pain evidence, not as rates).
- Payments arrive months after sales: an aggregator's example has January sales reported finally in early March and paid in early April; some stores report data only by the 20th of the next month. https://publishdrive.com/understanding-your-book-royalty-payments-key-dates-and-insights.html
- Page reads and ad numbers are estimates: one source gives a page-read rate of about 0.0042 (UK pounds) per page with no source of its own. https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/acos-amazon-ads-books/ (weak).
- Ads: the break-even point differs by format because the royalty after print cost is a small share of price; ad targets need to be set per title and format, and a conversion below about 2-3% means a product-page problem rather than an ad problem. Same Vappingo page.
- Audio royalty rules are changing in 2026 (legacy per-sale share replaced by a pooled share, per one guide), so the author cannot trust old per-sale models. https://scribecount.com/author-resource/audiobook-creation-guide/human-narration-for-authors (one vendor's guide, no external source).
- Hand-sold stock is real but small: one author's book-fair day (10 hours, 18 copies, gas 18.72 USD) left about 9 USD profit, and his conclusion was that the value was fans and word of mouth, not money. https://www.brianmcclellan.com/blog/the-economics-of-a-book-fair-from-an-authors-perspective (a traditionally published author's royalties, so NOT a self-publisher margin; shows the need to count time and travel).
- NOT verified: author forum threads on spreadsheets (searches returned articles and tool pages, not readable forum posts); reviews of the tools below (the one Capterra page opened had zero reviews).

**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one price, no tier that rises when income rises; costs per title and break-even (software tracks income, not the production budget); contractor jobs and payments; one view for every format with print margin after print cost; own-history compare; hand-sold stock and events; rights deals; pen-name profit and loss; verdict cells (keep, reprice, stop ads, write next book) instead of charts only; the owner keeps the data; no store login or import needed (enter or paste monthly totals in a clean layout).

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Each item is a real job with its calculation. Keep every item generic: no brand, platform, tax-form or agency names.)

1. **Title cost and break-even per edition.** Cost per title = editing + cover + formatting + narration + translation + identifier + other; break-even copies = cost / royalty per copy by format; months to pay back from actual sales. Verdict: "paid back / on track / behind".
2. **Production budget vs actual per title.** Quote, paid, balance, over/under budget by item; ranked list of titles most over budget.
3. **Contractor job ledger.** Per job: contractor, job, quote, deposit, milestones, due date, paid, balance; total paid per contractor per year and per title; alert for jobs overdue or unpaid; cost per finished hour for audio, cost per 1,000 words for editing.
4. **Production go/no-go for a new edition (audio, large print, hardcover, translation).** Inputs: cost, expected copies per month from the existing edition, royalty per copy; output: payback months and verdict. Add a scenario table (low, mid, high sales).
5. **Series planner with money case.** Planned books, cost each, release gaps, expected read-through per step (owner-entered), forecast series income, and "books to write to reach target income".
6. **Read-through by series step.** Copies of book n and book n+1 (same period or first-year), read-through percent per step, cumulative from book 1; flags under owner thresholds (default 25% warning on step 1, owner can set 60-70% as the healthy line); "where readers drop" ranked list.
7. **Value of a reader.** Revenue per book-1 buyer over the series (royalty x read-through chain); use as the break-even for ad spend on book 1; verdict: "ads justified up to X per sale".
8. **Break-even ad cost by format and store.** Break-even ad share = royalty after print or delivery cost / list price; show per title, format and store; target share as a percent of break-even (editable, default 30-40%).
9. **Campaign ledger and ranking.** Per campaign: spend, clicks, sales, page reads, royalties, cost per sale, ad share vs break-even, profit; rank best/worst; "stop / hold / raise" verdict.
10. **Ad compare with own history.** This month vs 3-month average vs same month last year by title and store; flag deterioration over 20% (editable).
11. **Click-to-sale conversion check.** Clicks, sales, conversion percent per title; under the owner's floor (default 2-3%) = verdict "fix the product page, not the ad".
12. **Price promotion result tool.** Before/during/after sales per day, price, promo cost; extra units and extra royalty; payback days; verdict "repeat / change / skip"; also page reads moved.
13. **Price-test comparison.** Two prices over equal periods: units, royalty per unit, total; break-even unit lift needed for a price change.
14. **Royalty ledger by store, format, country, currency.** Monthly entry grid with currency and rate; total in home currency; royalty per copy by edition.
15. **Earned vs paid reconciliation.** Month earned, expected pay month (owner-entered delay per store), received, difference; ranked list of stores with oldest unpaid month; alert for any month overdue by the owner's own delay.
16. **Currency and fee check.** Fee per payment, exchange loss, effective royalty after fees by store; flags payments where fees exceed an owner-set percent.
17. **Page-read income line.** Pages read, rate per page (editable monthly), income, share of total; sensitivity at -20% / +20% rate; labelled "estimate".
18. **Income by format and edition mix.** Share of royalties by ebook, paperback, hardcover, large print, audio; trend; verdict: "which format to push".
19. **Store dependence check.** Share of income by store; flag above an owner limit (default 70%); scenario "lose the largest store: income and cash months left".
20. **Pen-name profit and loss.** Revenue, direct costs, shared costs split by rule (equal / by revenue / by titles), profit and margin by pen name; ranked.
21. **Catalogue register (plain-field identifiers).** Per edition: identifier field, price by store, page count, release date, status; duplicates and missing fields check; counts by status.
22. **Release calendar with conflicts.** Releases, pre-order dates, promo dates and contractor deadlines on one timeline; flags two releases within the owner's minimum gap and tasks due before a release.
23. **Launch checklist with day counts.** Tasks by days before/after release (reviewers, newsletter, ads, price, pre-order); percent done; alert for overdue; "ready to launch" verdict.
24. **Reviewer/ARC list.** Reviewer, copy sent, date, due, review posted yes/no; response rate; reviews per 100 copies sent; ranked best reviewers.
25. **Launch result and launch share.** First 7/30 days sales vs 12-month sales for the title; own launch share per title (no outside benchmark is trusted); compare with last launch; verdict "launch worked / weaker than my average".
26. **Newsletter growth and cost per subscriber.** New, lost, net, list size by month; source (swap, magnet, ad, event); cost per subscriber by source; ranked sources; verdict "keep / stop".
27. **Swap and promo ledger.** Partner list, date, subscribers gained, clicks, cost; payback; "swap again?" verdict.
28. **Newsletter-to-sales link.** Sends, opens, clicks, sales in the following 7 days (owner-entered) per send; royalties per 1,000 subscribers; compare to own history.
29. **Box set / bundle case.** Component prices and royalties, bundle price, expected cannibalised sales, extra income, break-even bundle sales; verdict.
30. **Direct store margin.** Price - payment fees - tax collected - delivery/download cost = net by item; compare with the same item through a store; share of direct sales in income; goal tracker for the owner's direct-share target.
31. **Print-on-demand and author-copy margin.** Print cost by page count, royalty per copy, author copy cost, shipping; margin per copy for each price; minimum price to keep a target margin.
32. **Hand-sold stock ledger.** Stock in, per-copy cost, copies taken to an event, sold, returned, left, damaged; value in stock; reorder point; verdict "reprint now / wait".
33. **Event profit.** Table fee, travel, nights, hours, copies sold at price, cost of goods, new subscribers; profit and profit per hour; own-history rank of events; "go again" verdict; scenario with 20% fewer sales.
34. **Rights deal ledger.** Deal (generic: translation, licence, other), territory, language, term, fee, instalment dates, received; payments due in the next 90 days; deals expiring; effective income per deal.
35. **Translation business case.** Translation cost, edit cost, cover cost, price, royalty, expected monthly copies (owner-entered), payback months; compare translate vs write next book per 1,000 spent.
36. **Cash flow and 12-month forecast.** Opening cash, expected royalties by pay-month (from the delay table), known contractor payments, ad budget, tax set-aside; low-cash month alert; scenario sales -20%.
37. **Tax set-aside and expense log.** Expenses by category; set-aside percent of profit (editable, "confirm with your tax adviser"); pot balance; shortfall alert; year-end pack as a plain summary.
38. **Owner health check.** One tool that scores: margin, ad profit, cash cover months, store dependence, series read-through, titles under break-even; each with the owner's own target and a green/amber/red verdict; list of the three actions of the month.
39. **Small press / collective statements.** Authors, shares, advances, costs charged back, royalties earned, advance remaining, payable this period; statement per author in plain words; check that the shares sum to 100% and to the money received.
40. **Catalogue decisions: keep, reprice, repackage, retire.** Per title: age, last-12-month royalty, ad cost, production cost left to recover, trend vs own history, read-through role; ranked list with a verdict and one reason; plus a yearly "return on investment" test per title (default 10% as the owner's setting).

## Product wording rules

- No brand, software, platform, store, marketplace, distributor, bank or company names. Say "an online store", "an audiobook store", "a print-on-demand service", "an aggregator / distributor service" only in generic form ("a distribution service"), "your direct store", "your newsletter service", "a bank transfer".
- No law, tax-form, agency, licence, program or standard names (including identifier-scheme names and tax-form names). Say "identifier", "your local tax rules", "your tax adviser", "your local agency".
- No income or bestseller promises; no "make money" claims; every benchmark is labelled "your own number" or "a typical range, check current terms".
- No copyrighted titles, series names or real author names; demo titles are invented and generic; demo pen names invented.
- Gender-neutral wording: "the author", "the owner", "they".
- Say "page reads" (generic) for the page-read income line; "royalties" for author income; UK/EU/AU labels accepted: "turnover", "VAT" only as "sales tax" help text.
- Every royalty rate, price range, delay and tax percent in the product is an editable demo value with the note "check each store's current terms" or "confirm with your adviser".
