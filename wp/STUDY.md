# STUDY — wedding planning suite for couples (8 Excel workbooks, English edition, 29 USD, one-off)

Method: 25 web calls (8 searches and 17 page fetches) on 2026-11-16. Search results returned links only, so every number below was read from a fetched page unless marked NOT verified. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product.
Note on the brief: the section "Research 27" named in the prompt does not exist in /home/claude/work/research6_20261115.md (the file ends at Research 26). The only wedding evidence already in that file (Research 22) is re-used and marked "earlier": wedding planner spreadsheet listing 10,830 sales, wedding day binder 19,577 sales, generic budget planner 42,398 sales. Not repeated otherwise.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points, by month before the day

**12+ months (set the frame):** agree the total budget and who pays what (partner 1, partner 2, each family, savings per month); agree the three things that matter most; first guest-list draft by household and side (rough count decides venue size); pick season, day of week and date; shortlist venues; decide on a planner or day-of coordinator; open one shared place for contracts, quotes and the contact sheet; decide if the wedding is local, destination, small plus a party, or multi-event.
**9 months:** book venue and the two or three vendors that sell out first (venue, caterer, photographer, music); sign contracts and pay deposits; record what each contract includes, the cancellation terms and the final-headcount and final-payment dates; save-the-dates (earlier for destination or travel-heavy weddings); book room blocks and guest travel info; start the wedding party list and attire search; start the registry; engagement party or other early events.
**6 months:** décor and DIY plan with supplies list; invitations designed and ordered; attire ordered (dress, suits, wedding party) with fitting dates and alteration time; officiant and ceremony outline; hair, makeup and cake trials; honeymoon budget and booking; check the local marriage paperwork lead time; A/B list second wave planning; shower, bachelor and bachelorette plans owned by others but budgeted by the couple.
**3 months:** invitations out (the most quoted advice is about eight weeks before for a local wedding, longer for travel); RSVP tracking starts with a reply-by date; meal choices and short dietary notes collected; seating plan draft; run-of-show draft; vendor second payments; menu and tasting; favors and signage; rehearsal dinner, farewell brunch and other events booked; wedding party tasks assigned.
**1 month:** chase non-responders (by phone or message, by household); final headcount forecast and the figure given to caterer and venue by the contract date; table count and seating plan; final balances scheduled; tip envelopes planned; day-of contact sheet; fittings final; pack lists; speeches and music list; marriage paperwork done; confirm every vendor's arrival time and set-up needs.
**Final week:** confirm headcount and delivery times, hand over seating chart and place cards, pay remaining balances, prepare tip envelopes and who hands them out, assign day-of helpers (who carries what, who greets, who returns rentals), rehearsal, emergency kit, weather plan.
**The day:** run-of-show by minute; who needs to be where and when (wedding party, parents, vendors, officiant); vendor arrivals and meals for vendors; hand over tips; collect cards and gifts and give them to one named person; end-of-night load-out and returns.
**After:** thank-you notes (by household, tracked against the gift log), final vendor balances and any refund of a deposit, return rentals, honeymoon, name change and other paperwork in generic words, final budget vs actual, what the wedding really cost per guest.

**Recurring jobs through all months:** budget vs actual by category and vendor, payment due dates and cash needed this month, guest list changes (household, side, A/B tier, plus-ones, children), vendor follow-ups and quotes, task owners and overdue items.

**Pain points with sources:**
1. Overspend is the norm. One large wedding publisher states 69% of couples exceed their initial budget, with average hidden costs of 3,314 (about 9% of total spend) and unexpected fees for 75% of couples (postage 29%, alterations 29%, trials 21%, service charges 17%); 57% face a mandatory venue service fee; service charges and gratuities often 18-22% of vendor costs; delivery and set-up fees 200-500 per vendor; taxes and insurance add 9-15%. https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/whats-the-average-cost-of-a-wedding (a registry company's own marketing page; the page contradicts itself on the venue share, so treat the figures as indicative, not audited).
2. The leading planning publisher's own advice is to hold 5% of the budget as a "just in case" fund and warns that a tight timeline brings rush fees; it gives no overspend statistic. https://www.theknot.com/content/average-wedding-cost
3. Headcount is guessed. Couples' own words in a long forum thread: "We invited 310, 14 declined, 4 no showed"; "We have a huge decline rate. Like 60%" (elderly relatives seven hours away); "We invited 140, had 81 RSVP yes and around 65-70 actually show"; "We invited 248 and had about 140 RSVP yes ... over 30 no shows day of" (family conflict); "We invited 125. 109 RSVP'd as attending ... expected 101 to show, but only 62 came"; "We invited 103 and paid for a 100-person minimum ... 94 attended". Several said to plan for 100% attendance. https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-forums/how-many-guests-didnt-show-to-your-wedding/65c1e9c78632494b.html (forum anecdotes, no sampling; they show the spread, not a rate).
4. RSVP data is unreliable inside apps: a 2026 app comparison reports "RSVP responses sometimes do not appear" (affects catering headcount) and budget-tool line items resetting or duplicating after an update. https://unstar.app/blog/zola-the-knot-joy-weddingwire-minted-wedding-planning-apps-ranked-2026 (secondary blog, not primary reviews).
5. Vendor spam after one enquiry: the same comparison says one DJ enquiry led to contact from 15+ DJs within 48 hours and the form "effectively distributes contact info across the vendor network"; promotional emails reportedly continued after account deletion (secondary blog, single anecdote).
6. Trust in the big marketplaces is low: the vendor-side review page for one marketplace shows a 1.3 out of 5 TrustScore from 2,860 reviews, but the sampled page (20 reviews) is vendors' complaints (12-month contracts, price rise from 1500 to 3500 a year, fake leads), not couples'. https://www.trustpilot.com/review/weddingwire.com?page=5 . A magazine investigation reports 20+ vendors who believe they received fake leads and 200+ regulator complaints since 2018; the company denies it. https://petapixel.com/2025/04/17/the-knot-accused-of-selling-fake-leads-to-wedding-photographers . The article does not claim couples' data is sold; it is about lead quality.
7. Gift money loses a slice: registry cash gifts lose 2.5-2.9% to card fees; example math 125-750 on 5,000-30,000 of gifts; fee shown in settings while marketing says "free". (unstar link above.)
8. Budget tools in free planning apps are thin: one comparison says budget tracking is "minimal, so couples often pair it with a spreadsheet" (unstar link). This is the clearest demand signal for a spreadsheet suite.
9. Cash timing is invisible: couples pay partly from savings (47%), credit cards (31%) and cash gifts (25%) (Zola link); no source seen shows a cash-needed-by-month view in a free app (NOT verified for every app).
10. Big-family and cultural weddings: couples invite more than they can host; in one national report couples wanted to invite an average of 116 but capped at 88. https://www.sbs.com.au/news/the-feed/article/mid-week-vows-and-micro-guest-lists-aussie-weddings-are-changing-but-has-the-cost/nx0un3k98
11. NOT verified: couples' own words on Reddit and other forums about spreadsheets (search returned only articles and spreadsheet-tool blogs; no readable thread). NOT verified: chasing RSVPs, seating-plan conflicts and thank-you-note backlog as sourced complaints (known jobs, no source read).

## 6. Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first within each part. Part numbers refer to the 8 part titles listed at the end. Keep every item generic: no brand, code, law or licence names.)

**Part 1 - Budget, Payments and Cash Plan**
1. **Funding plan: who pays what.** Table of contributors (partner 1, partner 2, each family, savings per month, expected cash gifts as a separate, unconfirmed line). Calculation: confirmed funding vs total budget, shortfall or surplus, verdict "funded / short by X"; only 59% partly and 29% fully self-fund, so family contributions must be explicit and dated.
2. **Priority ranker.** Each partner scores 10 areas 1-5 (food, music, photos, venue, attire, flowers and décor, guests, travel, paper, extras). The tool averages, flags where partners disagree by 2 or more points, and ends with a ranked list that drives item 3.
3. **Budget split suggester.** From total, guest count and ranked priorities, show a suggested split per category using editable default bands (start from the 51/19/10/7/7/6 pattern as an example, labelled "typical, edit"), plus a "per-guest vs fixed" tag per category.
4. **Budget vs actual with three numbers per line.** Per category and vendor: planned, committed (contracted), paid, still due, and variance. Verdict per category: on track, watch, over. Roll up to the whole wedding.
5. **Hidden-cost reserve and true-budget view.** Reserve line (default 5%, editable) and a checklist of the usual surprises (postage, alterations, trials, service charge, delivery and set-up, taxes, insurance, tips, overtime) each with an expected amount; output "true budget" = plan + expected hidden costs, with the share of total.
6. **Payment schedule.** Every deposit, instalment and final balance with due date, amount, method note, paid yes/no; a "due in the next 30 days" list and overdue flags; totals by vendor tie to item 4.
7. **Cash needed by month.** Month grid of payments due against money available (savings added monthly plus family transfers); the first month cash goes negative is flagged, with the amount to move or the payment to renegotiate.
8. **Scenario: cut guests.** Enter "remove N guests"; per-guest items (catering, drinks, stationery, favors, rentals) scale, minimum-guarantee clauses and table rounding are respected, fixed items do not; result: saving, new cost per guest, and a verdict. Shows the UK pattern that per-guest cost can rise as the list shrinks.
9. **Scenario: move the date or season.** Compare two or three dates (weekday vs weekend, peak vs off-peak) by venue and vendor quotes entered per option; output total, difference and a verdict; recalculates the countdown and which vendor deadlines get tighter. Warn when the date leaves less than the typical lead time.
10. **Budget rebalancing.** When a category is over, rank other categories by a "flexibility" score (from item 2 priorities and how much is already committed) and suggest where to take the overage, with the cost to each; ends with a balanced budget or "still over by X".
11. **Cost per guest verdict.** Total (excluding items per guest cannot affect) divided by final headcount, split into fixed and per-guest cost, compared with the couple's own target and with an editable local benchmark; shows what each extra or fewer guest changes.
12. **Tips and gratuities planner.** List of vendors with "gratuity already included?" yes/no, suggested percent or flat amount (editable, optional in markets where tipping is rare), labelled envelopes, who hands them over, cash needed; total added to the true budget.
13. **Settle-up between payers.** Who actually paid each bill (partner 1, partner 2, parent), what each agreed to pay, and a final "who owes whom" at the end; avoids the common family dispute about contributions.

**Part 2 - Guests, Invitations and RSVPs**
14. **Household guest list with side balance.** One row per household (people, side, tier, contact status), counts by side and tier, the balance between sides against an agreed split, and the draft total vs venue capacity as a traffic light. Short dietary note only; no health details.
15. **A/B list cascade.** B list ordered by rank; when A-list declines arrive, the tool shows how many seats are open and which B households to invite next, with the last date an invitation can still be sent given the reply-by date and the lead time chosen.
16. **Final headcount forecast.** From RSVPs: confirmed yes + pending households times an expected-yes rate set by tier and distance (defaults editable; starting point 80% with more declines for travel guests) + undecided plus-ones; output low, likely and high, the catering-guarantee date countdown, and the cost at each figure. Verdict: "give the caterer X".
17. **RSVP chase list.** Ranked list of households still silent: tier, days since invite, contact status, deadline countdown, last chase date and channel; flags the households that change the forecast most.
18. **Meal and dietary summary.** Counts of each meal choice, children's meals, vendors' meals and short dietary notes (free text, kept short), reconciled to headcount; mismatch flag ("meals ordered 118, headcount 121"); printable per table.
19. **Plus-one and children policy test.** Choose policy (named plus-ones only, all couples, adults only, children welcome); the tool recalculates headcount, cost and capacity and shows the difference between policies; ends with the cheapest and most inclusive option.
20. **Invitation timeline and paper cost.** Dates for save-the-dates, invitations, reply-by and reminders from the wedding date (earlier lead for travel weddings); quantity by household (not by guest), postage by region (domestic/abroad), spare count; paper total feeds Part 1.
21. **Guest travel and room blocks.** Rooms held, picked up, cutoff date and attrition clause date; arrivals per day; guests who need a ride or help; a "rooms still to fill by cutoff" count and the cost exposure if the block is not filled.

**Part 3 - Vendors, Quotes and Contracts**
22. **Vendor pipeline.** Per category: contacted, quote received, shortlisted, booked, declined; follow-up date, response time; one view of which categories are still open with the time left before the typical booking lead time (editable).
23. **True-cost vendor comparison.** Up to four quotes per category side by side with base price, what is included and excluded, overtime, travel, service charge, taxes, delivery, extras, expected tip; output "true total" and a ranked list with a weighted score (price plus the couple's own ratings for fit, response, reviews); verdict "best value", "lowest price", "best fit".
24. **Contract deadline table and contact sheet.** Per vendor: what is included, cancellation and refund terms, final-headcount date, final-payment date, arrival and set-up time, insurance or permit needs; all dates feed the countdown dashboard (item 27); contact sheet prints for the day with phone, role and meeting point.

**Part 4 - Master Timeline, Checklist and Task Owners**
25. **Auto-dated master timeline.** Tasks generated from the wedding date by month before (12+, 9, 6, 3, 1, week, day, after) and by wedding type (local, destination, small plus party, multi-event), with a compressed mode when the date is under 12 months away (the buyer plans 4-18 months out): tasks are re-dated and the ones that no longer fit are flagged.
26. **Task owners and workload.** Owner per task (partner 1, partner 2, parent, friend, coordinator); counts by owner, overdue by owner, workload share; verdict "share looks uneven" when one person holds more than a set share.
27. **Deadline countdown dashboard.** Next 10 deadlines from every part (payments, RSVP date, headcount date, fittings, vendor cutoffs) with days left and red/amber/green; plus "this week" and "this month" lists.

**Part 5 - Venue Capacity and Seating Plan**
28. **Capacity and table count.** From headcount, table sizes (editable: 6, 8, 10, 12), head table, vendors seated, dance floor and buffet area entered by the couple; output tables needed, empty seats, and a "fits / too tight" verdict against the venue's stated capacity.
29. **Seating plan with rules.** Guests assigned to tables with keep-together and keep-apart rules; unseated list; table fill; conflicts listed; per-table meal and dietary summary; printable table list and place-card list.

**Part 6 - Wedding Party and Day-Of Run-of-Show**
30. **Wedding party roster.** Roles, tasks, attire status (ordered, arrived, fitted, altered), fitting dates and the alteration lead time, who pays for what; countdown to the last safe order date for each outfit (alterations were a surprise cost for 29% of couples in the cited survey).
31. **Run-of-show with overrun check.** Minute-by-minute schedule with duration per block and buffer; total time compared with the venue hours and vendor overtime costs; verdict "fits / over by N minutes, overtime cost X"; travel time between venues is a required row.
32. **Who needs to be where matrix.** A person-by-time grid derived from the run-of-show, and one printable page per person or group (wedding party, parents, vendors, officiant); a day-of contact list with a named person for gifts, cards, tips and returns.

**Part 7 - Events, Travel and Honeymoon**
33. **Multi-event planner.** Each event (engagement party, shower, bachelor and bachelorette weekends, henna or mehndi night, rehearsal dinner, after-party, farewell brunch; cultural labels offered as optional names) with its own date, invitees (a subset of the guest list), vendors, budget and cost per guest; a combined total and guest-nights; extended-family weddings can hold up to 250 guests and many events.
34. **Destination wedding module.** Travel and stay cost for the couple, wedding-party help, local vendor quotes in a second currency with an editable exchange rate, a lead-time row for "check the local marriage paperwork rules with your officiant or local office", and an expected-decline uplift for the forecast in item 16 (more declines for travel guests).
35. **Small wedding plus a party comparison.** Two-track layout: a small ceremony (courthouse or registry-office style) and a later celebration; compare "one big wedding" vs "small ceremony plus a party" on total cost, cost per guest, guest count and the number of events; verdict with the three biggest differences. Offers rows for the paperwork step only in generic words.

**Part 8 - Attire, Décor, Gifts and After the Wedding**
36. **DIY make-or-buy.** Per project: supplies list with quantity from guest or table count, cost to make, hours needed at an editable value per hour, cost to buy or rent; verdict "make", "buy", or "make half"; total supplies shopping list and the date to start so the work is done two weeks before.
37. **Gift and thank-you tracker.** By household: gift received (date, description or amount), thank-you written, thank-you sent, days since the wedding, a target (editable, for example 8 weeks) and the overdue list; cash gifts logged separately.
38. **Cash gifts and registry to fund link.** Show expected and received cash gifts against the honeymoon fund and the budget gap; mark them "not confirmed" until received and exclude them from the funding plan in item 1 until then; a line for any fee charged by a payment service entered by the couple.
39. **Honeymoon budget and plan.** Days, travel, stay, daily spend, a reserve, funds available (savings plus gifts), two options side by side, and a verdict "affordable / short by X"; also a minimoon option for a tight budget.
40. **After-wedding wrap-up and paperwork.** Final budget vs actual per category and per guest ("what the wedding really cost"), unpaid vendor balances, deposit returns, rental returns, and a checklist of name-change and paperwork steps in generic words with optional rows (the couple edits; no step is stated as required).

**Packaging rules for the shop listing and the files (not numbered):** a "Start here" tab and a "Replace demo data" instruction in every workbook; sample and blank copies; no macros; "works in Excel and Google Sheets" stated honestly (one competitor is Google Sheets only; another offers both); a one-page "Why a workbook" block in generic words (flat price, no vendor sees your data, no gift fees, your own numbers); every tool ends in a plain-words verdict; every default (guest-decline rate, reserve, tipping, lead times, payment timing) is an editable setting labelled "typical, edit"; no promised savings.

## Product wording rules
- No brand, app, website, registry, venue, vendor, payment or company names anywhere in the product (the names in this study are research sources only).
- No law, licence, agency or form names: write "your local marriage paperwork" and "check with your officiant or local office".
- No religious or cultural rule stated as a requirement; offer optional rows and optional event names (couples edit, hide or delete them).
- Gender-neutral wording: partner 1 and partner 2, the couple, the wedding party; roles such as best man or maid of honour appear only as editable labels.
- Guest data is limited to name, household, side, contact status, RSVP, meal choice and a short dietary note; no health details, no ages beyond "adult/child", no addresses beyond what a mailing count needs.
- No promises about savings or results: use "estimate", "typical (edit)", "your own numbers".
- Market benchmarks shown in the product are editable starting values, never presented as facts about the user's area; tips are optional and vary by market.
- Currency and date format are settings; wording works for US, UK, Canada, Australia and EU (accept "hen and stag do", "reception venue", "order of service", "evening guests" as labels).
