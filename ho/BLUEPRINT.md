# Home Management suite — eight parts (all share ho_core.py: the Okafor-Lindqvist household, two adults, two children, a grandparent who visits twice a month, a babysitter, a dog and a cat in an owned three-bedroom house with a small garden; 16 team rows; about 60 recipes, 45 chores, 50 maintenance tasks, 120 inventory items; about 2 years of history and 12 months ahead)

The demo household is "the Okafor-Lindqvist household" (fictional): adult 1 (Sam Okafor, HO-01) works office hours with two late days a week (Tuesday and Thursday); adult 2 (Riley Lindqvist, HO-02) works shifts (Early, Late or Off, the pattern changes every week and decides who cooks, drives and cleans); two children, Child 1 (Jo, about 12, school year 7, HO-03) and Child 2 (Kit, about 6, school year 1, HO-04); a grandparent (Ada Okafor, HO-05) who visits twice a month and helps with school pick-up; a babysitter (Mina Costa, HO-06) on some Friday evenings. Ten outside contacts (HO-07..HO-16) are the plumber, the electrician, the heating engineer, the vet clinic (dates only), the car mechanic, the school office, a carpool family, the neighbour who holds a spare key, the landlord or managing agent (for renters) and the dog walker. They live in an owned three-bedroom house with a small garden, with a family car and an older second car, a dog (Bruno) and a cat (Pip). They plan dinners for the week from about 60 household recipes (prep minutes, servings, a plain "preference / avoid" label, never a health or diet claim), shop once a week plus top-ups, keep a pantry, a fridge and a chest freezer (items with added and use-by dates), share about 45 recurring chores by room and frequency (children earn points, never money), keep a maintenance plan of 50 tasks (intervals are "typical, check the manual"), log repairs and the trades called in generic words, keep a home inventory of 120 items by room (purchase date, price, serial number, warranty end, manual location), a family calendar (school terms, two activities per child with carpools, birthdays, visits, holidays), pets (dates and stock only), vehicles (service, tyres, annual test, insurance and registration renewal dates in generic words), important documents and renewals, household contacts, and four projects (a bathroom refresh, a garden bed, hosting a holiday dinner for 12, a spring declutter with donations). A household of renters uses every part too: where a help text says "a trade", renters read "a maintenance request to the landlord"; a single adult with no children or pets leaves those rows empty and uses most sheets unchanged (every Start here tab says so). The suite is a home kit, never an app, never a safety service and never advice. Money appears only as simple cost lines of a repair, a purchase in the inventory, a gift or a project; household money is another suite.

Market note (STUDY.md section 3): the demand for home-management spreadsheets is real but the evidence is thin; the listings found are single-slice kits (a cleaning log, a chore chart, a maintenance tracker, an inventory sheet) at about 2 to 11 dollars, and the one seven-sheet kit found has almost no sales. The largest risk is demand, not competition: the suite wins on depth (eight linked workbooks of about 20 pages each), on a clear Start here page and on what apps lack (leftovers and freezer tracking, a fairness check without nagging, a "next due" for every task, several shops on one list), not on price. Say so in the listing copy only through real features, never through sales claims.
