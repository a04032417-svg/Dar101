# Household Finance suite — eight parts (all share hf_core.py: the Rivera-Okafor household with partner 1 salaried and paid every two weeks, partner 2 freelance with irregular income, two children and a grandparent who helps with childcare; 11 outside contacts; 14 accounts; about 20 bills and 14 subscriptions; 3 cards, a car loan and a student loan; 10 savings goals and sinking funds; about 2 years of history)

The demo household is "the Rivera-Okafor household" (fictional): partner 1 (Sam Rivera, HF-01) is salaried and paid every two weeks; partner 2 (Riley Okafor, HF-02) is freelance with irregular monthly income from two or three clients (Client A, Client B, sometimes Client C) and a small side income; two children (HF-03, about 11, and HF-04, about 7) and a grandparent (HF-05) who helps with childcare complete the household. Eleven outside contacts (HF-06..HF-16) are the landlord, partner 1's payroll office, two freelance clients, the tax preparer, the insurance agent, the childcare provider, the school office, the car mechanic, the utility company and the bank branch. Money sits in 14 accounts (a joint checking account, each partner's own account, an emergency savings account, two goal savings accounts, three cards, a car loan, a student loan, two retirement accounts and one brokerage account). The household pays rent (a mortgage switch next year is a scenario), utilities, phone and internet, insurance with yearly and monthly premiums, childcare and school costs, and about 14 subscriptions, some unused. Three cards at different rates, a car loan and a student loan carry minimum payments and an extra-payment plan. Ten savings goals and sinking funds (emergency fund, car insurance yearly premium, holidays, gifts, car replacement, home repairs, kids' activities, a family trip, a new laptop, a tax set-aside for the freelance income) are filled by monthly contributions. Partners share costs by an agreed split, keep a tax document checklist and deduction records, plan big purchases (a used car, a move) and review the year. A single adult with one income and no children uses most parts: every Start here tab says so and shows how to leave the partner 2, children and shared-cost sheets empty. Money is one generic currency, shares and rates are fractions, and every verdict is "a typical range, check your own numbers", never advice.

**11.
