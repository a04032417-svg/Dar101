# STUDY — household finance (a single adult, a couple or a family running its own money): market and workflow

Method: 24 web calls that ran (searches and fetches) on 2026-11-16, plus 4 search calls rejected for a bad parameter before running; builds on Research 28 in research6_20261115.md (not repeated: three Etsy listings at $1.95, $10.53 and $5.04 with about 1,000-1,400 item reviews and 7,000-16,000 sales each). Evidence is thin in places and says so. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. This is not a business bookkeeping suite: the buyer runs one household.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Each paycheck (weekly, every two weeks / fortnightly, or monthly):** enter the pay that arrived (take-home, not gross); assign every unit of it (zero-based: income minus all planned outgoings equals zero) or split it by percentage (needs / wants / savings and debt); move the set-aside amounts (bills, sinking funds, emergency fund, goals); check what is left to spend until the next pay; for two earners, split shared costs and show each partner's share; for irregular pay (shift work, freelance, side income) budget from a low "baseline" month and put the excess into a buffer.
**Weekly jobs:** log variable spending (groceries, fuel, eating out, kids); compare spent against the weekly allowance; check bills due in the next 7-14 days; check the account balance against the low point before payday; pay allowances to children; add side-income received.
**Monthly close:** compare planned against actual by category; pay minimums and the chosen extra payment on cards and loans (snowball: smallest balance first; avalanche: highest rate first); update balances and the payoff date; review subscriptions; top up sinking funds; update net worth by account (cash, savings, retirement, home value, car, cards, loans); settle shared costs between partners or housemates; carry forward the over/under spend.
**Quarterly:** review the year-to-date against the annual plan; check estimated-tax or self-employment set-aside if the household has freelance income (generic words, "check your local rules"); review insurance and utility contracts; re-rank debts; check emergency fund in months of expenses.
**Yearly:** annual costs calendar (insurance, car service and registration, holidays, gifts, school costs, home repairs); tax documents checklist (income statements, interest, charity receipts, medical receipts, work-expense records) and deduction records in generic words; year review (income, spending by category, savings rate, debt paid, net worth change); next-year plan; raise or renegotiate fixed bills.
**Life events:** big-purchase planning (car, move, wedding, a new baby, home repair): target, date, monthly amount to save, effect on the monthly budget; moving costs; loss of one income; parental leave; a new child's costs; separation of accounts; a new loan.
**Other jobs the buyer names:** bills calendar with due dates and "paid" status; subscriptions creep (what renews when and the yearly total); minimum-payment tracking; cash flow forecast; kids' costs and allowances; retirement and investment contributions as amounts only; groceries and variable spending envelopes.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
1. **App price and price rises.** The most repeated complaint about the best-known envelope-budget app: $14.99 a month or $99 a year; legacy users reportedly paid about $60 a year before costs "roughly doubled". "YNAB's pricing is the most frequent complaint" (a competitor's blog, not independent): https://pocketclear.app/blog/ynab-complaints-problems.html . Trustpilot (3,141 reviews, 4.6): "Love it but too pricey": https://www.trustpilot.com/review/youneedabudget.com
2. **Bank links break.** Trustpilot reviewer: "I have bad connection with my bank and always have to reautorize"; another: "It became too much for me to trouble shoot". A second app review lists re-authorisation every 30-90 days, store cards, mortgages and some Canadian banks hard to connect, "plan on 10-15 minutes per month for sync upkeep" (a competitor's article): https://envelopebudgeting.com/articles/monarch-money-review
3. **Learning curve and missing guidance.** Reviewer: "I think there's a month end rollover required that nobody showed or explained." Newcomers spend weeks on the method and some give up (pocketclear page above).
4. **Reporting after the fact.** The second app "reports spending after the fact and can't block transactions"; there is "no undo for reallocation mistakes"; setup is tedious and early transactions are miscategorised (envelopebudgeting page above).
5. **Spreadsheets are too hard or too complex.** The free spreadsheets that owners recommend are described as "not for beginners" (net-worth sheet) and "if you don't know how to use them, they won't do you much good" (feature-rich tracker): https://tiller.com/the-top-budget-spreadsheets-recommended-by-reddit/ (a vendor's page; no forum posts were readable).
6. **Subscriptions creep.** A survey of 1,000 consumers (C+R Research, 2022) found people guessed $86 a month but actually spent $219, $133 more: https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/business/money-report/consumers-spend-an-average-133-more-each-month-on-subscriptions-than-they-realize-study-shows/3067565/
7. **Couples and housemates need fair-split tools.** Listings exist for "split bills by income" with a "settle up" tab showing who pays whom in the fewest payments (https://www.etsy.com/listing/4592390904), which shows demand for income-proportional split; no reviews yet (see section 3).
8. NOT verified: owner-written complaints on forums about irregular income and paycheck timing (search returned blog articles, not readable threads). The need is taken from the product pattern (monthly or "paycheck" budget titles that sell in the thousands) and from the structure of the jobs above.

## 6. Gaps and improvements for the blueprint

(Most valuable first. Every item is a real job with the calculation it needs. Keep all wording generic.)

1. **Cash low point before each payday.** Forecast the account balance day by day from opening balance, pay dates and every bill; show the lowest balance and its date before each paycheck, with a verdict ("safe", "tight", "short by X on day Y").
2. **Paycheck split tool.** Enter one paycheck; choose zero-based or percentage method; the tool allocates to bills due before the next pay, sinking funds, debt extra, goals and spending money, and ends "Left to assign: 0".
3. **Any pay rhythm.** Weekly, every two weeks (fortnightly), twice a month and monthly, with the number of pay days in each month (some months have three), and "extra pay month" shown as a windfall to assign.
4. **Irregular-income baseline.** Take the last 6-12 months of income, compute the lowest month and the average, budget the baseline, and park the excess in a buffer; verdict: "months of buffer built" and "this month is above or below baseline".
5. **Bills calendar.** Every bill with due day, amount, paid status, late flag, and "due in the next 14 days" list; total of unpaid bills before the next payday.
6. **Subscriptions audit.** List each subscription with renewal date, monthly and yearly cost; ranked by yearly cost; "keep / review / cancel" column filled by the user; total saved if cancelled (a calculation, not a promise).
7. **Payoff order tool: snowball vs avalanche.** For up to 10 debts: month-by-month schedule, payoff date, total interest, and a verdict comparing both orders (difference in interest and months).
8. **Extra payment test.** "What if I pay X more each month": new payoff date, months saved, interest saved, for the whole debt list.
9. **Minimum payments check.** Total minimums against take-home pay (as a share), and "payoff date if only minimums are paid" for each card, with a warning when the minimum does not cover the monthly interest.
10. **Debt ratios.** Debt payments over gross and over take-home income, shown against typical ranges entered in Settings (default 36% and 20%), labelled as a general range.
11. **Sinking funds with monthly amounts.** Target, date, saved so far, "set aside per paycheck" that adjusts to pay frequency; ranked list by days left; "behind by X" flag.
12. **Annual costs to monthly.** A one-page list of yearly costs (insurance, car, holidays, gifts, school, repairs, subscriptions) converted to a per-month and per-paycheck amount, with the month each falls due.
13. **Emergency fund in months.** Essential monthly spending (from the budget) divided into the emergency balance; target months in Settings (default 3 and 6); months to reach target at the current monthly amount.
14. **Net worth by account.** Assets and liabilities per account type typed monthly; net worth line, change since last month and last year; share of net worth by account group.
15. **Net worth projection.** Monthly contribution and an assumed yearly growth rate typed by the user; a simple projection labelled "an example, not a forecast".
16. **Planned vs actual by category.** Per month, with variance, over-budget list ranked by amount, and a year-to-date column.
17. **Compare with my own history.** This month vs last 3-month average vs same month last year for every category, and for the savings rate.
18. **Savings rate.** Savings plus debt-above-minimum plus retirement contribution over take-home pay, monthly and year to date; compared to the household's own best month.
19. **Groceries and variable spending envelopes.** Weekly allowance, spent so far, days left, "can spend per day"; flag when the pace would overspend at month end.
20. **Fair-split calculator for partners.** Split shared costs equally, in proportion to take-home pay, or by a custom share; shows each partner's amount and a monthly transfer to the joint account.
21. **Settle up.** For housemates or partners paying costs from different accounts: who owes whom and the fewest payments to square up; a running balance carried month to month.
22. **Joint vs separate accounts map.** Which bills come from which account (partner 1, partner 2, joint), the transfer each payday and the personal spending money left to each partner.
23. **Lose one income test.** Set income of partner 1 or partner 2 to zero (or reduced) and show the new monthly gap, which categories to cut to close it, and months of emergency fund that cover the gap.
24. **Expense shock test.** Add a one-off cost (repair, medical, job gap) and show the effect on the low point, the emergency fund and the payoff date.
25. **Interest-rate change test.** Change the rate on a variable loan or mortgage by a typed number of points and show the new monthly payment and yearly extra cost.
26. **Kids' costs and allowances.** Per child: regular costs (childcare, school, activities), allowance amount and day, saved/spent split; yearly total per child and per household member.
27. **New baby plan.** One-off costs, monthly costs, expected income change during leave, and "monthly gap" and "months the savings last"; verdict in plain words.
28. **Wedding or event budget.** Items with estimate, paid, due date, deposit; total vs target; monthly saving needed to the date.
29. **Car purchase decision.** Price, deposit, trade-in, loan term, rate, running costs (fuel, insurance, service); monthly cost, total cost over years, share of take-home pay; verdict against the household's own limit.
30. **Moving plan.** Move date, deposits, moving costs, overlapping rent, new-home setup; total cash needed and "date you have it" from the current saving rate.
31. **Rent or buy comparison, generic.** Monthly cost of each with user-typed numbers; years to break even; labelled "your numbers, not advice".
32. **Tax documents checklist.** List of documents by type (income statements, interest and investment statements, charity receipts, medical receipts, work-expense receipts, childcare receipts) with "received", "filed" and due date, and a countdown; words "your tax office", "check your local rules".
33. **Deduction records log.** Date, description, amount, category (generic), receipt kept yes/no, tax year; totals per category and a list of missing receipts. No claim about what is claimable.
34. **Estimated tax set-aside for side income.** Typed percentage on side or freelance income moved to a set-aside account each time income arrives; running balance; "confirm with your adviser".
35. **Retirement and other contributions.** Amounts only: per paycheck, year to date, employer amount typed, yearly total; share of take-home pay; no product names, no advice.
36. **Insurance and contracts calendar.** Renewal dates, yearly premiums and "review 45 days before" alerts; total yearly insurance as a share of spending.
37. **Year review.** One page: total income, spending by category, savings rate, debt paid and interest paid, net worth change, biggest category changes vs last year, goals reached; ends with three "next year" lines typed by the user.
38. **Next-year plan.** Copies last year's actuals into next year's budget with a typed change per category; shows the monthly and per-paycheck effect.
39. **Start here and month-end checklist in every workbook.** Replace demo data, pay-day routine (what to type each paycheck), month-close routine (steps and order), plus "why a workbook": one flat price, no renewal, your data stays with you, nothing to link or re-connect. Buyers of competing apps complain about price rises and broken links.
40. **Shared Settings and verdict wording.** One Settings area for currency, pay frequency, partner names (partner 1, partner 2), targets (emergency months, debt-to-income range, savings rate, low-balance warning) and every verdict phrased as "typical range, check your own numbers"; same names, colours and checks across all eight workbooks.

**Suggested 8 part titles (no overlap):**
1. Money Plan and Paychecks (income, pay rhythm, irregular baseline, paycheck split, monthly budget)
2. Bills, Subscriptions and Due Dates (bills calendar, subscriptions, contracts and renewals)
3. Spending Tracker and Groceries (log, categories, envelopes, planned vs actual, history)
4. Debt Payoff Planner (cards and loans, snowball vs avalanche, extra payment, ratios)
5. Savings Goals and Sinking Funds (goals, annual costs, emergency fund, retirement contributions)
6. Net Worth and Accounts (accounts, assets, liabilities, projections, joint and separate map)
7. Partners, Kids and Shared Costs (fair split, settle up, allowances, children's costs, scenario tests such as losing one income)
8. Big Purchases, Tax Papers and Year Review (car, move, baby, wedding, documents, deduction records, year review and next-year plan)
Cash flow forecast and the low point live in Part 1, linked to Parts 2 and 4; scenario tests are in Part 7 and read from all others.

## Product wording rules

- No brand, app, bank, card, lender, broker, fund, insurer or company names anywhere in the product.
- No law, tax-form, agency, government programme or account-type brand names; say "your tax office", "a tax-advantaged retirement account", "check your local rules".
- No investment, tax, legal or credit advice; no promise of savings, credit score or results; payoff dates, interest saved and projections are "estimates from your numbers".
- Benchmarks only as "a typical range, check your own numbers", never as advice.
- No bank-link, app or automatic sync claims: the workbook takes typed or pasted data.
- Gender-neutral wording: partner 1 and partner 2, household member, child.
- No health details (medical costs are an amount and a category only).
- Accept regional words as labels (current account, fortnightly, council tax, overpayment, superannuation in generic words) without naming programmes or products.
