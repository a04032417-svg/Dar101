# STUDY — small US/UK/EU therapy / counselling group practice (market and workflow)

Method: about 25 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-15. Evidence is thin in places and says so. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Weekly jobs (owner and coordinator):** check next week's diary against each clinician's cap and fill gaps from the cancellation list; confirm and remind next-day sessions; chase unreplied enquiries and call-backs; screening calls and offers from the waiting list; submit claims for attended sessions within the practice rule; post payments and match remittances; follow up unpaid claims; check card and bank deposits; chase overdue balances; check late notes by clinician; rebooking gap check (clients with no next session); approve the coordinator's hours; weekly cash check; supervision of the associate.
**Monthly jobs:** invoice clients for their share (and late fees); contractor statements checked against contractor invoices; pay run (salary, split, per-session, hourly); claims close and ageing by payer; A/R review (30/60/90+); utilisation and revenue per clinical hour by clinician; profit and loss and cash; tax set-aside transfer; owner draw; privacy and device check; marketing spend and enquiry source review; group-program fill check.
**Yearly jobs:** fee rise and notice; sliding-scale and consent reviews; renewals (registration, insurance, background checks); continuing-education plan; policy review; contractor terms review; budget; records archive and destruction dates; year-end pack for the accountant.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Slow cash flow in group practices: complex billing, missing or expired pre-approvals and session caps, high denials, thin admin staff, fragmented tools, no tracking of metrics, no-shows. https://checkpointehr.com/?p=19407
- Cancellations touch several systems; mental-health benefit checks are manual and happen after booking; no bulk editing; clients want automatic move to self-pay with costs told in advance; buffer time between heavy sessions; scheduling linked to plans and outcome tracking. https://www.upheal.io/blog/scheduling-software-for-therapists-sucks
- Expenses rising faster than revenue (one 2025 survey: average expenses up 6.3%, S-corp up 11.2%); owners worried about income. https://www.joinheard.com/the-heard-2025-financial-state-of-private-practice-report
- Admin overload is the main group-practice complaint (owners doing billing, scheduling, hiring). https://www.gethealthie.com/blog/admin-overload-group-practices , https://ensorahealth.com/blog/7-admin-habits-for-a-thriving-group-practice/
- Owners advised to compare against their own history first, not industry numbers (so the product should trend each KPI month on month). https://ensorahealth.com/blog/therapy-practice-metrics-that-matter/
- NOT verified: forum threads (searches returned articles and review sites, not readable forum posts).

## 2. Leading practice-management software (research only)
Pricing per month seen (CostBench, 2026, https://www.costbench.com/software/mental-health-practice-management/): category median about $64; solo/start plans about $25-69; group plans $79-99 per user; one product $59.90-$99.90; one free tier; many custom quotes. Hidden costs listed: per-clinician pricing that rises with growth; add-ons for telehealth, claim filing, e-prescribing; card processing and clearinghouse fees; set-up and migration.
Features offered: scheduling and reminders, client portal, notes and treatment plans, telehealth, claims and remittance posting, client statements, reports, outcome measures, intake forms.
User complaints (Capterra reviews, https://www.capterra.com/p/130710/SimplePractice/reviews/): frequent price rises with short notice (one cited $99/month); "everything I enjoy is an add-on"; costly as team grows; no student/associate accounts for supervision; non-clinical staff cannot reach forms; clients cannot reschedule or cancel from reminders; reports and payment posting unreliable for groups; templates too generic; family/couples set-up hard.
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one flat 29 dollars, no per-clinician fee; the coordinator and associate get their own views; reports a group owner actually needs (profit by service, by clinician, cost of a room hour, contractor check); losing-service finder; clear alerts instead of 40 report screens; owner keeps the data.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. "Part n" = blueprint part. Keep every item generic: no brand, code, law or licence names.)

1. **Part 8 / Part 5 (all-in clinician cost).** Contractor "60% split" is not the true cost; add an all-in view: pay plus employer cost plus room time plus admin per session, for every pay type, and show practice margin per clinician against a margin target. The Salary or split comparison tool should use all-in cost, not only the pay line.
2. **Part 8 (owner-facing dashboard questions).** Add a "Is the practice healthy?" tool that scores five numbers at once: pay share of revenue (clinician pay 50-60% target), overhead share (15-25% target), profit margin (10-20%), cash cover months (3-6), utilisation (75-85%). Add these as editable targets in Settings, not hard-coded.
3. **Part 4 (self-pay cost estimate).** Add the missing tool and document: "Cost estimate for a self-pay client" (fee, expected number of sessions, late-cancel rule, deposit, package option, sliding share: total expected cost and a written estimate). Owners cite this as a standing duty; the blueprint has a fee quote only in Part 1 and Part 3.
4. **Part 4 (aged A/R).** Replace the single "days to payment" lens by ageing buckets (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+) for payer money; an average hides the old tail. Add "Days in A/R" with a flag over 40 and keep the 90+ bucket as its own KPI and chart.
5. **Part 4 (claim quality).** Add "clean claim rate" (accepted first time) and "claims not accepted first time" as KPI, gauge and tool; the blueprint tracks denials but not first-pass quality. Rename "Denials resolved" gauge to "Denials fixed and paid".
6. **Part 4 (benefit check before the first session).** Add a "Benefits check" table and form (session limit, pre-approval needed, client share, checked on, checked by) and a tool "Will this client's benefits cover it?" with an automatic self-pay fallback fee and cost shown before booking; this is the most repeated scheduling complaint.
7. **Part 4 (credentialing).** Add a small "Payer enrolment" table (payer, application sent, effective date, renewal due, status) using generic words; it is a real gap and a revenue gate for every new clinician. Link it to the hire business case in Part 8 (months before a new clinician can bill a payer).
8. **Part 2 (no-show definition).** Show three separate rates, not one: no-show, late cancel, and any cancellation. The blueprint's "No-show rate last 90 days" should be accompanied by "Missed and late-cancelled share of booked sessions" with a target under 10%. Put the 10% default in Settings.
9. **Part 2 (cap vs target).** Keep the cap check, and add a "target load" band (for example 22-25 sessions a week full-time) with a yellow warning at the top of the band and a red warning above it, since strain is reported beyond 25-30 a week.
10. **Part 5 (utilisation targets).** Add a utilisation band: below 70% idle, 75-85% healthy, above 90% strain. Use the three colours on the Utilisation gauge and in the clinician table; the blueprint has a single target.
11. **Part 5 (contractor checks).** Add "Contractor all-in cost" and "contractor effective split after payment-method fees". Add a contractor "split proposal" tool (new split, sessions, fee: effect on practice margin) so owners can negotiate. Retain the statement vs invoice check.
12. **Part 5 (associate).** Add a "supervision cost loaded onto the associate" line to Associate pay calculator (external supervision hours times rate) and show the associate's net margin per session; many owners forget the supervision fee in the margin.
13. **Part 3 (client payment collection).** Rename "Collection rate" to "Client payment collection rate" and add a "Net collection rate" (money received over money collectable after agreed concessions), with 95% target in Settings. Add a "Collection gap by cause" chart (sliding scale, waived late fee, write-off, unpaid).
14. **Part 3 (ageing).** Add the same ageing buckets and a "Balance over 90 days" KPI to Part 3 and keep the story invoice as the alert; add a "Payment promise" column (promised date) so chasing is logged by outcome.
15. **Part 3 (card / auto-pay).** Add "Cards on file" yes/no and "Share of clients paying by instalment"; owners say card-on-file at booking is the single best fix for late payment and no-show fees. Add a tool "Card on file effect" (late-pay balance, expected share moved: money not chased).
16. **Part 3 (receipt for client to claim back).** The blueprint calls this a "claim-back statement". Rename to "Receipt for claim back" in the headings (the common term is "superbill" in the US and "receipt for your insurer" in the UK), and add a plain mention under the title so searchers find it; keep one generic definition.
17. **Part 1 (free-of-charge screening and the first-contact response time).** Add "Hours to first reply" per enquiry and a KPI "Enquiries replied within 1 working day"; the gap is that most enquiries are won or lost on speed of first reply. Add a column and a gauge.
18. **Part 1 (lost enquiries by cause).** Add a cause split "no reply", "fee too high", "no suitable time", "no suitable clinician", "waited too long" to Lost enquiries; this is the owner's best marketing-versus-capacity signal.
19. **Part 2 (buffer and heavy-day protection).** Add "Back-to-back count" column and a tool "Heavy day check" (sessions in a row, minutes break, clinician) so owners can protect clinicians from back-to-back heavy sessions; this appears in complaints about scheduling.
20. **Part 2 (client-side rescheduling).** Add "Rescheduled by client" as a status or reason and a "Reschedule share" KPI, because reminders without a cancel/reschedule option are a top complaint; show "Reminder to cancel" lead time.
21. **Part 6 (notes).** Make the note rule a visible threshold: default 2 days, flagged at 48 hours; add a "Notes done within the rule" gauge (already Notes on time) and a weekly "Late notes by clinician" report row. Keep status-only wording.
22. **Part 6 (associate supervision).** Add "Supervision hours owed to date" (sessions seen divided by ratio) and "Hours logged" side by side, so the owner sees a debt number, not only the ratio; rename "ratio met" to "supervision up to date".
23. **Part 6 (renewals).** Add "Insurance cover for the practice" and "Data-protection registration" as generic renewal items (no names), plus "Cover for the associate"; add a red 30-day and amber 45-day split in the register.
24. **Part 6 (continuing education).** Offer the label "continuing education (or CPD)" once so UK users recognise it; add a "Hours still needed this year" KPI and a "Cost per clinician" column.
25. **Part 7 (referral source quality).** Show "Value per enquiry by source" and "Value per client by source" first in the Referral source report, ahead of enquiry counts, because owners care more about which source brings paying clients than which brings contacts.
26. **Part 7 (directory listing cost).** Keep the keep-or-drop tool but add "Months to pay back one directory listing" and a decision cell with plain words "keep", "renegotiate", "drop".
27. **Part 7 (groups).** Add "Facilitator pay as share of fees" and "Break-even sign-ups" as the first line of every group record; add "Waiting list for next group" so interest becomes a start date. Groups at $82 average per head in the US data are low-fee, so the business case should show profit per person and per hour of facilitator time.
28. **Part 8 (rent and overhead).** Add "Overhead share of revenue" (rent, cleaning, software, insurance, admin pay) as a KPI and gauge with a 15-25% default band; rename the existing room-cost lens "Cost per room hour used".
29. **Part 8 (tax and cash cushion).** Keep the tax set-aside but add "Months of expenses in cash" with a 3-6 months reserve band; the floor setting alone is not how owners talk about it.
30. **Part 8 (profit margin).** Add a profit margin band (10-20% for a group) next to the margin KPI; label "Owner pay plus profit" as one line in the profit and loss so owners see pay and profit together (15-30%).
31. **Part 8 (fee setting).** Add a tool "Is my fee right?" (cost per session all-in, target margin, local market band entered by the owner) rather than hard-coding market fees; US average individual fee is about 159 and UK typical private fee is 40-60 on listings, so the demo's 125 sits in range but the tool must take the owner's local band.
32. **Part 8 (losing service).** Extend the Losing service fix with "Sessions a week needed to break even" and "Fee that breaks even" so the owner gets one decision number; the story (Behaviour therapy) stays.
33. **Part 5 (trend, not benchmark).** Add a "Compare with my own history" row to each monthly clinician report (this month vs 3-month average vs same month last year); owners are told to compare with their own history first.
34. **Part 1 / Part 2 (waiting time target).** Add "Days to first appointment" to the practice-level scorecard, because university data show about 8 days and owners compare; keep 14 days as the editable default.
35. **Shared words (terms to rename).** Use "utilisation" once, then in tables "booked share of cap"; use "A/R" and "money owed" together once; use "contract (self-employed) clinician" and "employed clinician" consistently; use "associate (works under supervision)" and add "trainee" only in a sentence of help text, since a licence-type word must not appear.
36. **Shared words (payers).** Add one help line that "a third-party payer" may be an insurer, employer scheme or public body in your area, with no names; this widens the UK/EU fit and removes the risk of US-only wording.
37. **Shared settings.** Add: `net_collection_target` (pct 0.95), `max_days_ar` (int 40), `clean_claim_target` (pct 0.90), `missed_session_target` (pct 0.10), `overhead_target` (pct 0.20), `profit_target` (pct 0.15), `cash_cover_months` (int 4), `pay_share_target` (pct 0.55). Keep the 20-per-part limit by dropping settings a part does not use.
38. **Wrong assumption: one tax rate and one currency.** The blueprint's tax set-aside default of 25% sits inside the 25-30% range owners quote; keep it and add the sentence "confirm with your adviser" on every tax cell; keep one generic currency and a "fee per session" sample that works for 40-60 (UK) and 100-180 (US) by showing the fee table as editable demo values.
39. **Packaging for the shop listing.** Competitors sell "sample plus blank" files, "no macros", "works in Excel and Google Sheets", a setup guide and a one-page start sheet; add a "Start here" tab to each workbook and a clear "Replace demo data" instruction; this is what buyers praise in reviews of similar items.
40. **Positioning versus software.** Add a one-line "Why a workbook" block to every Start-here tab in generic words: flat price, no per-clinician fee, your data stays with you, reports built for group owners (profit by service, contractor check, room cost, losing service); this answers the main complaints about price rises and add-ons without naming any product.
