# STUDY — solo doula and small doula agency business (market and workflow)

Method: 25 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-15 (the limit), on top of the earlier Research 20 (TheModernDoula 11.6k sales; TheBirthWorkCo 7,555+ sales, now read live as 24.6k sales). Many search results gave titles only, so most figures come from fetched pages. Anything not read is marked NOT verified. Brand names appear only as research sources; none may reach the product.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner):** reply to enquiries (speed matters); phone/text check-ins with clients inside the on-call window; log hours of the day (prenatal visit, birth, postpartum shift) and miles driven; check who is on call today and who is backup; update expected due dates as clients report them; post any payment received.
**Weekly jobs:** consultation calls and follow-up for each prospect; send contracts and deposit requests; book prenatal and postpartum visits; check the next 6 weeks of due dates against the monthly cap; confirm backup doula for every client entering the on-call window; invoice postpartum hours worked; pay backup or agency doulas; check class and workshop sign-ups; ask finished clients for a review; thank referral sources.
**Monthly jobs:** births-attended and hours log closed; income by package, by add-on and by source; unpaid balances and payment-plan instalments due; pay agency doulas per client or per hour; backup doula pay run; mileage total; expenses; cash across uneven months (a birth package is paid early but earned late); owner pay and tax set-aside transfer; marketing/referral review; capacity check for the next 3 months of due dates.
**Yearly jobs:** price review and new package list; contract and policy refresh; training and certification renewal dates (generic dates); insurance and business-registration renewal dates (generic); budget and a time-off plan (one doula host says plan big trips about nine months ahead); tax pack for the accountant; thank-you to past clients and care providers; agency pay-split review; class calendar.

**Pain points (sources):**
- Overlapping due dates: "all 5 of my clients due in the same month all gave birth within 7 days of each other"; back-to-back births are more common than same-day ones; the advice is a monthly maximum client load, not spacing due dates. https://nationalbabyco.squarespace.com/doula-blog/tips-for-managing-back-to-back-due-dates
- On-call strain: always reachable "at the drop of a hat", feels like 24 hours a day, missed family days, lack of time off "a major contributor to burnout"; the host caps at about three births a month and spaces clients by two weeks either side of the due date. https://pug-moose-ytb9.squarespace.com/kaely-daily-podcast/how-do-i-take-time-off-as-a-doula
- Backup doula worries: no backup in place, "pausing to build a backup system isn't feasible" while earning; the same article asks how to raise backup in interviews. Another source says have several local backups and put on-call times, pay and duties in a contract. https://www.dona.org/doula-me-this-6-i-have-no-backup-doula ; National Baby Co. page above.
- Bookkeeping: a package is "income on the day the credit card cleared" though it covers prenatal visits, on-call time, birth and postpartum; 10-15% of contracts are changed or ended mid-way, so refunds must be tracked; backup doula pay is a risk if mishandled; self-employment tax and quarterly payments surprise owners. https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/03/independent-doula-birth-worker-postpartum-care-bookkeeping-asc-606-prepaid-birth-package-deferred-revenue-dona-cappa-scope-of-practice-section-280a-hsa-fsa-superbill-medicaid-doula-coverage-kpi-guide (an accounting vendor blog, not doulas' own words).
- Agency pay: doulas complain in groups about agency cuts; a doula podcast host answers that 25-30% is standard (some 45-50%, "a little ridiculous") because the agency covers vetting, paperwork, collection and software. https://pod.wave.co/podcast/the-doula-darcy-podcast/stop-complaining-about-doula-agencies-taking-a-commission
- Existing trainer tools are single trackers (testimonials, backup, expenses, certification, resources) in a database app at 67 dollars, so owners must join them up themselves (see section 3).
- NOT verified: Facebook-group and forum threads (not readable from here); only podcast and blog pages. UK pain points not found.

## 2. Leading doula and birth-worker business software (research only)
Monthly prices seen (https://agiled.app/blog/best-client-management-software-for-doulas, a vendor blog that ranks itself first): general small-business tools $12 to $59 a month (one $24 billed yearly, $29 monthly; $59-69 higher plan; others $12, $15, $19, $25 per user, $29, $35, $55); a free CRM. Doula-specific: one product $19 flat (starter) and $29 flat (premium; team management, text notices; extra per-claim fees), https://www.capterra.com/p/10034912/Doulado/ ; another flat-rate product with a client board by birth stage, invoicing with deposits and balances, a backup doula per client, e-signature, reports (https://www.capterra.ca/software/1239891/DoulaFlow).
Features: contracts with e-signature, intake forms, invoices and payment plans, client portal, calendar with on-call dates, reminders, reports, resource sharing.
User complaints: **none found.** Both doula-specific Capterra pages show 0 reviews; the ranking blog gives only the author's trade-offs: no birth-specific data model ("birth plan details need manual setup", "prenatal visits tracked via projects, on-call dates on the calendar"), form tools that are "not health-specific", contracts and invoicing "lighter" in practice tools. Per-user pricing in the team tool ($25 per user) is the cost that rises for agencies. Extra per-claim fees on one premium plan.
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** flat 29 once, no per-doula fee; an on-call and capacity forecast from due dates (the thing the calendars only "block"); package-to-hours profit (income per hour of prenatal + birth + postpartum + mileage), which no tool computes; backup and agency pay splits that tie to each client; uneven-month cash forecast; owner pay and tax set-aside; all data stays with the owner. No tool here offers a "should I take this client for this month?" verdict.

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Generic words only.)

1. **On-call forecast from due dates.** Enter each client's estimated due date, on-call start (default week 37-38) and end (due date plus a set number of days); show, per day for 6 months, how many clients are on call; flag days with 2+ and 3+. Ranked list of the busiest weeks.
2. **Can I take this client? verdict.** Input a new due date; count overlaps in the on-call window, check monthly cap and backup availability; output "yes", "yes with backup booked", or "decline or waitlist".
3. **Monthly cap setting and utilisation.** Cap by month (default 3 births), booked vs cap, and months left open; remaining capacity for the next 12 months.
4. **Overlap scenario test.** Simulate that all clients due in a month give birth within 7 days; show how many births land in the same 7 days and whether backup is enough.
5. **Backup cover check.** For every client entering the window, show the named backup, backup's own on-call load that week, and "no backup named" alerts.
6. **Backup pay and missed-birth tool.** Backup pay rule (flat or share of package or hourly); when a backup attends, split package between doula and backup; owner net after the split.
7. **Package profit per hour.** For each package: income divided by hours of prenatal + on-call contact + birth + postpartum + travel time; rank packages by income per hour and show the lowest.
8. **Actual hours per birth client.** Log hours by phase for each client (times and hours only); compare with the package plan; flag clients over plan by more than a set percent.
9. **Package price check ("Is my price right?").** Cost per hour from owner pay target and expenses; needed package price; compare with the owner's local range entered in Settings; verdict "raise", "hold", "lower".
10. **Pricing quote builder.** Birth + hours + add-ons + travel fee + discount; show deposit, balance date, and instalment schedule on one page.
11. **Deposit and balance tracker.** Deposit due, received date, balance due week (default 32-36 weeks), overdue amounts by client; ageing buckets 0-30, 31-60, 61+ days.
12. **Payment plan builder.** Split balance into instalments ending before the on-call window; show what is still unpaid when the window opens.
13. **Prepaid package earned vs unearned.** Allocation by phase (editable shares, example 20/30/35/15) so the owner sees money held for work not yet done; ends with a "cash not yet earned" figure.
14. **Refund and cancellation log.** Reason, date, amount kept, amount refunded; refund and change rate against a 5-12% setting; list of reasons by count.
15. **Postpartum hours ledger.** Hour packs (40/80/120) used vs bought, expiry date, balance, and invoice-ready line; flag packs unused near expiry.
16. **Weekly hours load.** Postpartum hours per week against a setting (default 20-30) plus birth hours; warn when total load passes the setting for 3 weeks.
17. **Mileage log with totals.** Trips, miles, rate in Settings, cost per client and per package; mileage added to package profit.
18. **Consultations and conversion.** Enquiry, consultation, signed, lost; conversion by source and by month; "hours to first reply" and share replied within 1 working day.
19. **Lost enquiries by reason.** Price, date full, no backup, chose someone else, no reply; shows the capacity-versus-price signal; flag declined above a setting (default 15%).
20. **Referral source value.** Per source: enquiries, clients, income, income per client, cost of gifts or fees; ranked by income not by count; "thank-you due" list for sources that sent clients this quarter.
21. **Referral gap list.** Sources that sent clients last year and none this year; follow-up date.
22. **Class and workshop economics.** Price, seats, sign-ups, break-even sign-ups, profit per class hour, and clients that came from class; compare each class with own history.
23. **Add-on services report.** Each add-on income and hours, attach rate per package, and revenue per hour so the owner knows which add-ons to keep.
24. **Combined package test.** Compare birth + postpartum bundle price against the sum of parts: discount given, hours, income per hour; verdict "bundle pays" or "bundle loses".
25. **Agency doula pay calculator.** Per-client or per-hour pay; agency share default 25-30%; owner margin per client after agency doula pay, backup, mileage and software.
26. **Agency capacity by doula.** Clients and on-call days per doula per month against each doula's cap; who is free for the next due date; ranked "best doula to assign".
27. **Agency split proposal tool.** New split, number of clients, average package: effect on owner margin and doula pay per hour; verdict against a margin target.
28. **Doula pay run.** Month statement per doula (clients, hours, split, mileage, adjustments) checked against what was paid; flag differences.
29. **Owner own-client load.** Owner as doula plus owner as manager: hours on own clients vs admin hours; owner pay per hour worked.
30. **Births attended log.** Date, client, start and end time, hours; per month and per doula; births per quarter against a setting; time between births (rest days).
31. **Time-off planner.** Blackout dates shown against due dates and on-call windows; "dates you can safely book off" list; warn when a booked client's window hits the dates.
32. **Review and testimonial pipeline.** Clients due for a follow-up and a review request (date after birth plus days); reviews received per month; clients asked vs answered.
33. **Training and renewal dates.** Generic items (certification renewal, insurance, registration, first-aid course) with due dates; red within 30 days, amber 60; cost per item for the year.
34. **Expense tracker by category.** Mileage, training, insurance, software, supplies, class costs, marketing; share of revenue vs setting; compare with own previous year.
35. **Uneven-month cash forecast.** From due dates and payment plans, forecast cash in per month for 12 months, expenses, owner pay; show the lowest month and months of cover (setting 2-3).
36. **Owner pay and tax set-aside.** Owner pay target, set-aside percent (setting; "confirm with your adviser"), transfer per month; "can I pay myself this month?" verdict from cash floor.
37. **Profit by package, by doula, by month.** Ratio checks: income per hour, pay share, margin; all three cuts side by side with 3-month average and same month last year.
38. **Compare with own history.** Every monthly KPI shown vs 3-month average and same month last year; flag drops over a set percent.
39. **Year-end summary and next-year plan.** Total births, hours, income, best source, best package; next-year births needed to hit the income target at current package prices; scenario for +/- 1 birth a month.
40. **Start-here tab and plain buying claims.** Each workbook: sample data and blank version, "Replace demo data" steps, no macros, works in Excel and other spreadsheet tools, one-line "Why a workbook" (flat price, no per-doula fee, data stays with you); settings in one tab with 20 or fewer values.

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, platform, training-body, certification-body, insurer, bank or company names. Say "your certification renewal", "your training", "your insurer".
- No law, regulation, agency, licence, program or standard names. Say "your local rules" and "confirm with your adviser" on tax cells. Do not mention health-account, public-payer or accounting-standard names.
- Strictly non-medical: no medical, clinical or safety claims, no health advice, no diagnosis or symptom words, no medical records. Client care is dates, hours, package and contact notes only; say "ask the client's care provider". Avoid week-of-pregnancy medical language; use "on-call start" and "estimated due date" as dates the owner sets.
- No outcome promises about births (no "better births", no results). The product counts hours, dates, money and capacity only.
- Gender-neutral wording for doulas and partners; the birthing client is "the client"; the partner is "support person".
- Prices and benchmarks are the owner's own settings or demo values; never present a market figure as a rule.
