# STUDY — HVAC and plumbing service contractor, 1 to 15 technicians (market and workflow)

Method: 28 web calls on 2026-11-15 (searches and fetches; this is 3 over the 25-call limit, stated openly). Several fetches were blocked or empty (see NOT verified marks). Built on research6 (Research 13 and 23) and research_20261113 (HVAC/electrician row), not repeating them. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Many benchmark pages are vendor or agency marketing; weak numbers are flagged.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner, office manager, dispatcher):** answer and log every call (new, repeat, warranty, member); book the job with a diagnostic or call-out fee; fill the dispatch board by technician, van and area; confirm and text arrival windows; assign parts to the job and check the van has them; collect job notes and photos from technicians; turn finished jobs into invoices the same day; take payment or deposit; log callbacks; check tomorrow's board; end-of-day cash and unpaid-job check.

**Weekly jobs:** estimates sent and not yet decided (follow-up list); close rate by option (good / better / best) and by technician; technician hours: on-the-clock vs billed vs paid, drive time, unbilled hours; van stock counts and restock list; supplier price changes and parts to re-price; callbacks and warranty visits with cost; maintenance-agreement visits due next 30 days; unpaid invoices and promised dates; lead-source count and spend; permit and inspection follow-ups; weekly cash view with payroll date.

**Monthly jobs:** job costing per job and per job type (labour hours at cost, parts, permit fees, disposal, subcontracted work, vehicle share); average ticket by job type and technician; gross margin by job type; commissions and spiffs; payroll and pay-run check; accounts receivable ageing; agreement members: new, renewed, lapsed, tune-ups done; installed-equipment age list for replacement leads; fuel and vehicle cost per van; lead cost per booked job by source; reviews and referrals as counts; tax set-aside; owner pay; profit and loss; the season check (are we ahead or behind last year's same month).

**Yearly jobs:** price-book and labour-rate review (costs rose; did prices?); technician pay review; heating and cooling season plan (hire, train, stock up, pre-season tune-up campaign); renewal of licences, insurance, registrations as generic dates; vehicle replacement plan; tool and equipment purchases; agreement price review; marketing budget; year-end pack for the accountant; slow-season cash plan.

**Pain points reported (sources, paraphrased):**
- Busy but broke: owners bill a lot but do not track actual time and materials against each estimate; one guide's example is a job billed 15,000 that cost 16,000 to deliver. Underpricing leaves out insurance, vehicle cost, callbacks and unbillable time (a 65/hour rate that costs about 70 to deliver). https://workzen-website.pages.dev/en/blog/2026-02-03-why-hvac-businesses-fail (blog; its headline failure statistics have no named study: NOT verified)
- Peak season can sink profit: pricing based on old labour and material costs, fuel not priced in, untracked drive time and warranty callbacks, low-margin installs, unrecorded field discounts and wasted materials, no job-level profit tracking, summer cash spent instead of saved for slow months. https://briefglance.com/articles/hvacs-summer-paradox-why-peak-season-can-sink-profits
- Seasonal cash flow, prices lagging cost rises on parts, labour and rent, inventory tying up cash (too much) or stalling jobs (too little), and late payment; suggested fixes: deposits, monthly agreement billing, financing offers. https://ftlfinance.com/3-common-cash-flow-problems-for-hvac-businesses-and-how-to-solve-them
- Reserve and set-aside rules of thumb: 3 to 6 months of operating costs in cash; set aside 10-15% of every payment (blog advice, not data). WorkZen link above; https://simprogroup.com/blog/hvac-business-kpis-guide (also: technician utilisation 70-85%, net margin 10-20%).
- Owner wears every hat (technician, dispatcher, bookkeeper, marketer) and burns out; missed calls and slow replies lose work. WorkZen link above (the "85% do not leave voicemail" and "400% faster reply" numbers have no named study: NOT verified).
- Software reviewers' own words (paraphrased): the most common complaint about a popular small-shop app is add-ons and missing features, wanting better agreement and maintenance-plan tools, monthly contract billing, inventory management, stored cards, employee scheduling and time-off, reporting; one reviewer called a billing increase "bait and switch". https://www.capterra.com/p/140363/HouseCall-Pro/reviews/?page=23 (4.7 from 2,748 reviews; the page shows reviews dated 2017-2022).
- Reviewers of the big app: expensive for small businesses, prices raised after heavy investment, a two-year lock-in, a promised price followed by a 35% increase, long set-up, hard to learn, slow support, inflexible reporting. https://capterra.com/p/150053/ServiceTitan/reviews/?page=7 (4.3 from 344 reviews).
- NOT verified: contractors' own words on trade forums or contractor groups. Searches returned articles, vendor blogs and review pages, not readable forum threads. The blueprint must treat the pain points above as industry-article evidence, not as direct quotes.

## 6. Gaps and improvements for the blueprint

Part titles are proposed at the end of this section. "Part n" refers to that list. Keep every item generic: no brand, code, law or licence names.

1. **Part 1 (call log and booking rate).** Log every call with source, type (no-heat, no-cool, leak, drain, quote, member, warranty) and outcome; compute booking rate by source and by who answered, and rank missed or unbooked calls by estimated lost job value.
2. **Part 1 (dispatch board).** One-week board by technician and van with job type, hours, drive time and area; flags double-booking, over-capacity days and jobs with no parts confirmed; works with no dispatcher (owner view) and with one (office view).
3. **Part 1 (job record to work order).** Each job: reason, site reference, address area, equipment on site, parts needed, permit needed yes/no, status; a printable work order and a customer-signature field as plain text, no repair instructions.
4. **Part 1 (permit and inspection log).** Per job: permit needed, applied date, fee, approved, inspection booked, inspection passed, days waiting; ranked list of jobs blocked by a permit and the daily cost of a van waiting.
5. **Part 1 (repeat and callback flag).** At booking, the sheet checks whether the site had a job in the last N days (editable) and marks it as a possible callback so the cost lands on the right job.
6. **Part 2 (flat-rate price book).** Price book rows built from cost: labour hours x rate, parts cost x markup, permit, disposal, trip; shows margin per line and flags lines that fall under the owner's margin target after a supplier price change.
7. **Part 2 (good-better-best builder).** Three options per estimate with cost, price, margin and monthly payment from an owner-entered financing factor; the verdict names the option that meets the margin target with the best close history.
8. **Part 2 (estimate follow-up list).** Open estimates ranked by value and age with next follow-up date; "money on the table" total; close rate by option, by technician and by job type, compared with the owner's last 12 months.
9. **Part 2 (price review tool).** Enter cost increases (labour, parts, fuel, insurance); the tool shows the price rise needed to keep the margin and which price-book lines to lift first (highest volume x lowest margin).
10. **Part 2 (call-out and diagnostic fee test).** Compare the fee against the cost of an unpaid visit (drive, time, vehicle) and show the break-even fee and how many diagnostic visits convert to repairs.
11. **Part 3 (job costing per job).** Actual hours x loaded labour cost, parts used, permit, disposal, subcontracted work, vehicle share; margin vs the estimate with a one-line verdict ("won money", "lost money, because ...").
12. **Part 3 (margin by job type).** Ratio check of margin, ticket and hours by job type (repair, tune-up, replacement, install, drain, water heater, repipe) with the owner's own target and a flag when a type is losing money.
13. **Part 3 (average ticket tables).** Ticket and margin by technician, van, customer site type and month; ranked, with each cell compared with the owner's own 12-month history.
14. **Part 3 (callback and warranty cost).** Each callback adds hours, parts and travel to a cost line, linked to the original job; callback rate by technician and job type; "cost of callbacks this season" in money, not only percent.
15. **Part 3 (replacement vs repair decision).** Inputs: equipment age, repair quote, replacement price, expected life as entered by the owner; the output is a plain recommendation sentence for the estimate, no technical claims.
16. **Part 3 (what-if on labour and parts).** Scenario test: labour cost +5%, parts +8%, close rate -3 points; shows effect on monthly profit and on break-even jobs per week.
17. **Part 4 (technician hours sheet).** Clock-on, billed, drive, shop and idle hours per technician per week; utilisation vs the owner's target; paid hours vs billed hours gap in money.
18. **Part 4 (pay calculator, all models).** Hourly, flat-rate, salary plus commission, and mixed; gross-to-total cost with employer extras as owner-entered percentages; margin per technician after all-in cost.
19. **Part 4 (commission and spiff).** Commission on sold or on collected jobs, spiff list per item sold, clawback on callback or unpaid invoice (owner rules in Settings); per-technician statement a technician can read.
20. **Part 4 (technician scorecard).** Ranked table: ticket, close rate, callback rate, utilisation, revenue per day, review count; each against own history, with a note box for the one-to-one talk.
21. **Part 4 (capacity and hire tool).** Booked hours vs available hours by week and season; "should I hire a technician or an apprentice?" with cost, ramp-up months and extra jobs needed to break even; verdict at the end.
22. **Part 5 (van stock sheet).** Per van: part, min, max, on hand, cost, last counted; auto restock list with supplier-run total; stock value per van and shrink between counts.
23. **Part 5 (parts price changes).** Log supplier price changes; shows which price-book lines and open estimates are now under margin; ranked by money at risk.
24. **Part 5 (parts used per job).** Parts leaving the van linked to a job; unbilled parts list (used but not on an invoice) by technician and week.
25. **Part 5 (vehicle cost per van).** Fuel, insurance, repairs, loan or lease, tyres, per van per month; cost per working day and per job; "keep, replace or add a van" verdict.
26. **Part 5 (tools and equipment).** Purchase log with cost, date, assigned technician and replacement year; yearly budget and a "what to buy this season" ranked list.
27. **Part 6 (site and equipment records).** One record per site reference: equipment type, make, model, serial and install year as plain fields; last service; a ranked "aged equipment" list for replacement leads with a call-this-week column.
28. **Part 6 (agreement book).** Members, plan, price, start, visits due by season, status; monthly recurring revenue, renewal rate, lapse list and revenue at risk this month.
29. **Part 6 (tune-up scheduling by season).** Visits due by month vs technician capacity; flags months where visits exceed the slack hours, and months with too few visits to fill the slow season.
30. **Part 6 (agreement profit).** Revenue per member minus cost of visits, travel and discounts, plus follow-on repair and replacement revenue from members vs non-members (owner's own data); verdict "price up", "hold" or "add a visit".
31. **Part 6 (pre-season campaign).** List of sites by last service and equipment age, reminder sent, booked, converted; cost per booked tune-up and per agreement sold.
32. **Part 7 (invoicing and deposits).** Invoice register with deposit asked, deposit paid, balance, due date, financing offer used (generic), and payment method; unpaid-job warning when a job is complete and not invoiced within N days.
33. **Part 7 (ageing and chasing).** Ageing buckets 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+ with a promised-date column and chase log; days to paid by job type and technician.
34. **Part 7 (seasonal cash forecast).** 12-month forecast from own monthly history: cash in (jobs, agreements, deposits), fixed costs, payroll, tax set-aside; months of cover and the lowest-cash month, with a "what if the summer is 15% slower" test.
35. **Part 7 (owner pay and tax set-aside).** Safe owner draw per month from profit and cash cover; set-aside percent as an editable setting with a "confirm with your adviser" line; owner pay plus profit shown together.
36. **Part 7 (profit and loss by season).** Monthly profit and loss with heating season, cooling season and shoulder months labelled, and a comparison with the same month last year.
37. **Part 8 (lead sources).** Spend, leads, booked jobs, sold jobs and revenue by source; cost per lead, cost per booked job and revenue per spend; keep / renegotiate / drop verdict per source; a note when a source is cheap per lead but costly per booked job.
38. **Part 8 (reviews and referrals).** Counts of requests sent, reviews received and referrals by technician and month; conversion of completed jobs to reviews; no platform names.
39. **Part 8 (renewals and licence dates).** Generic register: licence renewal date, insurance, vehicle registration, inspection due, technician training dates; red 30-day and amber 60-day split; cost of each renewal for the yearly budget.
40. **Part 8 (owner scorecard and "Start here").** One page for owner or office: margin, utilisation, callbacks, close rate, ageing, agreement renewals, cash cover, each green/amber/red against editable targets in Settings and against own history; every workbook has a "Start here" tab, sample plus blank, "no macros", "Excel and Google Sheets" and a one-line "Why a workbook" block (flat price, no per-technician fee, you keep your data).

## Product wording rules
- No brand, software, manufacturer, supplier, platform, bank or company names. Say "your field-service app", "your supplier", "the manufacturer", "your bank".
- No law, code, regulation, agency, licence, certification, refrigerant-rule or standard names. Say "your local rules", "local permits", "your licence renewal date".
- No safety, gas-safety, carbon-monoxide or health claims; no technical repair instructions. The suite manages the business, it does not teach repairs. Replacement-versus-repair tools use owner-entered numbers and give no technical claims.
- No income promises. Benchmarks are labelled "a typical range, check your own numbers" and are editable settings, never guarantees.
- Gender-neutral wording: "the owner", "the technician", "they".
- Customer personal data limited to a site reference, an address area and a phone number as plain fields.
- "VAT" and "GST" appear only as "sales tax"; keep currency generic; do not use the words lien, lien waiver, retainage, draw or change order (construction habits).
- Every tax or pay cell carries "confirm with your adviser".
