# STUDY — small non-medical home care agency (US first; Canada, UK, Australia accepted): market and workflow

Method: 24 web calls (searches and fetches) on 2026-11-15, on top of "Research 9" in /home/claude/work/research6_20261115.md (not repeated here except where used). Several pages were blocked or redirected (one trade-press site redirected to a host that was not allowed; Etsy shop pages and one listing unavailable; a labour-statistics page not allowed). Anything not read is marked NOT verified. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product. Many numbers come from software vendors' blogs, which have a reason to make problems look big: weight them accordingly.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily jobs (owner, scheduler/care coordinator, office manager):**
- Answer new enquiries (phone first), return missed calls, book the assessment visit; log source and outcome.
- Fill tomorrow's open shifts; handle call-offs (a caregiver cannot come) and find cover; confirm changes to the client and the replacement.
- Watch check-ins: who has not arrived, who is late, who has not checked out; phone client and caregiver; record late or missed visits and the reason.
- Answer caregiver questions (hours, pay, mileage, swaps); post payments received; handle after-hours calls (mostly scheduling).
- Quick cash look: money in, payroll due, invoices unpaid.

**Weekly jobs:**
- Build or finalise next week's schedule from client hours and caregiver availability; check each caregiver's hours against their limit and the overtime line; post the schedule (advice seen: at least two weeks ahead for fixed clients).
- Match new clients to caregivers by skills, area, language, availability and personal fit; check continuity (how many different caregivers each client saw this week).
- Compare check-in and check-out times with the schedule and fix differences; collect time sheets or confirm electronic times; collect mileage and travel entries.
- Prepare payroll: regular, overtime, holiday premium, mileage, travel time, bonuses, deductions.
- Invoice by actual hours (weekly, bi-weekly or monthly clients); send statements; chase overdue balances; submit or follow third-party payer claims (status and amounts only).
- Recruiting: review applicants, phone screens, interviews, offers, onboarding steps in progress.
- Review open assessments and agreements not yet signed; follow up quotes.

**Monthly jobs:**
- Close payroll month; reconcile pay to hours worked and to invoices; payroll cost percentage (taxes, workers' insurance, benefits) check.
- Invoice close, ageing of balances (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+), prepayment and deposit balance check, write-offs.
- Gross margin per client hour by client and by service type; list of clients below target margin; client hours gained and lost (growth and churn).
- Caregiver reports: hours, overtime share, late/missed visit counts, call-offs, tenure and turnover; first-90-day leavers.
- Renewals due in the next 60 days (identity or background re-checks, training, licence or insurance renewals in generic words).
- Profit and loss, office overhead, cash forecast for 13 weeks, owner draw, tax set-aside transfer.

**Yearly jobs:**
- Rate card review and notice to clients; caregiver pay-scale review; holiday calendar and holiday rates set.
- Budget and hiring plan (caregivers needed to reach next hours target); marketing budget and source review.
- Renewal of business insurance and licence (generic), policy and handbook review, agreement templates refreshed.
- Year-end pack for the accountant; tax estimate; client satisfaction check; archive and destruction dates for old records.

**The funnel in numbers owners track (generic):** enquiries, assessments booked, agreements signed, first visit done, hours per week at start, hours per week after 90 days, reason for loss.

**Pain points reported (sources):**
- Scheduler burnout and call-offs. Owners' words: "My weekday scheduler basically is 24-7, has no life. Nobody should work that way." "Call-outs are an immediate and urgent problem, and they tend to push other things to the back burner." "90% of the after-hour phone calls is scheduling. It's handling call-offs." One owner would never have an in-house scheduler again after "conflict, blame, or turnover". Around 1,000 weekly care hours is named as the point where the manual approach breaks (equal to about 25 caregivers at 40 hours, 33 at 30, 40 at 25, 50 at 20 hours a week). https://www.phoebe.work/blog/why-home-care-agencies-start-to-break-at-1000-hours (vendor blog, quotes are from owners as printed there)
- Overtime is built into the schedule by default; uneven extra hours look like favouritism; paper logbooks and spreadsheets are slow and error-prone; schedules posted too late leave no time to replace people; on-call standby increases absence and quitting. https://homecaremag.com/may-2021/common-problems-shift-planning-homecare-agencies (no numbers given)
- Labour is the largest controllable cost and the problem is seen only after payroll closes; late cover is paid at premium rates; lopsided schedules push some caregivers into overtime; scattered visits raise travel cost. https://shiftcare.com/blog/shift-costing-home-care-why-tracking-staff-costs-real-time-matters (vendor; no numbers)
- Profit hides in the details: some clients lose money after travel, insurance and admin are allocated; "profitable on paper, short of cash" while waiting for payers; private-pay clients may stop paying when their funds run out; caregiver misclassification risk; turnover above 30% raises recruiting and training cost and can hurt collections. https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/06/non-medical-home-care-agency-bookkeeping-payroll-billing-margin-tracking (bookkeeping vendor blog; example numbers, not survey data)
- First 90 days: about 57% of caregiver turnover happens in the first 90 days (about 11.9% of all caregivers; 8.9% leave in months 4-12), quoted from an industry benchmark body on a blog. https://homecareatlas.com/blog/home-care-agency-kpi-benchmarks
- Software complaints repeat the same themes: steep learning curve, weak or fixed reports ("reporting feature needs improved"), payroll miscalculated times, missing integrations, price climbing as active clients grow, extra charge for training (see section 2).
- NOT verified: forum threads (searches returned vendor articles, not readable owner forums); true owner-to-owner posts about spreadsheets for home care could not be fetched.

**What a spreadsheet must do better:**
1. Flat 29 dollars once, no per-client or per-caregiver fee, no price rise when hours grow.
2. Owner-defined reports: margin per client hour, overtime risk before payroll, continuity per client, funnel conversion, cash forecast, tax set-aside — no fixed report menu.
3. Payroll preparation from hours with visible rules (overtime line, holiday premium, mileage, travel time) the owner can edit and check line by line, so "miscalculated times" are found before pay day.
4. Decision tools with a verdict (keep or raise rate for this client, accept this new client at this rate, hire the next caregiver or not).
5. No training fee, one start sheet, demo data to replace, runs offline; owner keeps their data.
6. Honest limits: no live app, no GPS, no automatic check-in. The workbook takes check-in times typed or pasted from whatever the agency uses and compares them with the schedule.

## 6. Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first. Keep every item generic: no brand, law, agency, programme or standard names. Non-medical: service types and hours only.)

1. **Margin per client hour, live.** Each client row: bill rate minus loaded pay (pay rate times one plus payroll burden), minus paid travel time and mileage allocated, equals margin per hour and per week; flag below the owner's target (default 35%) in red; ranked list of clients worst-first.
2. **"Should I keep this client?" verdict.** Inputs: hours, bill rate, caregiver pay, travel, cover premium paid, late payments; output: keep, raise rate by X, or release, with the rate that reaches the target margin.
3. **"Can I take this new client?" tool.** Enter requested days/hours, area, language, skills; show caregivers who could cover without overtime, margin at the quoted rate, and the minimum rate that stays above target; verdict accept, quote higher, or decline.
4. **Rate card builder by service type and time band.** Weekday, evening, weekend, night, holiday, live-in day rate, minimum visit length; automatic comparison of each band's margin against its pay band; blocks a band whose margin is below target.
5. **Minimum-visit loss check.** For each client with short visits: hours billed at the minimum vs hours worked plus travel; shows true margin per visit.
6. **Quote and agreement sheet.** From assessment hours and rate card: weekly cost, monthly estimate, deposit, notice and cancellation terms in plain words; prints as a service agreement; marks "signed on".
7. **Enquiry funnel with response speed.** Hours to first reply, enquiries to assessments to agreements to first visit, conversion at each step, loss reasons (price, no cover, timing, chose another provider, no reply); source value ranking (which source brings paying hours).
8. **Time from first call to start of care** in days, by source, with an alert for open enquiries older than 2 days.
9. **Weekly schedule grid** per caregiver and per client with total hours, overtime warning at the owner's line, days off check, and rest between visits check.
10. **Open-shift board** that lists uncovered visits, ranks candidate caregivers by match score (skills, language, area, availability, hours already booked, continuity history) and shows the cost of each option (regular vs overtime vs premium).
11. **Caregiver matching score** weights set by owner (skills, area, language, availability, client preference as yes/no).
12. **Continuity index per client**: number of different caregivers per client in 4 weeks, share of hours by main caregiver; alert when a client sees more than the owner's limit.
13. **Call-off and cover log**: reason (generic), notice given, cover found in minutes, premium paid; per caregiver call-off rate per 100 scheduled shifts; monthly cost of cover.
14. **Fill-rate gauge**: scheduled hours covered by the planned caregiver vs by cover vs not covered; history by week.
15. **Scheduled vs actual hours.** Paste check-in and check-out times; computes difference by visit, flags late (beyond owner's minutes), short, missed, and over-long visits; counts per caregiver and client.
16. **Late and missed visit register** with rate per 100 visits, trend, and a "who must I call today" list from today's schedule.
17. **Billable vs paid hours** per week: hours delivered, hours billed, hours paid (including travel), and the gap; the gap in money is the "leak".
18. **Travel and mileage log**: distance between consecutive visits, paid travel time, mileage amount at the owner's rate, cost per client; suggestion list of visit pairs that should share a caregiver by area.
19. **Payroll preparation sheet.** Per caregiver per pay period: regular, overtime (daily or weekly rule set by owner), holiday premium, travel time, mileage, bonus, deductions; total check against hours billed; a "pre-payroll warnings" list (overtime about to be reached, missing check-out, negative net hours).
20. **Overtime forecast.** Midweek forecast of who will reach overtime by the end of the week and the cost; "move this visit to X to avoid overtime" suggestion table.
21. **Payroll cost per hour** (pay, burden, overtime, travel) as a ratio to bill rate by month; alert when above the owner's ceiling (default 65% of revenue).
22. **Holiday calendar** with the extra pay and extra charge per holiday and a forecast of the holiday's margin.
23. **Client invoicing by actual hours.** Invoice drafted from delivered hours per band, deposits applied, travel or mileage charges if agreed, with a mismatch check against the agreement hours.
24. **Deposits and prepayment ledger**: balance per client, weeks of hours left, alert when balance covers less than one week.
25. **Ageing of balances** in buckets, collection rate, days to be paid by private pay vs third-party payer, with "compare with own history".
26. **Third-party payer claim tracker (status and amounts only)**: submitted, accepted, paid, short-paid, rejected; amount outstanding by age; days to be paid; resubmission list.
27. **Cash gap forecast (13 weeks).** Payroll goes out weekly or bi-weekly; invoices come in by client type with their own delay; lowest cash week and the credit or draw needed; scenario slider: "payer pays 15 days later".
28. **Recruiting funnel.** Applicants by source, phone screens, interviews, offers, starts; cost per hire; days to start; conversion per step; source ranking by caregivers still active after 90 days.
29. **Onboarding checklist per caregiver** with due dates and a "ready for first shift" yes/no that blocks scheduling in the grid when incomplete (generic checklist wording: identity check, references, training done, agreement signed).
30. **Renewal dates register** (credentials, training, insurance, licence in generic words) with 60/30/0-day colours and a list of caregivers who cannot be scheduled after the date.
31. **Turnover and cost.** Leavers by tenure (first 90 days, 4-12 months, over 1 year), annual turnover rate, cost per replacement from owner's own inputs, and "cost of turnover this year"; compare with own history.
32. **Hire-or-not decision.** Hours waiting to be covered vs caregiver capacity, margin gained per added caregiver, months to pay back recruiting and onboarding cost; verdict.
33. **Client hours growth and loss**: new hours, lost hours, net change per month, churn rate per month, reasons; forecast of weekly hours in 3, 6, 12 months.
34. **Hours target planner.** Weekly hours needed to cover overhead and owner draw at current margin; gap in hours and number of caregivers (using 1,000-hour scheduler threshold as an editable marker for when to hire office help).
35. **Overhead and break-even.** Office costs by line, overhead as a percentage of revenue, break-even weekly hours at current margin; scenario: raise rates by X%, lose the weakest client, add a scheduler.
36. **Pay-rate scenario test.** Raise caregiver pay by X per hour: effect on margin, on estimated turnover (owner's assumption) and the rate rise clients would need.
37. **Rate-rise planner.** Rank clients by margin; propose a rate rise per client; show income effect and a "client could leave" risk marker (owner's judgement) with expected net effect.
38. **Owner pay and tax set-aside.** Profit after overhead, owner draw already taken, set-aside rate (editable, "confirm with your adviser"), amount to move this month, cash after set-aside.
39. **Owner dashboard "Is the agency healthy?"** One score from: margin per hour, overtime share, fill rate, collection days, turnover, cash weeks of cover; each against an editable target with green/amber/red and the single worst item first.
40. **One-person start-up mode and packaging.** A "Start here" tab in each workbook, "Replace demo data" steps, sample and blank versions, no macros, works in Excel and Google Sheets; start-up page that hides caregiver-count-heavy tools and lets a one-person agency use enquiries, quotes, schedule, invoices, payroll and cash; one-line "Why a workbook" block: flat price, no per-client fee, your data stays with you, reports built for agency owners.

### Suggested 8 part titles (no overlap)
1. **Client Enquiries, Assessments and Service Agreements** — enquiry funnel, service needs and hours, rate card, quote, agreement, start of care (gaps 3, 4, 6, 7, 8).

4. **Caregiver Payroll and Overtime** — payroll preparation, overtime forecast, holiday rates, payroll cost ratio, mileage pay lines (gaps 19-22).

## Product wording rules
- Strictly non-medical: no diagnoses, conditions, medications, clinical notes, vital signs or care advice; care needs recorded only as service types (companion, personal care help, homemaking, respite, live-in) and hours; no health or safety guarantees.
- No brand, software, platform, bank, insurer or company names in the product. Same for law, regulation, agency, licence, programme, payer-programme or standard names; use "your local rules", "your licence or insurance renewal", "a third-party payer" (insurer, public body, employer scheme).
- Words: "caregiver" with "carer / care worker / support worker" shown once as help text; "schedule (rota, roster)"; "bank holiday" as help text for holiday rate; "private pay (self-funded)".
- Gender-neutral wording ("they", "the client", "the caregiver").
- Tax and pay cells carry "confirm with your adviser"; no legal or employment-status advice.
- All targets (margin, burden, overtime line, travel pay, turnover, days to pay) are editable settings with sample values, not claims about the market; say "example" on sample data.
- Do not claim live tracking, apps, or automatic check-in; the workbook takes typed or pasted times.
