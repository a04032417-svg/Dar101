# STUDY — small nonprofit / charity organization (market and workflow)

Method: 25 web calls (6 searches, 19 fetches counting two searches that returned only link lists) on 2026-11-15, building on "Research 22" in research6_20261115.md (not repeated: the dedicated Etsy shop NonprofitResourcePro, 2,539 sales, 4.8 from 158 reviews, sells Canva/Word templates, no spreadsheets; grant-tracker listings exist at $7-60). Evidence is thin in places and says so; anything not read is marked NOT verified. Brand names appear in this study only as research sources; none may reach the product.

Buyer: executive director, founder or operations / development lead of a small nonprofit (budget about 50,000-2,000,000, 0-15 paid staff, many volunteers, volunteer board). A one-person "founder plus volunteers" start-up must be able to use most parts.

## 1. Real jobs and pain points

**Daily (founder / office lead):** log each gift (cash, cheque, card, online payout, in-kind) with donor, fund, campaign and appeal; send thank-you and acknowledgement; note pledges received; log volunteer sign-ups and shifts worked; log participants served; answer donor and volunteer enquiries; note a next step on every warm donor or funder.
**Weekly:** reconcile gifts logged to money banked; thank-you backlog (gifts with no acknowledgement sent); next-steps list for major-gift prospects; shifts to fill next week; grant deadlines in the next 30 days; event task and ticket count; cash check against upcoming bills; recurring-gift failures to chase; follow-up on pledges due.
**Monthly:** gift summary by fund / campaign / appeal / source; recurring donors count and monthly recurring income; new, repeat and lapsing donors; pledge payments due and overdue; restricted-fund balances and money released from restriction; grant spending against each grant budget; staff time split across grants and programs; budget vs actual by program, management and fundraising; cash flow and months of reserve; volunteer hours and value; participants served and cost per participant; board pack (cash, reserve, pipeline, risks).
**Quarterly:** funder reports due; lapsed-donor outreach (gave last year, not this year); major-gift pipeline review; board attendance and committee check; forecast for the remaining year; event debrief (net raised, cost to raise one dollar).
**Yearly:** budget by program and function; year-end giving statements to donors; end-of-year appeal and campaign goal; annual retention and upgrade analysis; grant calendar for next year; board terms and rotation, conflict declarations; annual report numbers (money in, money out, people served, volunteer hours); membership renewals if dues exist; records archive; year-end pack for the accountant; reserve policy review.

**Pain points reported (sources; directors' own words where found):**
- Small nonprofits run on spreadsheets and cannot afford or run the software: "we rely primarily on Excel spreadsheets and, occasionally, paper forms"; "most data is not centralized"; "can lead to duplication, inconsistent data, and extra work when preparing reports"; "dozens of Excel workbooks that the organization has created over the years"; "most are either beyond our budget or require technical capacity that our small team cannot yet support"; "we do not have a dedicated IT team". Posters track "applicants, reviewers, scores, recipients, grants, projects, donors, partners, budgets" and "volunteers, participants" in one place. https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/microsoftfornonprofits/question-for-small-nonprofits/4354891
- Reporting is the loudest software complaint: "We still struggle with the report feature"; "The power of reporting and on-line forms could be improved"; "The report filters are a bit difficult to understand"; "Reporting can sometimes be limited". Capterra reviews of one donor system. https://www.capterra.com/p/131207/Bloomerang/reviews?page=2
- Grants are poorly served: reviewers "want recurring reporting tasks and better grant tracking overall"; cannot "assign multiple responsible parties for tasks"; a volunteer module is "an additional paid option"; "some features do not exist or require additional payment". Same page.
- Bulk work is hard: "Wish there was a way to bulk update that was simpler"; "Editing/importing data is also a little awkward"; one 1-star director reports fields missing after a data move and a "terrible onboarding process"; another paid for training that "never happened". Same page.
- Thank-you at scale: a user wants "the ability to batch send customized thank you letters"; another had trouble producing year-end receipts. Same page.
- Records model: a business or household "can't have multiple contacts without creating a separate record"; wish to track deceased contacts with a date. Same page; https://capterra.com/p/123954/Little-Green-Light/reviews/ (also: "Bulk imports" of gifts hard; dashboard updates once a day; users ask for "a fundraising thermometer, and expense tracking"; forms weak on phones; interface "plain vanilla"; 4.8 from 317 reviews, so small-org satisfaction is high on price and support).
- Volunteer recruitment is the hardest volunteer-manager roadblock for 33% (survey figure quoted without source, weak). https://bloomerang.com/blog/fundraising-statistics
- Sector context: donor count fell about 3.6% in 2025 while revenue rose about 5%, so fewer donors give more (a retention warning for small groups). https://www.donorperfect.com/nonprofit-technology-blog/fundraising-software/afp-fep-q4-2025/ ; https://www.nonprofitpro.com/article/donor-losses-slow-as-fundraising-strength-holds-into-third-quarter/
- Article titles seen but NOT read (so no claim made): "Spreadsheets Are Not Useful Donor Databases" (NonProfit Times), "Excel Is Not a Database" (NonProfit PRO), "15 Reasons Spreadsheets Don't Work for Nonprofit Accounting" (vendor blog), "Why spreadsheets cannot be the answer" (vendor blog). Expect the product to be told it is "not a database"; answer with per-donor ids, duplicate checks and one gift log that all views read.
- NOT verified: reddit or other forum threads by fundraisers (search returned vendor articles); staff-time-on-fundraising data.

## 2. Leading donor-management, fundraising and nonprofit software (research only)

Prices per month seen: https://costbench.com/best/nonprofit-crm-for-small-nonprofits/ (2026; page admits product "pros" are generic): one small-nonprofit system $45-135, starting at $45 for up to 2,500 contacts, unlimited users, no set-up fee; one system $40-125 (with the vendor's own FAQ saying $119 a month is needed for full CRM features, so the list price misleads); another $40-125; two products "custom pricing"; no free tier on any listed. Also listed as hidden costs: onboarding fees, training, premium support, API access, storage overages, per-user fees, integration costs. A 2026 price roundup page (donorsnap.com/blog/affordable-nonprofit-crms) and eight other roundups were seen as links only: NOT verified for other products (DonorPerfect, Neon, Virtuous, Zeffy-type free tools).
Features offered: constituent records, gift entry and receipts, recurring gifts, appeals and campaigns, email, online forms, pledges, reports, light task lists, basic grant and volunteer modules (often paid add-ons), events and tickets in larger tiers, accounting export.
User complaints (Capterra, see Section 1): weak reporting and filters; limited customization; one contact per record; awkward import and bulk update; email and letter formatting; no batch thank-you; grant tracking and recurring report tasks weak; volunteer and other modules cost extra; price rises; refund and onboarding disputes (one 1-star each); mobile app weaker than desktop; no direct link to bookkeeping for some.
**What a spreadsheet must do better:** one flat 29 dollars, no per-contact tiers, no add-ons for grants or volunteers; the whole organization in eight files (gifts, retention, grants and restricted money, events, programs, volunteers, board, budget and cash) with cross-links by donor id and fund; a "next step" list that ends in a decision rather than a report screen; restricted-fund balance and release checks that the donor systems leave to accounting; cost per participant and cost to raise one dollar ready for grant reports and the board; thermometer and goal tracking built in (a requested missing feature); owner keeps the data; honest limits (a workbook is not a mass-email tool or card processor, and says so).

## Gaps and improvements for the blueprint
(Most valuable first within each group. "Part n" = suggested part below. Each item is a job plus its calculation, in generic words.)

**Part titles (8, no overlap):**
1. Donor Book and Gift Log
2. Donor Growth, Retention and Major Gifts
3. Grants and Restricted Funds
4. Events and Campaigns
5. Programs and People Served
6. Volunteers and Members
7. Board and Governance
8. Budget, Cash and Annual Report

**The 40 features:**

1. **Part 1 (one gift log).** One gift register with donor id, date, amount, fund, campaign, appeal, source, payment method, restricted yes/no, acknowledgement sent date; every other workbook reads it. Check column: missing donor id, missing fund, duplicate (same donor, date, amount), gift above banking.
2. **Part 1 (thank-you backlog).** Ranked list of gifts with no acknowledgement, oldest first, with days waiting and the rule (default 2 working days, editable); verdict "send today" for the top ten.
3. **Part 1 (year-end giving statements).** Per donor total by year with a printable statement page for the whole list, a "gifts that need a written acknowledgement over your set amount" flag, and a plain-words note "ask your accountant" (never "valid for tax").
4. **Part 1 (pledges).** Pledge schedule with paid to date, balance, next due date, overdue days; "pledges due this quarter" feeds the cash forecast in Part 8; write-off proposal for pledges overdue beyond a set number of days.
5. **Part 1 (recurring donors).** Recurring list with monthly amount, start date, last received, status; "failed or missed" flag; monthly recurring income and its share of all gifts; annualised value of the recurring base.
6. **Part 1 (in-kind and matching).** In-kind log with a value entered by the donor or the organization and a "value basis" note; matching log linking a donor gift to the employer match expected, received, and still to chase; chase list ranked by amount.
7. **Part 1 (households and several contacts).** One organization or household with several contacts and one giving total; fixes the "one contact per record" complaint; duplicate finder by name and email.
8. **Part 2 (retention and lapse lists).** For any two years: donors who gave last year and not this year (lapsed list ranked by last gift amount and years given), retained, new, recaptured; retention rate overall, new-donor and repeat-donor, each next to the organization's own prior year (the only default target).
9. **Part 2 (upgrade and downgrade).** Per donor change from last year to this year with a ranked "upgrade ask" list (gave regularly, never asked for more) and a "downgrade watch" list; average gift and median gift side by side.
10. **Part 2 (second-gift conversion).** First-time donors from each year and share that gave a second time within 12 months, with the gap by appeal; the sector's weakest number gets its own tool: "Second-gift plan: who, when, by which message".
11. **Part 2 (major-gift pipeline).** Prospect, capacity entered by the user, stage (identified, cultivation, ask made, committed, closed), ask amount, probability set in Settings, next step and date; weighted pipeline total; overdue next steps ranked; "ask this month" list with a verdict.
12. **Part 2 (appeal and source results).** Per appeal and source: gifts, donors, money, average gift, cost entered, cost to raise one dollar, and a keep / fix / stop verdict against the 0.25 setting.
13. **Part 2 (revenue concentration).** Share of the year's gifts from the top 5 and top 10 donors, and "if our biggest donor left" scenario showing the budget gap; common in a small group, almost never in software.
14. **Part 2 (donor-count and revenue story).** Donors, money and average gift for three years with a plain sentence ("fewer donors giving more") because the sector shows that pattern.
15. **Part 3 (grant pipeline).** Stages: prospect, letter of inquiry, application, awarded, declined, withdrawn; funder, amount asked, amount awarded, dates, owner, probability; weighted income by quarter that feeds Part 8; win rate from the organization's own submitted and won counts (never a sector number).
16. **Part 3 (payment schedule and reports due).** Each award with instalments (due, received) and each report (due date, owner, status); one calendar list for the next 90 days sorted by date with "late" in red; closes the "recurring reporting tasks" complaint and the buyer request for report-date tracking.
17. **Part 3 (grant budget vs actual).** Per grant: budget lines, spent to date from a spending log, remaining, share of award period elapsed vs share spent, forecast to end; verdict "on track / underspending (risk of returning money) / overspending".
18. **Part 3 (restricted funds).** Fund list with opening balance + restricted gifts + grant money - released to spending = balance; check that no restricted fund goes negative and that spending coded to a restriction does not exceed what is available; "release from restriction" journal with date and amount.
19. **Part 3 (restricted vs unrestricted position).** One screen: total cash, of which restricted, of which designated, = free money; warns when cash looks healthy but free money is low.
20. **Part 3 (staff time across grants).** Staff by percentage of time per grant and program; check that each person totals 100% each month; cost allocated to each grant feeds the grant budget vs actual; flags over- and under-charging to a grant.
21. **Part 3 (grant "should we apply?" tool).** Hours to write (user estimate), cost per hour, amount asked, probability from own history, reporting burden, restriction fit; expected value per hour and verdict apply / apply if time / skip.
22. **Part 4 (event budget and result).** Per event: tickets by type, table or sponsor packages, auction and raffle items with value and sale price, donations on the night, direct costs, staff and volunteer hours; net raised, cost to raise one dollar, net per attendee, benefit-to-attendee value per ticket (editable) so the donor part is shown separately.
23. **Part 4 (event go / no-go).** Before booking: expected sales scenarios (low, expected, high), break-even tickets, sponsors needed to cover costs; verdict "run", "run smaller", "do not run"; comparison with last year's own event.
24. **Part 4 (sponsors).** Sponsor log with level, promised, received, benefits owed and delivered; renewal list with a next step.
25. **Part 4 (campaign goal and thermometer).** Goal, raised to date from the gift log (with pledges split from cash), days left, pace needed per week, forecast to finish from current pace; built-in thermometer chart (a missing feature users request).
26. **Part 4 (campaign / event comparison).** Last three years side by side by campaign: raised, cost, donors, new donors; "which fundraising method costs us the least per dollar" ranking.
27. **Part 5 (programs and participants).** Participant list by name, contact status, program, start and last-attended date, count of sessions; nothing sensitive; "not seen for X days" list; people served per program per month and per year (unique and total visits).
28. **Part 5 (cost per participant).** Program cost from Part 8 (direct plus allocated share) divided by participants served; trend by quarter; scenario "if we serve 20% more with the same money"; ready sentence for a funder report.
29. **Part 5 (outcomes).** Up to five user-defined outcome counts per program (for example "completed", "found next step"), target vs actual, and a "report numbers" sheet that collects people served, outcomes, cost per participant and volunteer hours for any funder report in one place.
30. **Part 5 (waiting list and capacity).** Capacity per program, enrolled, waiting; weeks until the list clears; verdict "add a session / hold".
31. **Part 6 (volunteers and shifts).** Roster, availability, shift calendar with needed vs signed up, gaps ranked by date; hours worked per person and program; reminder list for tomorrow's shifts.
32. **Part 6 (volunteer value and retention).** Hours times an editable value of one volunteer hour (shown as "your setting"); volunteers retained from last year; lapsed volunteers list ranked by hours given, with a "reach out" next step; volunteers who also give (join to Part 1 by donor id).
33. **Part 6 (membership dues).** If dues exist: member list, level, due date, paid, renewal rate, lapsing in 30 days, dues income forecast; it can be switched off for organizations without members.
34. **Part 7 (board roster and terms).** Name, role, committee, start, term end, terms served; "terms ending in the next 6 months" list; seats vacant vs bylaws seat count entered by the user; skills matrix with gaps.
35. **Part 7 (attendance and giving).** Meeting attendance per member per year, flagged under a set share; personal giving per member from the gift log; share of the board that gives (a common funder question), "give or get" target and progress.
36. **Part 7 (conflicts and obligations calendar).** Yearly declaration of interests per member with date, items declared, and a dated obligation calendar (policy reviews, insurance renewals, filing of the yearly information return, reports); due and overdue list; wording says "your local rules" only.
37. **Part 8 (functional budget).** Budget and actual by program, management and general, and fundraising; each shared cost allocated by a visible rule (staff time, floor area, headcount); program-expense share shown against the organization's own history (no 75% hard-code); alert when fundraising share rises.
38. **Part 8 (cash flow and reserve).** 12-month cash forecast from the gift log run-rate, pledges due, recurring income, grant instalments, event net and expenses; lowest cash month; months of reserve (free money divided by average monthly expense) with a 3-month default band; "can we afford this hire / this program" tool with verdict.
39. **Part 8 (scenario tests and annual report).** Scenarios: lose the biggest donor, lose one grant, donors fall 5%, recurring grows 10%, costs up 8%; each shows year-end cash and months of reserve. An "annual report numbers" sheet gathers total money in by source, money out by function, people served, volunteer hours and value, donors and retention in plain sentences.
40. **Part all (packaging and honesty).** A "Start here" tab and a "Replace demo data" instruction in each workbook, sample plus blank files, no macros, works in Excel and Google Sheets, a "Why a workbook" line (flat price, no per-contact fee, no paid add-ons for grants or volunteers, data stays with you), a plain "what this is not" line (not a mass-email tool, not a payment service, not accounting or legal advice), and a settings tab with every threshold editable (thank-you days, reserve months, cost-to-raise limit, volunteer-hour value, lapse window, overdue days, amount that needs a written acknowledgement). Also shared words: use "gave last year, not this year" next to "lapsed"; "free money" next to "unrestricted"; "trustee" next to "board member"; "regular giver" next to "recurring donor".

## Product wording rules

- No brand, software, platform, payment-processor, bank or company names anywhere in the product, its tab names, help text or listing.
- No law, regulation, agency, tax-form, tax-status, accounting-standard, licence, program or standard names. Say "your tax-exempt status rules", "your yearly information return", "your local donation tax relief" (never a country scheme name), "your funder's rules", "ask your accountant".
- Never claim that a receipt, acknowledgement, statement or report meets any legal or tax requirement; say only "a record of the gift you can keep; check with your accountant what your rules need".
- No promise of grant awards, fundraising results, retention or any income; probabilities, forecasts and verdicts are the user's own inputs and are labelled "your estimate".
- Donor and participant data kept as names, contact status, amounts and dates only; no health, family, religion, immigration or other sensitive data; no free-text notes about a person's condition.
- Gender-neutral wording throughout (they, the donor, the member, the chair).
- Benchmarks: no sector number shown as a "standard"; defaults are editable settings labelled "starting point" (reserve months, cost to raise one dollar) and everything else compares with the organization's own history.
- Volunteer hour value is an editable setting with no number or source named.
