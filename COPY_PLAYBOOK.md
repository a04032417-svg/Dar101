# COPY PLAYBOOK — image text, titles and listing descriptions for the premium Excel business suites

Written 2026-11-15 from about 40 searches and fetches. Evidence strength is marked on every rule: STRONG (replicated or large, or a marketplace's own rule), MODERATE (published studies, narrow lab setting or old), WEAK (one study, contested, a blog claim, or anecdote), NONE FOUND (we looked and found nothing usable). Where I only know a finding from memory and did not re-open the source this session, I say so.

Hard rules that apply to every line below: English only; no brand names except WhatsApp (Excel and LibreOffice allowed); never the word "compatible"; no Excel version numbers; no fake urgency or scarcity; no medical claims; no testimonials, no claimed reviews or sales we do not have.

## 0. Five things the owner must decide or check (found during the research)

1. **Etsy asks sellers to avoid text overlays and collages on the FIRST photo** "to optimize for search" (Etsy Seller Handbook, https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1347574487014). Our image 1 carries a headline. The size of the ranking effect is not published, so I cannot say how much it costs. Safe option: on Etsy only, make image 1 carry the least text (the product name and one short line, section 4a "Etsy-light hero") and put the full promise on image 2. On other marketplaces keep the full hero.
2. **"Connected" workbooks.** BLUEPRINT.md says each part is a standalone workbook that carries its own copies of its reference tables; the parts share one story, one set of names and the same list values, but nothing in the blueprint says a number in one file updates another file. "Connected" can therefore read as a promise of live links between files. Etsy requires that statements "must be truthful and supported by evidence" (https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1450964704111), and a buyer who expects linked files can open a "not as described" case (Etsy may force a refund). Safer wording used in this playbook: "8 workbooks, one practice" or "8 workbooks that use the same names and lists". Keep "connected" only if the engine really links the files.
3. **AI disclosure on Etsy.** Etsy's rules say "Sellers must disclose within their listing description if an item is created with the use of AI" (https://www.etsy.com/help/article/4511). The owner must decide what applies to these files and images and add Etsy's required line if it does. I did not read Etsy's full AI policy, only this sentence.
4. **File-format sentence.** The brief gives "Standard .xlsx files for Microsoft Excel and LibreOffice". It contains the word Microsoft, which is a brand name. I used it exactly as given. If the no-brand rule is meant strictly, use "Standard .xlsx files for Excel and LibreOffice".
5. **Counts come from BLUEPRINT.md, not from built files.** Only parts 1 and 2 have code in /home/claude/work/th so far. Every number printed on an image or in a description (20 tables, 18 tools, 12 forms, 12 documents, 12 checklists, 34 messages, 30 KPIs, 22 charts, 6 gauges per workbook; so 160 / 144 / 96 / 96 / 96 / 272 / 240 / 176 / 48 over eight) must be re-counted from the final built files before it is published. Etsy and every marketplace treat a wrong count as a false claim.

## 2. Proven science and experiments

Format: finding, source, strength, how we use it in image text and listings.

### 2.1 Concise, scannable, objective writing beats promotional writing
- Finding: five versions of one site, same facts. Against the promotional control, usability rose 124% for the combined version (concise, scannable, objective), 58% for concise text (about half the words), 47% for scannable layout, 27% for objective language. Users disliked promotional style because they had to filter out exaggeration.
- Source: Morkes and Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group, 1997, https://www.nngroup.com/alertbox/9710a.html (fetched).
- Strength: MODERATE (old, small web-reading test, but the direction is repeated in later Nielsen Norman Group work).
- Use: no hype adjectives (ultimate, powerful, amazing, perfect). State the object and the number. Half the words you first wrote. Etsy itself tells sellers to move words like "perfect" out of titles.

### 2.2 Reading pattern: F-pattern (supported), Z-pattern (no source found)
- Finding: eye tracking of 232 users on thousands of pages found a dominant F: a long top bar, a shorter second bar, a vertical scan down the left edge. Readers notice the first two words of a line much more than the third. An 11-years-later study confirmed it. Nielsen says the shape is a rough generalisation (E and L shapes also occur).
- Source: Nielsen, 2006, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/ (fetched).
- Strength: F-pattern MODERATE-STRONG for text pages. It was measured on text-heavy web pages, not on 8-image galleries. Z-pattern: NONE FOUND in this research (it is a designer convention; do not cite it as science).
- Use: start every headline, chip and feature line with the information-carrying word (noun or verb), never with "The", "Our" or "Your amazing". Put the headline top-left, the number or key chip left-aligned under it, and the proof element (dashboard crop) next. Keep each image to one reading path: headline, sub-headline, chips.

### 2.3 Specific, precise numbers signal expertise (when the number is right)
- Finding: precise numbers signal confidence and expertise and make an unfamiliar product seem more reliable; round numbers feel more emotional and fluent; consumers lean on round numbers when browsing and precise numbers near purchase; a precise estimate that turns out wrong damages trust more than an approximate one.
- Source: review of numerical-precision research, Berkeley CMR, 2024, https://cmr.berkeley.edu/2024/11/unlocking-the-persuasive-power-of-numbers (fetched); original work on the signalling effect of numerical precision (Xie and Kronrod, Journal of Marketing Research) is listed at https://collaborate.umb.edu/en/publications/is-t-he-dev-il-in-t-he-det-ails-the-signaling-effect-of-numerical/ (title seen; not opened).
- Strength: MODERATE (lab experiments, several papers).
- Use: "20 tables, 18 tools, 12 forms" is stronger than "dozens of features", provided it is exactly true. A wrong count is worse than a vague one, so count from the built files (decision 5 above). Use round numbers for emotion-led lines ("One payment") and precise ones for the "what is inside" image.

### 2.4 Anchoring and price framing
- Finding (anchoring): people's estimates move toward a number they saw first. The review of the literature calls anchoring "one of the most robust cognitive heuristics" and lists mood, knowledge and motivation as moderators.
- Source: Furnham and Boo, Journal of Socio-Economics, 2011, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:soceco:v:40:y:2011:i:1:p:35-42 (abstract fetched). The classic origin is Tversky and Kahneman, 1974 (from memory, not opened).
- Strength: STRONG in the lab, MODERATE in real shops (size varies).
- Finding (pennies-a-day): showing a one-time cost as a small recurring amount changed which expense people compared it with and raised evaluation and compliance in laboratory studies. Source: Gourville, Journal of Consumer Research, 1998, https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v24y1998i4p395-408.html (abstract fetched). Strength: MODERATE (one lab paper; I did not check replications).
- Finding (left digit): a 9-ending price looks smaller only when the leftmost digit changes (2.99 against 3.00), and more so when a competitor's price is close. Source: Thomas and Morwitz, JCR, 2005, https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v32y2005i1p54-64.html (abstract fetched). Strength: MODERATE-STRONG, small effect.
- Use for the "why not software" image:
  - The honest anchor is the recurring fee of the category. Our therapy research (STUDY.md, from a 2026 pricing survey of practice software) found a category median of about 64 a month, group plans of 79 to 99 per user per month, and complaints of per-clinician pricing that rises as the team grows. We may say "Practice software is often billed per clinician, every month" (supported). We may only print a number if the source and date are in the listing text and the number is current when published. Etsy says price claims "must be truthful and supported by evidence".
  - Amortised frame (true arithmetic): 29 spread over 36 months is 0.81 a month, 29 over 12 months is 2.42 a month. State it as "about 0.81 a month over three years", never as a saving claim.
  - Show 29 as "29 dollars", whole number, left digit 2. Do not add decimals. A currency symbol would read faster ($29) but the brief's workbook rules forbid one inside the files; for the marketplace images the owner decides.
  - Never show a struck-through "was" price unless it is the genuine usual price: Etsy says the higher price must be "the genuine, usual price" offered for a substantial period.
  - No competitor or software brand names anywhere.

### 2.5 Loss aversion and loss framing
- Finding: prospect theory (Kahneman and Tversky, 1979, from memory) says losses weigh more than equal gains. A 2018 review argues the evidence does not show losses generally loom larger than gains and calls for a context-specific view.
- Source: Gal and Rucker, Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2018, https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3049660 (abstract fetched).
- Strength: WEAK to CONTESTED as a general rule.
- Use with care: frame around **a concrete, checkable loss in the owner's own numbers** ("find the service that loses money", "see cash trouble 13 weeks ahead"), not around fear ("you are losing thousands"). Never invent a loss figure, never add urgency or a countdown. The therapy demo story (one service loses money after contractor pay, room time and materials) is a true feature, which is why a loss-framed headline is honest here.

### 2.6 Social proof, and what to use when there are no reviews yet
- Finding: in a study of online purchases, a product with five reviews had a purchase likelihood 270% higher than one with none; the effect was bigger for higher-priced items (380% against 190% for a cheaper one); likelihood peaked at average ratings of 4.0 to 4.7 and fell towards a perfect 5.0; returns diminish after the first few reviews. Source: Spiegel Research Center, Northwestern, 2017, https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/How-Online-Reviews-Influence-Sales (fetched). Strength: MODERATE-STRONG (large company dataset; industry-sponsored).
- Finding: in an experiment with 14,341 participants choosing songs, showing download counts made popularity more unequal and less predictable; a ranked list made it much stronger. Source: Salganik, Dodds and Watts, Science, 2006 (summary at https://acawiki.org/Experimental_Study_of_Inequality_and_Unpredictability_in_an_Artificial_Cultural_Market, fetched). Strength: STRONG as an experiment; it says social information matters, not how large the effect is for a 29-dollar file.
- What this means for us: at 29 dollars, price is high for the category, and reviews matter more as price rises. We have none yet, and we may not fake or imply them (Etsy's seller policy prohibits reviews that "falsely inflate a shop's review score", https://www.etsy.com/legal/policy/seller-policy-effective-through/1283095227943). Etsy gives digital buyers 100 days from first download to review.
- **Substitutes that are allowed and honest** (no study I found tests these for zero-review digital files, so the evidence for the substitutes is WEAK; they are logical, not proven):
  1. **Demonstration instead of testimony:** real sample-data screens showing real numbers (the image 2 dashboard crop). "Sample data shown" printed on it.
  2. **Exact counts** (2.3) as a proof of depth.
  3. **Transparent scope:** a "not for" line and a "status only, no note content" line. It lowers the fear of a wrong purchase.
  4. **A free sample or one-page preview** if the owner chooses: Someka gives a free demo; a free single sheet is a recognised way to reduce risk (WEAK evidence). Owner decision.
  5. **Process proof:** instructions image and a start-in-three-steps image (buyers repeatedly praise instructions, 1.2).
  6. **Earn the first genuine reviews:** ask buyers through the marketplace's normal post-purchase message. Never offer a discount or gift for a review, and never write one yourself. A practitioner article claims even 1 to 3 real reviews lift conversion (WEAK, unsourced).
  7. Do not print "bestseller", "trusted by", "loved by", star icons or invented numbers of customers.

### 2.7 Risk reversal (guarantees)
- Finding: in two experiments, money-back guarantees raised purchase intentions and willingness to pay a price premium; part of the effect is a positive emotional response; for experience goods the return conditions should be stricter than for search goods. Source: Suwelack, Hogreve and Hoyer, Journal of Retailing, 2011, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jouret:v:87:y:2011:i:4:p:462-478 (abstract fetched). Strength: MODERATE.
- Marketplace limits: on Etsy a seller may state a no-returns policy for digital files, but if a buyer opens a "not as described" case Etsy "may force a refund" (https://www.insightagent.app/guides/etsy-refunds-returns-guide, a third-party guide; Etsy's own digital refund wording was not found). On Gumroad the seller can show a custom refund policy on the page; Gumroad says clear expectations lead to higher conversions and fewer disputes but gives no data (https://gumroad.com/blog/p/specify-a-refund-policy-for-your-products).
- Use: the owner chooses the policy; the copy only repeats what the policy really says. A narrow, keepable promise ("If a file does not open, we fix it or refund you") is safer than a broad one. Put it in the last image and the FAQ, only once it is the real policy.

### 2.8 Cognitive fluency and readability
- Finding: statements shown in highly visible colours were judged true more often than the same statements in low-visibility colours (229 students, 32 statements). Anything easier to process tends to be judged more true. Source: Reber and Schwarz, 1999, https://api.isr.umich.edu/publications/effects-of-perceptual-fluency-on-judgments-of-truth/ (fetched). Strength: MODERATE (a real effect; small; some fluency effects have replicated poorly).
- Also known (from memory, not re-opened this session): needlessly complex words make an author look less intelligent (Oppenheimer, Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2006); a hard-to-read font makes a task seem harder to do (Song and Schwarz, Psychological Science, 2008). Strength: MODERATE.
- Use: maximum contrast between text and background; headline at the largest size, sub-headline at about half; plain words (the brief's own rule 6 already bans jargon); no more than two type sizes plus chips; check the image at thumbnail size (250 by 250 pixels, WEAK source) and on a phone: more than 60% of Etsy traffic is said to be mobile (WEAK, two blogs give 60% and 65%, neither sourced).

### 2.9 Benefit against feature, and concrete against abstract
- Finding: for events near in time, people weigh feasibility and concrete constraints more; for distant events, desirability and abstract benefits. Decisions made from far away can look different up close. Source: Trope, Liberman and Wakslak, 2007, https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/2007-Construing-DM-JCP-17%282%29.pdf (fetched). Strength: MODERATE as theory; NONE FOUND for listing images specifically.
- Use: a buyer of a download decides now and will use it today, so concrete "how it works" and "what is inside" images matter. Use an outcome (benefit) in the headline and the concrete feature name plus a one-line reason beneath. Never a feature name alone ("Pay tab"), never a benefit alone ("Peace of mind").

### 2.10 Jobs to be done
- Finding: customers "hire" a product for a job; segment by the job, not by demographics; the milkshake case found about 40% of milkshakes bought in the morning by commuters. Source: Christensen, Harvard Business Review, 2016 (case summary at https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/6496.html, fetched). Strength: WEAK as evidence (anecdotes, no controlled comparison); useful as a writing method.
- Use: write each headline as the job the owner does on a Monday: "check a contractor statement", "see who is waiting", "know cash for 13 weeks". The therapy STUDY.md lists the real weekly jobs (diary against caps, claims, chasing balances, pay run, cash check); use those words.

### 2.11 Curiosity gap
- Finding: curiosity arises from a perceived gap in knowledge and motivates closing it. Source: Loewenstein, Psychological Bulletin, 1994 (abstract at https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/, fetched). Strength: MODERATE as theory; WEAK for headline click-through (no controlled test found).
- Use: allowed in the form "question on the headline, answer in the sub-headline of the same image" ("Which service loses you money?" then "Profit per service type after pay, room time and materials"). Never withhold the answer, never tease something the file does not contain. Honest accuracy matters more than the gap on a marketplace with a "not as described" rule.

### 2.12 Serial position (first and last) and Von Restorff (one thing stands out)
- Serial position: the first and last items in a list are remembered best (Murdock, 1962: from memory, not re-opened). Strength: STRONG for remembering lists; NONE FOUND for image galleries.
- Von Restorff: one item that differs from the rest is remembered better (von Restorff, 1933). The mechanism is still debated, most studies use short word lists, and the sources say nothing about several isolated items at once (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Restorff_effect, fetched). Strength: MODERATE for one item.
- Use: image 1 carries the promise, image 8 carries the price and file format (the closing decision). In each image make ONE element different (the number or one accent-coloured chip), not three.

### 2.13 Choice overload
- Finding: across 63 conditions from 50 experiments (5,036 participants) the mean effect of having more options was virtually zero; effects varied; no single condition was enough to cause overload. Source: Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd, JCR, 2010, https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v37y2010i3p409-425.html (fetched). Strength: CONTESTED.
- Use: do not defend a short image by citing "choice overload". Keep chips to 3 to 5 for the plain reason of legibility (2.1, 2.8 and the guides in 1.3), and let the "what is inside" image carry the complete list.

### 2.14 Things I looked for and did not use
- The "because" copy-machine study (Langer, 1978): replications are mixed (WEAK); not used.
- Video lift figures (40 to 70%): unsourced blog claims (WEAK); a later add, not a rule.
- Z-pattern and "70% of clicks are decided by the first image": no source (NONE FOUND).

### 2.15 Testing is low-power for a small shop
At a 2% visit-to-purchase rate, detecting a 25% relative lift (2.0% to 2.5%) with 80% power needs about 14,000 visitors per variant (two-proportion rule of thumb, n = 16 p(1-p) / d squared, my own arithmetic). A new shop will not reach that, so do not read small A/B differences as proof. Change one thing at a time, wait several weeks, judge only large shifts, and rely on the principles above.

### 3.4 Ko-fi, itch.io, Shopify
NOT RESEARCHED in this pass (no official pages read). Use the Etsy and Gumroad rules as the safe common set: truthful claims, no brand names, no fake reviews. Check each platform's own policy page before listing.

### 3.5 Rules for all marketplaces (our rule set, consistent with the sources)
- No fake or implied reviews, testimonials, sales counts, "bestseller", "trusted by".
- No false or unprovable claims: no time-saved numbers ("in 5 minutes") unless measured; no money-saved numbers; no outcome promises; nothing medical or clinical.
- No competitor or software brand names. WhatsApp only where the product's own send links need it, never in text.
- No fake urgency or scarcity: no countdowns, "last chance", "only today", invented stock limits. A genuine launch discount is allowed only with a real end date and a genuine usual price.
- The file-format sentence is exact: "Standard .xlsx files for Microsoft Excel and LibreOffice". Never "compatible", never an Excel version number.

## 4. THE PLAYBOOK

### 4a. The eight images (design unchanged; only the text changes)

**Headline rules** (all STRONG or MODERATE, see section 2)
1. 4 to 9 words. Start with a noun or a verb that carries information (F-pattern, 2.2).
2. Digits for numbers. One number at most in a headline, except image 7.
3. No adjectives of praise. No "ultimate", "powerful", "amazing", "perfect", "complete" (2.1).
4. Name a checkable thing the file does. Never a result the file cannot guarantee.
5. Name the job in the owner's Monday words (2.10), from the profession's own term list.
6. A question headline must be answered in the sub-headline (2.11).
7. No time or money claims unless measured. No "in minutes".

**Sub-headline formulas**
- S1 "[Count] [format] for [audience]: [job 1], [job 2] and [job 3]."
- S2 "[Count] live numbers, from [example 1] to [example 2]. Sample data shown."
- S3 "[Tool name] shows [calculation] from [inputs you type]." or "[Calculation] after [cost 1], [cost 2] and [cost 3]."
- S4 "Plus [N] [thing], [N] [thing] and [N] [thing]."
- S5 "[Category product] is often billed [unit], every month. This is [price], once."

**Chip rules:** 2 to 3 words, noun phrases, 3 to 5 per image, copied from real tool or KPI names so the buyer finds the same words inside. One chip in the accent colour (Von Restorff, 2.12).

**Signature feature line:** "Tool name: [verb] [what], [for whom or when]." Example: "Contractor statement check: shows the amount due and the gap from their invoice."

### 4b. Product name and marketplace title

### 4c. Listing description structure
1. **First 160 characters = hook:** profession + what it is + count + the three biggest jobs + price model. Keywords in the first sentence (Etsy advice, 3.1).
2. **Line 2:** who it is for and the one pain it removes, in the owner's words.
3. **What you can do:** 4 to 5 outcome bullets, each starting with a verb and naming a real tool.
4. **What is inside:** the list of parts and the real counts (verified, 2.3).
5. **Who it is for / not for:** includes what it does not do (portal, booking, video, claim filing). Protects against "not as described".
6. **How it works in 3 steps.**
7. **File format:** "Standard .xlsx files for Microsoft Excel and LibreOffice. No macros. No subscription. One-time price of 29 dollars." State it plainly near the top for Etsy (the best-reviewed Etsy listings state what the file is and what is needed in the first two lines, 1.1) and again here.
8. **FAQ:** 4 to 7 questions from real doubts: per-clinician fee, client notes, legal or tax advice, currency, changing data.
9. Optional: refund line (only if the real policy), AI disclosure line (if Etsy requires it).
Length: the product spec wants 900 to 2,500 characters; marketplace versions can be longer (the Gumroad guide suggests 400 to 800 words, WEAK).

```

Therapy Private Practice Suite is for the owner-clinician of a small group practice who pays software fees per clinician every month and still cannot see which service makes money.

- Get a 13-week cash forecast with a warning when cash drops below your floor, and a safe owner draw.
- Track late notes, renewals and supervision hours with alerts. Status only: the workbooks never hold note content or a diagnosis.

WHAT IS INSIDE: 8 WORKBOOKS
1 Intake, Screening and Waiting List
2 Scheduling, Caseload and Rooms
3 Client Fees, Accounts and Packages
4 Third-party Payer Billing and Claims
5 Clinician Pay and Productivity

WHO IT IS FOR
Owner-clinicians, practice coordinators and associates in small group practices with employed, contract and associate clinicians.
NOT FOR: a client portal, online booking, video sessions or claim filing. These are workbooks, not a web service.

HOW IT WORKS
```

Facts used and where they come from: 8 parts and their names, per-part counts, tools named in the bullets, status-only privacy rule, "check your own local rules", fictional demo practice, plain amounts with no symbol: BLUEPRINT.md and BRIEF.md. ### 4d. Pre-publish checklist
1. Every count re-counted from the built files.
2. No: the word "compatible", an Excel version number, a brand name (other than the allowed ones), a currency symbol inside the files, a review or testimonial, urgency, an unmeasured time or money claim, a diagnosis or clinical claim.
3. "Connected" removed or confirmed true (decision 2).
4. First image text matches the marketplace (decision 1); AI line checked (decision 3).
5. Refund line matches the real policy on that marketplace (2.7).
6. Sample data is labelled "Sample data shown" on dashboard images.
7. Title ≤140 characters, key phrase inside the first 40; 13 tags each ≤20 characters; description's first 160 characters hold the hook.
8. Image tested at 250 by 250 pixels and on a phone.
